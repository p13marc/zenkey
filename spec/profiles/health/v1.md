# `health.v1`: how well a service serves

| | |
|---|---|
| **Text version** | 0.1 ([`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md)) |
| **Status** | draft |
| **Written against** | core 0.23 ([`../../core.md`](../../core.md)) |
| **Uses** | [`freshness.v1`](../freshness/v1.md), text 0.1 |
| **Evidence** | [`conformance/`](conformance/), [`scenarios.md`](scenarios.md) |

`health.v1` lets a service say how well it serves, in one of three
levels, and lets a reader tell that word apart from the service's absence,
from a word gone stale, and from no word at all. Its standard contract,
[`health.v1.toml`](health.v1.toml), gives a service three resources:

```text
zk2/<system>/<service>/health.v1/state/status          one Status: the service's level, re-put at least every 30 s
zk2/<system>/<service>/health.v1/state/checks/{check}  up to 64 Checks: named parts of that level, put on change
zk2/<system>/<service>/health.v1/stream/faults         Faults: occurrences a level cannot carry, clock_ahead first
```

The status declares `freshness.ttl_s = 60`, so the contract uses
[`freshness.v1`](../freshness/v1.md), and so does every instance that
implements it (core §3.3). This text says what the levels mean, who puts
what and when, and what a reader may conclude.

---

## 0. Conventions

The keywords, the roles and the evidence tags are core §0's. Here,
`[F: <file>]` names a fixture in [`conformance/`](conformance/),
`[Sc: §n]` a section of [`scenarios.md`](scenarios.md), and `core` marks
the core's own evidence. References to `docs/zk2/architecture.md` (its
§3.12 and §4.7) are informative: every rule is stated here.

Terms:
- **Service, owner** are core §1.5's and §0's. The owner of a service's
  `health.v1` keys is that service (core §6).
- **Level:** one of `OK`, `DEGRADED` and `FAILED`, in that order. A level
  is **worse** than another when it comes later. `UNSPECIFIED` is not a
  level (§2.6).
- **Status:** the owner's one member of `state/status`. **Check:** a
  member of `state/checks/{check}`. **Fault:** a sample of `stream/faults`.
- **Current check:** a check the owner answers with a value (put, and not
  deleted), whose level is a level.
- **Effective level:** the worse of the status's level and the worst
  current check's.
- **Reader, subscriber, GET reader** are `freshness.v1`'s (its §0). A
  **tool** is core §0's.
- **Reading:** what one reader observed of one service at one instant:
  its presence, its descriptor, its status, and its checks.

## 2. Rules

### 2.1 The levels

- **What the owner says.**
  - `OK`: it serves its contracts as configured. Every resource its
    descriptor claims exposed (core §3.3) is served, and does its job.
  - `DEGRADED`: it serves with reduced function. Some of what it claims
    works less well, or not at all: an upstream lost, a fallback in use, a
    backlog growing.
  - `FAILED`: it is present, and cannot do its primary job. The owner
    decides which job is primary, and its `reason` says what failed.
- **Absent is presence's word, not a level.** A service whose instance
  token a complete read does not hold is absent (core §8.1). A reader MUST
  NOT report an absent service `FAILED`, or at any level: health is not
  asked of it. An archive's status for it is last-known, never current
  (core S6). `[F: judgements.json]` `[Sc: §6]`
- **`FAILED` is a present owner's word about itself.** A service that
  says nothing has said nothing: silence is not a verdict (core O5).

### 2.2 Aggregation

- **The status is never better than its checks.** An owner MUST NOT hold
  a status better than its worst current check. It MAY hold a worse one: a
  service can fail in ways no check names. `[F: judgements.json]`
  `[Sc: §3, §7]`
  - A check at an unknown level (§2.6) bounds nothing.
- **The order of the puts.** No two keys change at once (core §2.1), so an
  owner MUST order its puts so that every intermediate state keeps the rule
  above. `[Sc: §3]`
  - A check that gets worse than the status: the status first, then the
    check.
  - A check that gets better, or is deleted: the check first, then the
    status, if it improves.
- **No roll-up across services.** A status is one service's. No service
  puts a status for another (core §6), and a parent service's status does
  not stand for its device services, nor theirs for it (§2.9).
- **A tool's roll-up** of many services is the worst level among the
  verdicts it established (§5, "healthy" and "unhealthy"). It counts the
  stale, the unobservable and the not-asked services apart, and folds none
  of them into a level. `[F: rollups.json]`
- **A status better than a check** breaks the rule. A reader reads the
  service at the effective level, and says why (§2.11). A tool that reports
  the break itself, as a finding about the owner, SHOULD see it in two
  readings a grace apart, as core §8.1 asks of shapes read from presence:
  a reader that pairs a subscription's delivery with a GET's reply can pair
  two values that were never current together.

### 2.3 Who puts what, and when

- **Every service.** A service SHOULD implement `health.v1`: it is the
  interface every service of a deployment implements (core §8.1, the
  tokenless set). It puts its own health and no other service's (core §6).
- **The status before the tokens.** An owner MUST put its status before
  core §8.2's step 4, and so before its instance token. A GET made when the
  token appears, or when the descriptor lists `health.v1`, then finds it.
  `[Sc: §1]`
  - Core §8.2 says SHOULD of every state value an owner holds at start.
    This profile makes it a MUST for the status, and the core keeps its
    SHOULD (core 0.23).
  - An owner that has not yet established its level puts `UNSPECIFIED`,
    with a `reason` such as `"starting"`, and puts its level once it knows
    it (§2.6).
- **Confirmed every 30 s.** The status declares `freshness.ttl_s = 60`, so
  its owner confirms it at least every 30 s, by a change or a re-put of the
  value unchanged, as `freshness.v1` §2.4 requires. `[Sc: §1, §2]`
- **Never deleted while the service runs.** An owner MUST NOT delete its
  status while it runs. `[Sc: §1]` When it closes, it leaves the status:
  its tokens going are presence's word (§2.1), and an archive keeps the last
  status as last-known.
- **`since_ns`** is when the level last changed, in nanoseconds since the
  Unix epoch on the owner's clock. A change of level sets it. A change of
  `reason` alone, and a re-put, keep it. A reader shows it, and never ages
  the status by it (§2.4).
- **Checks are put on change.** An owner MUST put a check when its level or
  its `detail` changes, and MUST delete a check it retires (core S3).
  `[Sc: §3]`
  - A check declares no horizon, so it is not re-put (`freshness.v1` §2.3,
    §2.4). The status vouches for it (§2.4).
  - An owner holds at most 64 checks, the contract's cardinality, which its
    descriptor MAY lower (core §3.3).
  - **A check it can no longer run** (its probe cannot reach what it
    probes) is not left at its last level. The owner puts it `UNSPECIFIED`,
    with a `detail` saying why, or deletes it: a check left as it was would
    be vouched for by a status the owner cannot back (§2.4).
  - `checked_at_ns` is when the check found the value put, on the owner's
    clock. It is informative, and a reader never ages a check by it.
  - A check's name is the `{check}` parameter, slugged in the key like any
    value (core §1.4).
- **Faults are occurrences.** An owner publishes a fault when something
  happens that a level would lose: a component restarted, a connection
  dropped, the clock ahead (§2.5). Each sample stands alone (core §2.1).
  - `faults` declares no horizon. Silence on it is its normal state, and a
    reader concludes nothing from it (core O5).
  - A fault's `level` is the occurrence's severity: `DEGRADED` or `FAILED`,
    or `OK` for one that degrades nothing. A fault does not move the status
    by itself. An owner that an occurrence leaves worse off puts its status
    too (§2.2), unless its clock is ahead (§2.5).
  - Its `code` is §2.10's. Its `detail` is free text for a person, and
    `at_ns` is when it happened, on the owner's clock.

| Resource | Put | Deleted | Re-put |
|---|---|---|---|
| `state/status` | before step 4, then on every change | never while the service runs | at least every 30 s (`freshness.v1` §2.4) |
| `state/checks/{check}` | on every change of level or detail | when retired | never: the status vouches for it (§2.4) |
| `stream/faults` | on each occurrence | not state | never: each sample stands alone |

### 2.4 Freshness, and what a fresh status vouches for

- **By `freshness.v1`, at 60 s.** The status's freshness is
  `freshness.v1`'s: how the owner confirms it (its §2.4), how a subscriber
  and a GET reader age it (§2.5, §2.6), several observations (§2.7),
  archives (§2.8), constrained faces (§2.9), a clock ahead (§2.10). This
  text restates none of it. A reader MUST judge the status's freshness as
  `freshness.v1` does, at the contract's horizon. `[F: judgements.json]`
- **Stale is not a level.** A stale status says that the owner has not
  confirmed its level within 60 s. A reader MUST NOT report a stale status
  as `FAILED`, as `DEGRADED`, or at its last level as though current
  (`freshness.v1` §2.11). `[F: judgements.json]` `[Sc: §2]`
- **A fresh status vouches for the owner's checks.** `checks/{check}`
  declares no horizon, so freshness is not asked of a check (`freshness.v1`
  §2.3), and a check carries no freshness of its own. An owner that
  confirms its status is running, and holds its checks current: it deletes
  a check it retires, and leaves none it cannot run (§2.3). So a reader
  MUST read a check's level as current only when the owner answered the
  check and the status is fresh in the same reading. With the status stale
  or unobservable, the checks read with it are the owner's last word, not
  current. `[F: judgements.json]`
  - **Why one heartbeat.** Confirming each of 64 checks would cost 64 puts
    per service every 30 s. The status's re-put costs one
    (`freshness.v1` §2.4, the cost).
- **How a reader reads checks.** A check is put on change, so a
  subscription declared after a check was put sees nothing of it until it
  changes. A reader GETs the checks from the owner (core S4): one selector
  over the owner's `health.v1/state/**` answers the status and every check
  together (core S2).

### 2.5 A clock running ahead

- **The status cannot say it.** An owner whose clock guard finds it ahead
  of its router beyond the delta stops writing state, re-puts included
  (core §4.3, `freshness.v1` §2.10). The status is state.
- **The fault.** When its guard first holds its writes, an owner MUST
  publish a fault with code `clock_ahead` and level `FAILED`. `[Sc: §4]`
  - Its `detail` names the offset it measured, where it has one.
  - A fault is a stream sample, not state, so the guard does not hold it.
  - While the guard holds, the owner SHOULD publish it again once per
    status interval, 30 s, so that a reader that joined later hears it.
  - Once the guard releases, the owner publishes no more `clock_ahead`
    faults for that drift, and re-puts its status (`freshness.v1` §2.10).
    The confirmed status is the evidence that the drift ended.
- **The status goes stale, by design.** 60 s after its last confirmation,
  a reader judges it stale (`freshness.v1` §2.10). It does not turn
  `FAILED`: the level the owner last put stands as its last word, aging.
  `[Sc: §4]`
- **The routers and the fault's stamp.** A session with its HLC enabled
  stamps every put it is not given a stamp for, a stream sample included
  (zenoh 1.10.1, core Appendix B). The fault therefore carries a stamp
  from the owner's clock, which is ahead. A router re-stamps such a
  future-dated put, or drops it when it runs with
  `timestamping.drop_future_timestamp` (core §4.1).
  - A deployment that sets `drop_future_timestamp` loses the fault, and its
    readers see only the stale status.
  - A reader does not age a fault by its stamp, nor by `at_ns`, which is
    the owner's clock.

### 2.6 Unknown levels

- **`UNSPECIFIED` means unknown.** `LEVEL_UNSPECIFIED` (0) is not a level.
  Neither is a number the enum does not list, which proto3 decodes as that
  number (its enums are open). A reader MUST read either as unknown, and
  never as `OK`: a status at an unknown level answers "is this service
  healthy?" unobservable, unless a check answers *no* (§2.11).
  `[F: judgements.json]`
- **A payload that does not decode** as `health.v1.Status` is unknown too.
  `[F: judgements.json]`
- **When an owner puts it.** An owner puts `UNSPECIFIED` only while it
  cannot tell: at start (§2.3), or for a check it cannot run (§2.3).
- **A level a later minor adds** reads as unknown to a reader built before
  it. That is the safe direction: unknown, never `OK`.
- A check at an unknown level bounds nothing (§2.2). A fault at one is an
  occurrence of unknown severity.

### 2.7 The tokenless set

- **Recommended near the budget.** A deployment near the presence budget
  (core §8.3) SHOULD put `health.v1` in its owners' tokenless set (core
  §8.1, U22). Every service implements it, so its interface token adds one
  token per service and says nothing the instance token does not.
- **Found by descriptor, never by token.** A service implements
  `health.v1` when its descriptor lists `health.v1` in `interfaces`,
  whether `"token"` is `true` or `false` (core §3.3). A reader MUST find
  `health.v1`'s providers through instance tokens and descriptors, and
  MUST NOT conclude that a service does not implement `health.v1` from a
  missing `alive/health.v1/…` token. `[F: judgements.json]` `[Sc: §5]`
  - A present service whose descriptor does not list `health.v1`: health is
    not asked of it.
  - A present service whose descriptor could not be read: unobservable.
- **The split-brain check** does not cover a tokenless interface (core
  §8.1, §6). Two instances of one service putting one status are §6's to
  find, through their instance tokens.

### 2.8 Constrained faces

- **Presence does not cross.** Across a constrained face (core §8.5),
  neither tokens nor descriptors cross. A consumer knows that a service
  implements `health.v1` from its deployment, as it binds statically (core
  R7).
- **A status that crosses is liveness evidence.** It is the one state
  every `health.v1` service holds with a horizon. Where the face lets
  `state/status` cross, a subscriber across it judges the service's
  liveness from it (core R7, `freshness.v1` §2.9), and its health from its
  level. `[Sc: §8]`
- **Stale across a face is not down.** The link may be out, and the
  service up (`freshness.v1` §2.9, §2.11).
- **Nothing crossed yet.** A subscriber across the face that has received
  nothing of the status knows of no status: presence does not cross, so it
  cannot tell a service that never started from one gone silent
  (`freshness.v1` §2.5, "a member it knows"). Its health is unobservable,
  never stale. Once a delivery has crossed, silence ages it. On the near
  side a present, listed owner holds a status (§2.3), so there silence
  ages it from the start. `[F: judgements.json]`
- **A face closed to the status.** Where the face does not let
  `state/status` cross, health is unobservable across it, whatever else
  crosses. A tool MUST say so, and MUST NOT report the service unhealthy,
  failed or down. `[F: judgements.json]` `[Sc: §8]`
- **Faults alone.** A face that lets `stream/faults` cross, and not the
  status, gives the far side the occurrences (a `clock_ahead`, the
  application's faults) and no heartbeat. `faults` has no horizon, so its
  silence is its normal state, and says nothing of liveness or health.
- **The cost** across the face is the status's re-put: one put per service
  every 30 s (`freshness.v1` §2.4), and each check change.
- **zenoh-modem's RF face lets nothing of `health.v1` cross.** In
  `examples/zk2/zenoh-modem/link.md`, `face-deny-device-state` denies
  `zk2/h-3fa9c2d41b7e/rf0/*/state/**`, the status included, and
  `face-deny-companion-streams` denies `…/rf0/health.v1/stream/**`.
  Health is unobservable from the ground, and liveness comes from
  `modem.v3`'s four link streams (`freshness.v1` §2.9; the mapping's gap
  14). A gap of that mapping: Appendix B.

### 2.9 Device-as-service

- **Each device service publishes its own health.** A device service
  (core §1.5) implements `health.v1` for itself: its status says how well
  it serves its device. zenoh-modem derives it from the device's `oper`,
  and ZenSight gives each SNMP device service its own (Appendix B).
- **A parent's status is its own.** It speaks for the parent service's
  own function (polling, say), and not for the devices it serves as
  services. A tool reads each device service's health from that service's
  status, never from its parent's (§2.2).
- **A device modelled as a member** (core §8.1, a member token per member)
  is not a service, and has no status of its own. Its service MAY report it
  as a check named for the member, within its 64 checks (§2.3). A service
  with more members than that reports them in its own interfaces.

### 2.10 Fault codes

- **The form.** A fault's `code` is `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*`: an ASCII lowercase
  letter, then ASCII lowercase letters, digits and `_`. An owner MUST
  write its codes in that form. `[F: codes.json]`
- **The profile's codes.** This table is the profile's vocabulary of
  codes. A code in it means what the table says, from any owner.

  | Code | Level | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | `clock_ahead` | `FAILED` | The owner's clock is ahead of its router beyond the delta, and its state writes are held (§2.5, core §4.3). |

  A later text of this major MAY add codes. That changes no contract.
- **An application's codes** are every other code in the form. A reader
  shows them as written, and interprets none. An application SHOULD prefix
  its codes with a name of its own and `_` (`tc_netns_gone`,
  `modem_link_down`), so that a code this profile adds later is not already
  in use with another meaning.
- **A code not in the form** is still an occurrence: a reader shows it
  verbatim, and MAY report it as malformed. `[F: codes.json]`

### 2.11 What a reader concludes, in order

A reader answers "is this service healthy?" from one reading by the first
of these steps that answers. `[F: judgements.json]`
1. **Presence** (core §8.1):
   - absent, a complete read without its instance token: **not asked**,
     `absent` (§2.1). An archive's status, if one was read, is shown as
     last-known (core S6), and plays no part;
   - its token not seen, in a read possibly incomplete or refused (core
     §8.1): **unobservable**, `presence_incomplete`;
   - across a constrained face, where the deployment's word stands for the
     descriptor: when the face does not let `state/status` cross,
     **unobservable**, `face_closed` (§2.8); otherwise step 3;
   - present: step 2.
2. **The descriptor** (§2.7): `health.v1` not in its `interfaces`, **not
   asked**, `not_listed`; the descriptor not read, **unobservable**,
   `no_descriptor`. The interface token plays no part.
3. **The status's freshness**, `freshness.v1` §2.7 over every observation
   of `state/status`:
   - stale because nothing was delivered, across a constrained face:
     **unobservable**, `nothing_crossed` (§2.8);
   - stale otherwise: **stale**, with `freshness.v1`'s reason;
   - unobservable: **unobservable**, with `freshness.v1`'s reason;
   - not asked because only an archive was read: **not asked**,
     `last_known` (core S6);
   - not asked because the status is deleted: **unobservable**, `deleted`.
     The owner broke §2.3;
   - fresh: step 4.
4. **The status's level**: a level, or unknown (§2.6). Then step 5.
5. **The checks** read with the status (§2.4). Only current checks count.
   Checks not read count as none.
   - With the status at a level:
     - a current check worse than the status: **unhealthy**,
       `inconsistent`, at the effective level (§2.2);
     - otherwise, by the status's level: `OK` is **healthy**, `ok`;
       `DEGRADED` is **unhealthy**, `degraded`; `FAILED` is **unhealthy**,
       `failed`.
   - With the status unknown:
     - a current check `DEGRADED` or `FAILED`: **unhealthy**, `check`, at
       the worst check's level. A *no* can rest on a check;
     - otherwise: **unobservable**, `unknown_level`, or `undecodable` for a
       payload that did not decode. A *yes* rests on the status alone.

The answer is a verdict, a reason, and, for healthy and unhealthy, the
level it rests on.

## 3. Contract

The standard contract is [`health.v1.toml`](health.v1.toml), with its
types in [`proto/health/v1/health.proto`](proto/health/v1/health.proto).
Revision 1.0 is
`sha256:e9dbcdcb2fc325ca3121fdcacf7badd0091f62d97ab4c7d7a5f022dd8d6671df`,
published in [`../.history/health.v1/`](../.history/health.v1/) (core
§9.7). It was written first among the walkthrough's examples, and the
same bundle is in `examples/zk2/.history/health.v1/`, which is
append-only. A new revision is published in `spec/profiles/.history/`.

| Resource | Kind | Type | QoS | Annotations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `status` | `state` | `health.v1.Status` `{level, reason, since_ns}` | `reliable`, `block` (state's default) | `freshness.ttl_s = 60` |
| `checks/{check}` | `state`, `check` a string, cardinality 64 | `health.v1.Check` `{level, detail, checked_at_ns}` | `reliable`, `block` | none |
| `faults` | `stream` | `health.v1.Fault` `{code, level, detail, at_ns}` | `reliable`, `block` | none |

- **`Level`** is `LEVEL_UNSPECIFIED` = 0, `LEVEL_OK` = 1,
  `LEVEL_DEGRADED` = 2 and `LEVEL_FAILED` = 3 (§2.1, §2.6).
- **No application field.** `Status` carries the level, a reason and when
  the level last changed, and nothing an application adds. An application's
  own numbers stay in its own interfaces (Appendix A).
- The interface `uses = ["freshness.v1"]`, for the status's horizon.

## 4. Vocabulary

None. `health.v1` defines no annotation key (§1). Its codes (§2.10) are
payload values, not annotations.

## 5. What a tool may conclude

"Not asked" (nobody put the question, or its premise does not hold) and
"unobservable" (it was put, and the reading could not answer it) are
different facts, and neither is a "no" (core O5). The fixture
[`judgements.json`](conformance/judgements.json) states the verdict
tokens: `healthy`, `unhealthy`, `stale`, `unobservable`, `not_asked`, each
with a reason class (§2.11).

| Question | Yes | No | Unestablished | Finding on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is this service healthy? (`healthy`, `unhealthy`, `stale`) | Present and listed, or across a face that lets the status cross; its status fresh (§2.4) at `OK`; no current check worse (§2.2). | **Unhealthy:** a fresh status at `DEGRADED` or `FAILED`; a current check worse than the status (`inconsistent`, at the check's level); a status at an unknown level with a current check `DEGRADED` or `FAILED` (`check`). **Stale**, its own verdict: the status not confirmed within 60 s, by `freshness.v1`; never `FAILED`, never down (§2.4, §2.8). | **Not asked:** absent (presence's word, §2.1); the descriptor does not list `health.v1` (§2.7); only an archive's status was read, which is last-known (core S6). **Unobservable:** the presence read possibly incomplete or refused; the descriptor not read; a face closed to the status, or nothing of it crossed yet (§2.8); the status's freshness unobservable (a GET silent, an untrusted clock, the age near the horizon, `freshness.v1` §2.5, §2.6); the status deleted (§2.3); its level unknown, or its payload undecodable, with no check bad (§2.6). | No: unhealthy and stale are findings. |
| Does its status agree with its checks? (§2.2) | A fresh status, and no current check worse. | A current check worse than a fresh status at a level: a finding about the owner, which a tool reports from two readings a grace apart (§2.2). | **Not asked:** as the first row's. **Unobservable:** the status not fresh, its level unknown, or the checks not read. | No. |
| How healthy are these services? (a roll-up, §2.2) | The worst established level, `OK`. | The worst established level, `DEGRADED` or `FAILED`. | Each stale, unobservable or not-asked service counted apart; no established verdict at all: no worst level. `[F: rollups.json]` | No, and every stale service. |
| Is this service's clock ahead? (§2.5) | A `clock_ahead` fault from it, with no confirmation of its status heard since. | Its status confirmed, by a subscriber, after its last `clock_ahead` fault, or with none heard: its guard did not hold that write. Never from the faults' silence. | **Unobservable:** no fault heard, and the status not confirmed within the reader's window. **Not asked:** as the first row's. | Yes. |

**Which cause** a stale status has (the owner stopped, its writer closed,
its clock ahead, the link down) is unobservable from the status alone. A
`clock_ahead` fault names one, and presence tells an absent owner apart,
where it crosses (`freshness.v1` §2.11).

## Appendix A. Against v1

v1's health was the **health document**, `state/<producer>/health`, on
the `v1` branch:
- in the framework state set (RFC 04 §1.4), with the `common` token
  `health`;
- an application's document: ZenSight's `HealthSnapshot`, carrying
  `host_id` and `source`, which made it one of the two identity bridges
  (RFC 06 §6.2);
- refreshed at ≤ `ttl_s`/2 and aged by the staleness contract (RFC 04
  §1.2), with the producer's `alive` token absent meaning "suspect";
- allowed one neutral object, `self_stats` (RFC 04 §1.2, v1.32), which
  RFC 13 §3's budget judge read;
- with ZenSight's `@/status` running/offline flag merged into its status
  field (RFC 11 §3).

**What changed.**

| v1 | `health.v1` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An application's document, no level defined | A standard contract with three ordered levels and their meaning (§2.1, §3) | A tool reads any service's health without the application's source. |
| One document | A status, named checks, and a stream of faults (§2.3) | A level, its parts and its occurrences change at different rates, and only the status needs a heartbeat (§2.4). |
| No rule between the parts | The status never better than its worst current check, with the puts ordered (§2.2) | A reader can trust a status without reading 64 checks, and can tell when it should not. |
| `alive` absent ⇒ suspect, then stale | Absent is presence's word, stale is freshness's, and neither is a level (§2.1, §2.4) | Core R7 and O5; "suspect" was a fourth state no tool could act on. |
| `host_id` and `source` in the document | The system and service are in the key, and the host in the descriptor's `meta` | zk2's key carries the identity (core §1.1), so no bridge is needed. |
| The clock: nothing | A clock running ahead is a `clock_ahead` fault, and the status goes stale (§2.5) | Core §4.3: an owner ahead stops writing state, so a status cannot report it. |
| Found by `v1/*/state/*/health` | Found by instance tokens and descriptors, tokenless or not (§2.7) | U22: the framework set's interface tokens are what a deployment above the presence budget cuts. |

**What deliberately did not change.**
- **The refresh** at half the horizon, by `freshness.v1`.
- **One status per service,** retired by nobody but its owner.
- **Silence is not a verdict.** A missing document, or a missing fault, is
  unobservable, never "healthy".

**What is not ported.**
- **`self_stats`** and the budget judge. A population bound is core
  (§2.2, `cardinality`); a process's own memory is an application's
  number, in its own interface.
- **Application fields on the status.** Decided for #721: `Status`
  carries no application-specific attribute. ZenSight's `HealthSnapshot`
  fields (`devices_total`, `devices_responding`, `self_stats`) and
  zenwatch's counters stay in each application's own interfaces, or
  become checks.
- **The identity bridge** (RFC 06 §6.2): see "What changed".
- **The `sensor` registration document** (RFC 04 §5): the descriptor
  replaces it (core §3.3).

**The record.** `docs/zk2/architecture.md` §3.12 lists
`health.v1` as "Health interface", §4.7 puts it "on every service", and
its S7 row (its line 550) says an owner ahead "reports through
`health.v1`". This text makes that report a fault (§2.5), since the
status, being state, is what an owner ahead stops writing.

## Appendix B. The adopters' mappings

| Adopter | Its v1 health | In `health.v1` |
|---|---|---|
| tcgui (`examples/zk2/tcgui/README.md`) | `BackendHealthStatus` | The status. `host_id` and `backend_name` go to the descriptor's `meta`; the namespace and interface counts are dropped, derivable from the interfaces' states. |
| ZenSight (`examples/zk2/zensight/README.md`) | `HealthSnapshot` on every producer | The status, on every service, with checks where a part has a name; per device service under device-as-service (§2.9). Its application fields stay in ZenSight's interfaces (Appendix A). |
| zenwatch (`examples/zk2/zensight/README.md`, its `publish.rs`) | its own `health` document | The status, beside an interface of its own for its rules' state. |
| zenoh-modem (`examples/zk2/zenoh-modem/README.md`) | none: devices report `oper` | Recommended on each device service, its status from `oper`: `up` is `OK`; `down` and recoverable is `DEGRADED`; `down` and not recoverable is `FAILED`. |

**A gap: zenoh-modem's RF face.** The mapping recommends `health.v1` on
each device service, and its RF face (`link.md`) denies the device's state
and `health.v1`'s streams (§2.8). Nothing of `health.v1` crosses, so the
ground sees none of it: liveness comes from the four link streams, and
health is unobservable there. A deployment that wanted the ground to see a
device's health would let `state/status` cross, at one put every 30 s; one
that let `faults` cross alone would get the occurrences without a
heartbeat. The mapping's `link.md` is unchanged by this text.
