# `health.v2`: how well a service serves, in packed records

| | |
|---|---|
| **Text version** | 0.2 ([`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md)) |
| **Status** | experimental |
| **Written against** | core 0.113 ([`../../core.md`](../../core.md)) |
| **Uses** | [`freshness.v2`](../freshness/v2.md), text 0.12: the status's horizon, its heartbeat, and the heartbeat's ahead time (§2.4, §2.5). It restates [`health.v1`](v1.md)'s rules by reference, text 0.11, and replaces its records, its words and its clock question. |
| **Evidence** | [`conformance/v2/`](conformance/v2/), [`scenarios-v2.md`](scenarios-v2.md), and `health.v1`'s for the rules it keeps |

`health.v1` lets a service say how well it serves, and lets a reader tell
that word from the service's absence, from a word gone stale and from no
word at all. Its records are protobuf messages with free text: 33 bytes
for a status, 43 for a check and 56 for a fault, as measured.

`health.v2` keeps every rule of `health.v1` and changes three things:
- **The records are packed,** each of one size: a status and a check in
  6 bytes, a fault in 8 (core §7.6). An owner needs no protobuf and no
  text to write them.
- **The words are numbers.** A reason, a detail and a fault's code are
  indices into tables: the profile's, and the service's own, which one of
  its contracts holds (§2.10).
- **A heartbeat answers the clock question alone.** While an owner's
  clock guard holds, its heartbeat's time says so (core §7.5). The owner
  publishes its `clock_ahead` fault once, and no more each period (§2.5).

Its standard contract, [`health.v2.toml`](health.v2.toml), gives a
service the same three resources:

```text
zk2/<system>/<service>/health.v2/state/status          one Status: the service's level, vouched for every 30 s
zk2/<system>/<service>/health.v2/state/checks/{check}  up to 64 Checks: named parts of that level, put on change
zk2/<system>/<service>/health.v2/stream/faults         Faults: occurrences a level cannot carry, clock_ahead first
```

Nothing is sent again unchanged, as since `health.v1`'s revision 1.1: the
owner's heartbeat confirms the status. So this major saves few bytes on a
quiet service. What it gives is one wire: small records of one size, and
a clock question that needs no fault stream.

---

## 0. Conventions

The keywords, the roles and the evidence tags are core §0's. Here,
`[F: <file>]` names a fixture in [`conformance/`](conformance/), and
`[Sc: §n]` a section of [`scenarios-v2.md`](scenarios-v2.md). `v1 §n`
names a section of [`health.v1`](v1.md), and a fixture of v1's says so,
as in `[F: v1 rollups.json]`.

**By reference.** Every rule of `health.v1` holds for an owner, a reader
and a tool of this major, as written, except where §2 replaces it.
- Where a rule of v1 names `health.v1`, the interface or its keys, it
  reads `health.v2` and its keys.
- Where it names a level, it reads the level of the same name in lower
  case. `UNSPECIFIED` reads `unknown`.
- Where it names `since_ns`, `checked_at_ns` or `at_ns`, it reads
  `since` or `at` (§2.3). Where it names a free-text `reason` or
  `detail`, or a code's text, it reads a word (§2.10).

Terms are v1 §0's, and these:
- **Level:** one of `ok`, `degraded` and `failed`, in that order.
  `unknown` is not a level (§2.6).
- **Record:** a `Status`, a `Check` or a `Fault` of the standard packed
  schema (§3).
- **Word:** a status's `reason`, a check's `detail` or a fault's `code`:
  a number from 0 to 63 (§2.10). Its **kind** is one of those three.
- **Table:** the names of the words of one kind. Each kind has the
  profile's table and, where the service declares one, the service's
  own (§2.10).
- **Ahead time:** core §7.5's code for a heartbeat's time, written
  while its owner's clock guard holds.
- **Read heartbeat:** `freshness.v2` §2.5's. A heartbeat or an owner
  heartbeat that a reader's steps read, whatever its sections say.
- **One instant:** a reading whose two snapshots show the owner's state
  as of one seq (§2.2).

## 2. Rules

### 2.1 The levels

v1 §2.1 holds: what `ok`, `degraded` and `failed` say, and that `failed`
is a present owner's word about itself.
- **Absent is presence's word, not a level.** A reader MUST NOT report
  an absent service `failed`, or at any level: health is not asked of
  it. `[F: v2/judgements.json]` `[Sc: §6]` No record takes part: the
  owner's instance token is what a complete read lacks (core §8.1).

### 2.2 Aggregation

v1 §2.2 holds: no roll-up across services, and a tool's roll-up.
`[F: v1 rollups.json]` These items are restated.
- **The status is never better than its checks.** An owner MUST NOT hold
  a status better than its worst current check. It MAY hold a worse one.
  `[F: v2/judgements.json]` `[Sc: §3, §7]`
  - A check at `unknown`, and one whose record a reader refuses (§2.6),
    bound nothing.
- **The order of the puts, by seq.** Every put and delete of state
  carries its owner's seq, one count for the status and the checks (core
  §4.3). An owner MUST order its puts so that, for each seq of its run,
  the status as of that seq is not better than the worst current check
  as of it. `[Sc: §3]`
  - A check that gets worse than the status: the status first, then the
    check.
  - A check that gets better, or is deleted: the check first, then the
    status, if it improves.
  - That is v1's order. The seq is what lets a reader check it.
- **One instant.** A GET over the owner's `health.v2/state/**` is
  answered by two snapshots, the status's and the checks' (core §4.2).
  A reading is **one instant** when all of these hold:
  - both snapshots are complete (core §7.5);
  - they are under one run, in width and value, and one asof;
  - no live entry and no tombstone of that run has a seq above that
    asof. An entry that states another run is an earlier run's, and
    breaks nothing.

  Each snapshot then shows its collection as of that asof exactly, so
  the two were current together. `[F: v2/snapshots.json]`
- **An owner SHOULD build both snapshots of one GET at one asof.**
  `[Sc: §7]` A reading of an owner that does not is never one instant,
  and a tool falls back to v1's two readings.
- **A status better than a check** breaks the rule. A reader reads the
  service at the effective level, and says why (§2.11).
  - A tool MAY report the break itself, as a finding about the owner,
    from one reading that is one instant. `[F: v2/snapshots.json]`
    `[Sc: §7]`
  - From any other reading it SHOULD see the break in two readings a
    grace apart, as v1 §2.2 says. `[Sc: §7]` A subscription's delivery
    paired with a GET's reply can pair two values that were never
    current together.

### 2.3 Who puts what, and when

v1 §2.3 holds: every service, the status before the tokens, confirmed
every 30 s, never deleted while the service runs, checks put on change,
and faults as occurrences. `[Sc: §1, §2, §3]` These items change.
- **One major per instance.** An instance has one status. An owner MUST
  NOT implement `health.v1` and `health.v2` in one instance. `[Sc: §1]`
  A service moves from one to the other with a new instance (§2.12).
- **An owner that has not yet established its level** puts `unknown`
  with the reason `starting`, and puts its level once it knows it
  (§2.6).
- **`since`** is when the level last changed, on the owner's clock, in
  whole seconds from the deployment epoch (core §7.6). A change of level
  sets it. A change of `reason` alone keeps it. A reader shows it, and
  never ages the status by it (§2.4).
  - **It is optional.** An owner whose clock reads no instant the epoch
    codes leaves it absent. A device with no real-time clock at boot
    reads 1970. Under a catalog's later epoch that instant has no code,
    and core §7.6 forbids a record written for it.
  - So such a device still puts its status, in 2 bytes.
- **A check's `at`** is when the check found the value put, on the
  owner's clock, in whole seconds. It is optional as `since` is, and
  informative: a reader never ages a check by it.
- **A check it can no longer run** is put at `unknown`, with the detail
  `cannot_run` or one of its own, or deleted, as in v1.
- **A fault** carries its `code` (§2.10), its `level`, an `argument` and
  an optional `at`.
  - Its `level` is the occurrence's severity, as in v1.
  - Its `argument` is the code's own number, where the code's table says
    what it counts. It is `not_available` where the code takes none.
  - Its `at` is when it happened, on the owner's clock, in whole
    seconds.
- **Nothing is put again unchanged.** The heartbeat confirms the status
  (`freshness.v2` §2.4), and a check is put when its level or its detail
  changes. `[Sc: §1, §3]`

| Resource | Put | Deleted | Confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `state/status` | before step 4, then on every change | never while the service runs | by its heartbeat, at least every 30 s, never re-put (`freshness.v2` §2.4) |
| `state/checks/{check}` | on every change of level or detail | when retired | never: the status vouches for it (§2.4) |
| `stream/faults` | on each occurrence, a `clock_ahead` once per hold (§2.5) | not state | never: each sample stands alone |

### 2.4 Freshness, and what a fresh status vouches for

v1 §2.4 holds, word for word: the status is judged by `freshness.v2` at
60 s, stale is not a level, and a fresh status vouches for the owner's
checks. `[F: v2/judgements.json]` `[Sc: §2]`
- **A reader MUST NOT report a stale status** as `failed`, as
  `degraded`, or at its last level as though current.
  `[F: v2/judgements.json]` `[Sc: §2]`
- **How a late joiner reads.** It GETs the owner's `health.v2/state/**`
  (core S4). The status's snapshot holds one entry, with its
  confirmation: vouched for at the snapshot's time, or its last
  confirmation (`freshness.v2` §2.4). The checks' snapshot holds every
  current check, and each one retired within the window as a tombstone.
  `[F: v2/snapshots.json]` `[Sc: §1]`
  - One that only subscribes holds nothing of a status put before it
    joined. Its first heartbeats diverge, and `freshness.v2` §2.5's
    re-read seeds it.
- **The heartbeat is unchanged.** The status is the contract's one
  collection, of one member. Its section is a 32-bit digest and one bit,
  in a body of 10 bytes (core §7.5).

### 2.5 A clock running ahead

This section replaces v1 §2.5.
- **The status is not held.** An owner whose clock guard finds it ahead
  of its router beyond the delta stops only the state writes that carry
  a Zenoh timestamp (core §4.3). The status and the checks carry none on
  a session without timestamping (`freshness.v2` §2.4). So the owner
  puts them as at any other time, and its heartbeat goes on vouching for
  its status. `[Sc: §4]`
- **The status does not say it.** A clock ahead is no level. An owner
  that the drift leaves worse off puts its status as §2.2 says.
- **The heartbeat says it.** While its guard holds, an owner writes the
  ahead time as its heartbeat's time (`freshness.v2` §2.10, core §7.5).
  A reader that hears one heartbeat knows.
- **The fault, once.** When its guard begins to hold, an owner MUST
  publish one fault with the code `clock_ahead` and the level `failed`.
  `[Sc: §4]`
  - Its `argument` is the offset it measured, in whole seconds, rounded
    up. An offset of 65,534 s or more is written 65,534. Where it
    measured none, it is `not_available`. `[F: v2/records.json]`
  - A fault is a stream sample, not state, so the guard does not hold
    it.
  - While that guard holds, the owner SHOULD NOT publish the fault
    again. `[Sc: §4]` Its heartbeats repeat the word each period, at no
    cost.
  - A guard that releases and later holds again begins a new hold, with
    a fault of its own.
- **Once the guard releases,** the owner's next heartbeat carries an
  instant again: the evidence that the drift ended.
- **How a reader reads it.** A reader MUST answer §5's clock question
  exactly as the fixture does. `[F: v2/clock.json]` `[Sc: §4]` The last
  heartbeat it read answers:
  - its time is ahead: **yes**, `ahead`;
  - its time is an instant: **no**, `confirmed`. The owner's guard did
    not hold when it wrote it;
  - its time is unknown: **unobservable**, `time_unknown`. The owner's
    clock reads no instant, which says nothing of ahead;
  - none read: **unobservable**, `nothing_heard`.
- **Which heartbeats count.** A heartbeat covering `health.v2` counts,
  and an owner heartbeat of the status's class, `host` (`freshness.v2`
  §2.4), each where it is a read heartbeat. Its sections play no part:
  the time is the owner's word of its clock.
  - A heartbeat of another revision, a refused one and an older one are
    not read, and count for nothing.
- **Whether it is asked** is §2.11's first two steps: an absent
  service, and one whose descriptor lists neither major, are **not
  asked**. In every other case the heartbeats answer: a descriptor not
  read and a face closed to the status change nothing.
- **Nothing else answers.** A put of the status and the faults' silence
  say nothing of the clock.
  - **Nor does the fault.** A `clock_ahead` fault says that a hold began
    when it was published. It is published once, so it cannot say that
    the hold lasts. A reader shows it as the occurrence it is, with its
    offset, and answers from the heartbeats.
- **So a late joiner is answered within one period,** by the first
  heartbeat it reads. Where the status crosses a face, its heartbeat
  crosses with it (v1 §2.8), and the answer with the heartbeat.
- **No Zenoh timestamp.** A fault of this major is outside core §4.3's
  table, which lists `health.v1`'s alone. So its put carries none on an
  owner's session (core §8.2).
  - No router then re-stamps it or drops it as a future-dated put (core
    §4.1). v1 §2.5's items on the routers and the fault's stamp have
    nothing to say here.
  - Its `at` is the owner's clock, which is ahead during a drift. A
    reader ages nothing by it.

### 2.6 Unknown levels

This section replaces v1 §2.6.
- **`unknown` is no level.** It is the record's code 0, v1's
  `UNSPECIFIED`. A reader MUST read a status at `unknown` as unknown,
  never as `ok`: it answers "is this service healthy?" unobservable,
  unless a check answers *no* (§2.11). `[F: v2/judgements.json]`
- **A record a reader refuses is unknown too.** Core §7.6's reading
  refuses a record by its tag, its length or its pad. A reader MUST read
  a status whose record it refuses as unknown, never as `ok`, and never
  at a level read from its bits. `[F: v2/records.json]`
  `[F: v2/judgements.json]`
  - The reason is `undecodable`, as for v1's payload that does not
    decode.
- **Every code is a value.** `level` fills its 2 bits, so no number is
  left unlisted. A fifth level would take a new major.
- **When an owner puts it.** An owner puts `unknown` only while it
  cannot tell: at start, or for a check it cannot run (§2.3).
- A check at `unknown`, or refused, bounds nothing (§2.2). A fault at
  `unknown` is an occurrence of unknown severity.

### 2.7 The tokenless set

v1 §2.7 holds. A reader MUST find `health.v2`'s providers through
instance tokens and descriptors, and MUST NOT conclude that a service
does not implement `health.v2` from a missing `alive/health.v2/…` token.
`[F: v2/judgements.json]` `[Sc: §5]`

### 2.8 Constrained faces

v1 §2.8 holds: presence does not cross, a status that crosses is
liveness evidence, stale across a face is not down, nothing crossed yet,
a face closed to the status, and faults alone. `[F: v2/judgements.json]`
`[Sc: §8]` These items change.
- **The standard contract exposes nothing `link`.** No resource of it
  declares `link.exposure`, as none of v1's does: a standard contract's
  exposure would be every deployment's.
  - So its status is of the `host` class (`freshness.v2` §2.4). An owner
    heartbeat that covers it is the `host` key's, which crosses no face
    where `freshness.v2` is listed (`freshness.v2` §2.9).
  - A face that lets the status cross carries the heartbeat covering
    `health.v2`, as v1 §2.8 says.
- **The clock question crosses with the heartbeat.** A far reader that
  hears the heartbeat covering `health.v2` answers it (§2.5). One that
  hears the faults alone sees the occurrences, a `clock_ahead` among
  them, and answers the question unobservable.
- **The cost** across a face is the heartbeat, a 16-byte payload every
  28.5 s. Each change of the status or a check adds a put of 12 bytes,
  and each fault one of 14 (Appendix B).
- **zenoh-modem's RF face** lets nothing of `health.v1` cross today, as
  v1 §2.8 says. Its mapping moves with the reference's default (§2.12).

### 2.9 Device-as-service

v1 §2.9 holds, each device service implementing one major for itself.

### 2.10 Words

This section replaces v1 §2.10. A status's `reason`, a check's `detail`
and a fault's `code` are **words**: numbers from 0 to 63, 6 bits each.

| Number | Whose | Named by |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | none given | nothing: the owner gives no word |
| 1 to 31 | the profile's | the tables below |
| 32 to 62 | the service's own | its own table of that kind (§4) |
| 63 | no table's | nothing: the record's `out_of_range` |

- **The profile's tables.** A number in them means what the table says,
  from any owner.

  | Reason | Name | Meaning |
  |---:|---|---|
  | 1 | `starting` | The owner has not yet established its level (§2.3). |
  | 2 | `serving` | It serves its contracts as configured. |
  | 3 | `stopping` | It is closing. |
  | 4 | `check` | Its level is its worst current check's (§2.2). The checks say which. |
  | 5 | `upstream_lost` | Something it depends on is gone. |
  | 6 | `fallback` | It serves by a fallback. |
  | 7 | `backlog` | Its work accumulates faster than it does it. |
  | 8 | `misconfigured` | Its configuration keeps it from its job. |

  | Detail | Name | Meaning |
  |---:|---|---|
  | 1 | `ok` | The check passed. |
  | 2 | `unreachable` | What it probes could not be reached. |
  | 3 | `timeout` | The probe ended at its timeout. |
  | 4 | `refused` | What it probes refused it. |
  | 5 | `cannot_run` | The check could not be run (§2.3). |

  | Code | Name | Level | Argument | Meaning |
  |---:|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | `clock_ahead` | `failed` | the offset measured, in whole seconds (§2.5) | The owner's clock is ahead of its router beyond the delta, and its state writes that carry a Zenoh timestamp are held (§2.5, core §4.3). |

  `[F: v2/tables.json]`
  - A later text of this major MAY name more numbers from 1 to 31. That
    changes no contract. A reader built before it shows such a word as
    its number.
  - A word states no level. `starting` beside `ok` is a level and a
    reason, each the owner's.
- **A service's own tables.** A service names its own words in up to
  three annotations of one of its own contracts: `health.reasons`,
  `health.details` and `health.codes` (§4). Each is a list of names. The
  name at index i, from 0, is the word 32 + i.
  - **A name** is `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*`, as v1 §2.10's codes are. A table
    holds at most 31, none twice.
  - **Where.** A contract writes each key once, on `[defaults]` (core
    §9.1), where it reaches every resource (core §9.3). The canonical
    form carries it on each (core §9.5), which is where a reader of a
    bundle finds it. The contract lists `health.v2` in `uses` (core
    §10).
  - **v1 §2.10's advice holds.** A service prefixes its names with a
    name of its own and `_`, as in `tc_netns_gone`. A word a later text
    adds is then not already in use with another meaning.
- **A contract's table.** A reader MUST read a contract's tables exactly
  as the fixture does. `[F: v2/tables.json]` For each key, a contract
  holds the table its resources carry, or none. Its declaration is
  invalid, and it holds none, for the first of these:
  - `not_used`: a resource carries the key, and the contract's `uses`
    does not list `health.v2`. A contract that loads cannot hold it
    (core E020). A reader of resolved annotations that come from
    elsewhere can;
  - `value`: a resource's value is not a list of at most 31 names, none
    twice;
  - `disagrees`: two resources carry two different lists.

  A resource that does not carry the key takes no part.
- **An instance's table** of one kind is the one table among the
  contracts it implements: the revisions its descriptor names (core
  §3.3). `[F: v2/tables.json]`
  - Where none holds one, it has none.
  - Where two hold two different lists, it has none (`differ`). Its
    owner then writes no word from 32 to 62 (below).
  - A reader reads it from those of the named revisions it holds. A
    revision it does not hold adds no table, and neither does one it
    holds that the descriptor does not name.
  - Across a constrained face no descriptor crosses. The deployment's
    word names the revisions, as it names the interface (v1 §2.8, core
    §8.6).
- **Writing a word.** An owner MUST write each word as the fixture
  does. `[F: v2/tables.json]`
  - No word given: 0.
  - A name of the profile's table of that kind: its number.
  - A name of its instance's own table: 32 plus its index.
  - Any other word: `out_of_range`. The words stay in the owner's log.
- **Reading a word.** A reader MUST read each word as the fixture does.
  `[F: v2/tables.json]`
  - 0: no word.
  - 1 to 31: the profile's name, or the number where this text names
    none.
  - 32 to 62: the name at that index of the instance's own table, or the
    number where the reader holds no table or the table ends before it.
  - `out_of_range`: a word no table names. A reader shows that, never a
    name.
- **Never another revision's table.** A reader MUST NOT show a name
  read from a revision its owner's descriptor does not name.
  `[F: v2/tables.json]` `[Sc: §9]` A table read under the wrong revision
  gives the wrong word. A reader that holds neither the descriptor nor
  the deployment's word shows numbers.
- **A table grows by appending.** A change to a table is an annotation's
  change, a review (core §9.8). A later revision SHOULD append names,
  and SHOULD NOT remove, reorder or rename one. `[F: v2/tables.json]`
  Then every number keeps its word across revisions.
- **A service with no table** costs nothing: its tools show `32` as a
  number. An owner that declares none writes the profile's words, 0 and
  `out_of_range`.
- **A check's name stays text.** It is the `{check}` parameter in the
  key, slugged like any value (core §1.4).

### 2.11 What a reader concludes, in order

v1 §2.11's five steps hold, read over this major's interface and
records. A reader MUST answer "is this service healthy?" as the fixture
does. `[F: v2/judgements.json]`
- **Step 2, the descriptor.** A descriptor whose `interfaces` holds
  `health.v2` is read by this text. One that holds `health.v1` and not
  `health.v2` is read by `health.v1`'s. One that holds neither: **not
  asked**, `not_listed`.
  - One that holds both breaks §2.3. A reader reads `health.v2`.
- **Step 3, the status's freshness** is `freshness.v2`'s verdict over
  every observation of `state/status`, as in v1.
- **Step 4, the status's level** is its record's `level`. A record at
  `unknown` gives `unknown_level`, and one the reader refuses gives
  `undecodable` (§2.6).
- **Step 5, the checks.** Only current checks count: those the owner
  answers with a record at a level.

The answer is a verdict, a reason, and, for healthy and unhealthy, the
level it rests on. The verdicts and the reasons are v1's.

### 2.12 Beside `health.v1`

- **An owner implements one major** per instance (§2.3). A service moves
  with a new instance, as `config.v1`'s owners move to `config.v2`.
- **A reader reads either.** A reader MUST read a service by the major
  its descriptor lists, and `health.v2` where it lists both.
  `[F: v2/judgements.json]` `[Sc: §1]`
  - A tool that reads a deployment makes two reads: one over
    `zk2/*/*/health.v2/state/**`, one over `…/health.v1/state/**`.
  - Its verdicts, its reasons and its roll-up are one set for both.
- **Readers first.** A reader built before this major finds no
  `health.v1` in a `health.v2` service's descriptor. It answers **not
  asked**, `not_listed`: safe, and blind. A deployment moves its readers
  before its owners.
- **The heartbeat's ahead time** is `freshness.v2`'s, and an owner of
  either major writes it. Only this major answers yes from it:
  `health.v1` reads it as it read the unknown time (v1 §2.5).

## 3. Contract

The standard contract is [`health.v2.toml`](health.v2.toml), with its
types in the packed file [`health.v2.json`](health.v2.json). Revision
2.0 is
`sha256:b447f613bea7bdc24703500280a20bf0cf20fb27cbb38ecd25f5164b0ae005b1`,
published in `../.history/health.v2/` (core §9.7). Its heartbeat's tag
is 1 (core §7.5).

| Resource | Kind | Type | QoS | Annotations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `status` | `state` | `packed:Status` | `reliable`, `block` (state's default) | `freshness.ttl_s = 60` |
| `checks/{check}` | `state`, `check` a string, cardinality 64 | `packed:Check` | `reliable`, `block` | none |
| `faults` | `stream` | `packed:Fault` | `reliable`, `block` | none |

The interface `uses = ["freshness.v2"]`, for the status's horizon. It
declares no `link.exposure` (§2.8).

**The schema.** The packed file's artifact id is
`sha256:d270e1487bb6b2688aa34e5cb55804a14edc61a64e5fdd88d167b21ca4eaa983`
(core §9.4). `[F: v2/schema.json]`

```text
Status   level · reason · since · "..."              42 bits, 6 bytes
Check    level · detail · at · "..."                 42 bits, 6 bytes
Fault    code · level · argument · at · "..."        58 bits, 8 bytes

level      enum unknown, ok, degraded, failed; no escape           2 bits
reason     int 0 to 62, reserved out_of_range                      6
detail     int 0 to 62, reserved out_of_range                      6
code       int 0 to 62, reserved out_of_range                      6
argument   int 0 to 65,534, reserved not_available                 16
since, at  time, no milliseconds, optional                         1 + 32
"..."      the extension marker                                    1
```

- **The tags.** `Status` and `Check` have one layout, so one tag, 3. A
  `Fault`'s is 11 (core §7.6). A status's put has CH `0x03` under a
  16-bit run, and `0x13` under a 32-bit run. A fault's has `0x0b` or
  `0x1b`. `[F: v2/records.json]`
  - The tag tells a status from a check not at all. The key does.
- **The sizes.** With its time, a status and a check are 6 bytes, and a
  fault 8. With none, they are 10 and 26 bits: 2 and 4 bytes.
  `[F: v2/records.json]`
- **In a snapshot** the checks are collection 0 and the status
  collection 1: the contract's `state` resources in the canonical form's
  order (core §7.5). A snapshot's CH carries the tag 3 for either.
  `[F: v2/snapshots.json]`
- **No unused code.** Every code of every field is a value or a declared
  reserved code. So core §7.6's reading refuses a record of these types
  by its tag, its length or its pad, never by a code.
- **It grows behind its marker.** A later text appends optional fields,
  which a reader of this one ignores (core §7.6). A marker read as 1
  ends this text's reading: the record's level and words stand.
- **No application field,** as in v1. An application's own numbers stay
  in its own interfaces.

## 4. Vocabulary

| Key | Type | On | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `health.reasons` | a list of at most 31 names, each `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*`, none twice | any resource; written once, on `[defaults]` | The service's own reasons: the name at index i is the word 32 + i of a status's `reason` (§2.10). |
| `health.details` | the same | the same | The service's own details, for a check's `detail`. |
| `health.codes` | the same | the same | The service's own fault codes, for a fault's `code`. A code's `argument` means what the service says. |

W105 reads this table for a contract that lists `health.v2` (core §10).
Any other key `health.<key>` is outside it. The standard contract writes
none: a service's tables are in a contract of its own.

## 5. What a tool may conclude

The verdict tokens and the reasons are v1 §5's, and
[`judgements.json`](conformance/v2/judgements.json) states them.

| Question | Yes | No | Unestablished | Finding on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is this service healthy? (`healthy`, `unhealthy`, `stale`) | As v1 §5's first row, over `health.v2`: its status fresh at `ok`, no current check worse. | **Unhealthy** and **stale**, as v1's. | **Not asked** and **unobservable**, as v1's. A status at `unknown`, or one whose record the tool refuses, with no check bad: unobservable (§2.6). | No: unhealthy and stale are findings. |
| Does its status agree with its checks? (§2.2) | A fresh status, and no current check worse. | A current check worse than a fresh status at a level: a finding about the owner. A tool reports it from one reading that is one instant, or from two readings a grace apart (§2.2). | As v1's. | No. |
| How healthy are these services? (a roll-up, §2.2) | As v1's, over services of either major. | As v1's. | As v1's. `[F: v1 rollups.json]` | No, and every stale service. |
| Is this service's clock ahead? (§2.5) | The last heartbeat read has its time ahead (`ahead`). | The last heartbeat read has its time an instant (`confirmed`): its guard did not hold then. Never from the faults' silence, nor from a put of the status. | **Unobservable:** the last heartbeat read has its time unknown (`time_unknown`); no heartbeat read in the reader's window, whatever fault was heard (`nothing_heard`). **Not asked:** as the first row's. `[F: v2/clock.json]` | Yes. |

- **A word** is shown as its name, where a table in hand names its
  number (§2.10), and as its number where none does. 0 is shown as no
  word. `out_of_range` is shown as a word no table names: never as a
  name, and never as no word.
- **`since` and `at`** are shown as the owner's clock read them, to the
  second. An absent one is shown as absent, never as the epoch.
- **Which cause** a stale status has is unobservable from the status
  alone, as in v1. A clock ahead is no cause: the guard holds no status
  (§2.5).

## Appendix A. Against `health.v1`

| `health.v1` | `health.v2` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Protobuf records: a status of 33 bytes, a check of 43, a fault of 56, as measured | Packed records: 12, 12 and 14 bytes of payload, 6, 6 and 8 of record (§3) | One wire for a small owner: no protobuf, no text, one size |
| `reason`, `detail` and `code` as text | Words: numbers into the profile's tables and the service's own (§2.10) | A table's word costs 6 bits. A word no table names stays in the owner's log |
| A fault's `detail` as text | A 16-bit `argument`, the code's own number (§2.3) | A number is what a code's reader acts on |
| `since_ns`, `checked_at_ns`, `at_ns`: nanoseconds since the Unix epoch | `since`, `at`: whole seconds from the deployment epoch, optional (§2.3) | A reader shows them and ages nothing by them. A device with no real-time clock still puts its status |
| `UNSPECIFIED`, a number the enum does not list, a payload that does not decode: unknown | `unknown`, a record refused: unknown (§2.6) | The same guarantee: unknown is never `ok` |
| The order of the puts | The same order, stated by seq, and checkable from one GET at one asof (§2.2) | Every put carries the owner's seq, and a snapshot its asof |
| A `clock_ahead` fault again every period, and the heartbeat's time unknown | The heartbeat's ahead time, and one fault per hold (§2.5) | A heartbeat alone answers, where faults do not cross and for a late joiner |
| The clock question: yes from a fault, no from a heartbeat whose time is known | The last heartbeat read: an ahead time is yes, an instant is no. A fault is an occurrence, and answers nothing (§2.5, §5) | The unknown time no longer stands for two things, and a fault published once cannot say that a hold lasts |
| A fault MAY carry its owner's Zenoh timestamp, which a router can re-stamp or drop | A fault carries none (§2.5) | Its `at` is in the record, and a stamp is 26 bytes beside a 14-byte payload |
| One interface, `health.v1` | Two interfaces, an instance implementing one (§2.3, §2.12) | A record's kind cannot change within a major (core §9.8) |

**What deliberately did not change.**
- **The three guarantees.** The status is never better than its worst
  current check. Absent is never `failed`. Unknown is never `ok`.
- **Stale is not a level,** and a fresh status vouches for the checks.
- **The confirmation:** `freshness.v2`'s heartbeat, at 60 s, with no
  re-put. `health.v1`'s revision 1.1 already had it.
- **Three resources, their kinds and their QoS.** `faults` stays a
  stream.
- **§2.11's steps,** their verdicts and their reasons, and the roll-up.

**What is narrowed.**
- **A reason and a detail in words.** A word no table names is
  `out_of_range` on the wire. `health.v1` carried any text.
- **A fault's detail.** It is one number of 16 bits.
- **A time's resolution** is one second, where v1's was a nanosecond.

## Appendix B. The bytes, computed

Every figure is computed from §3's layouts through the wire baseline's
model (`docs/zk2/measurements/wire-baseline/`): RF, the lean stack, the
face's 3-byte frame, a 16-bit run. `health.v1`'s payloads are measured.
No `health.v2` byte has crossed a wire.

| | `health.v1` | `health.v2` | SDU on RF, v1 → v2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| a status put | 33 B | 12 B, a 6-B record | 58 → 37 B |
| a check put | 43 B | 12 B, a 6-B record | 68 → 37 B |
| a fault | 56 B | 14 B, an 8-B record | 81 → 39 B |
| a late joiner's status snapshot | 49 B | 28 B | |
| the heartbeat | 16 B | 16 B | 39 B, 118 KB a day |

- **W2, a day.** Four status changes cost 232 B under `health.v1` and
  148 B here: 84 B less. Health's periodic bytes are its heartbeat's,
  which this major does not change.
- **A clock ahead for an hour.** `health.v1`'s fault each 28.5 s is
  10.2 KB. Here it is one fault, 39 B.

## Appendix C. The adopters' mappings

None uses this major yet. Each adopter's services implement `health.v1`
(v1's Appendix B), and move when the reference's default does (§2.12).
Their catalogs then append `health.v2`, and their existing indices stay
(core §8.6).
