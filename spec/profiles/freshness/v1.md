# `freshness.v1`: how long a value stays current

| | |
|---|---|
| **Text version** | 0.1 ([`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md)) |
| **Status** | draft |
| **Written against** | core 0.21 ([`../../core.md`](../../core.md)) |
| **Uses** | no other profile |
| **Evidence** | [`conformance/`](conformance/), [`scenarios.md`](scenarios.md) |

`freshness.v1` gives a resource a **staleness horizon**: how long one of
its values stays current without being confirmed. An owner confirms a
state member by putting it again, a reader ages what it holds, and a tool
says *fresh*, *stale*, or that it cannot tell.

```toml
[interface]
name = "tc.netif"
major = 1
uses = ["freshness.v1"]

[resources."interfaces/{ns}/{iface}"]
kind        = "state"
type        = "json:NetworkInterface"
params      = { ns = "string", iface = "string" }
cardinality = 1024
annotations = { "freshness.ttl_s" = 60 }   # re-put at least every 30 s; stale after 60 s
```

It defines one annotation, `freshness.ttl_s`, and no contract and no
kind. An instance whose contract uses it lists it in its descriptor's
`profiles`, as core §3.3 lists every profile a contract uses.

---

## 0. Conventions

The keywords, the roles and the evidence tags are core §0's. Here,
`[F: <file>]` names a fixture in [`conformance/`](conformance/),
`[Sc: §n]` a section of [`scenarios.md`](scenarios.md), and `core` marks
the core's own evidence. References to `docs/zk2/architecture.md` (its
decisions D15 and D16) are informative: every rule is stated here.

Terms:
- **Horizon:** a resource's `freshness.ttl_s`, read by §2.1. **ttl** below
  is its value in seconds.
- **Member:** one concrete key of a resource (core §2.2).
- **Confirmation:** a put of a member by its owner, a change or a re-put
  of the same value (§2.4), or a sample of a stream. A delete is not a
  confirmation: it ends the member (core S3).
- **Age:** the time since the last confirmation a reader observed (§2.5,
  §2.6).
- **Fresh:** the age is within the horizon. **Stale:** it is beyond.
- **Reader:** a consumer or a tool that judges freshness. A
  **subscriber** reads deliveries; a **GET reader** reads the owner's
  replies (core S4).
- **The delta:** the HLC's maximum delta of the deployment's routers, 500
  ms unless the deployment configures another (core §4.1).

## 2. Rules

### 2.1 The value

- **A whole number of seconds,** from 0 to 2^53−1. A reader MUST read
  `freshness.ttl_s` exactly as the fixture does. `[F: horizons.json]`
  - **An integral float is that integer:** `60.0` is `60`, and `-0.0` is
    `0`. The canonical form writes both as the integer (core §9.5, JCS), so
    a reader of a bundle cannot tell them apart, and neither may a reader
    of the source.
  - **Not a horizon:** a fraction (`1.5`), a negative number, a number
    above 2^53−1, a string (`"60"`), a boolean, an array or a table. A
    reader MUST NOT judge a resource's freshness from such a value, and
    reports it *unobservable*, naming the value (§5).
    `[F: horizons.json, judgements.json]` A contract linter SHOULD warn of
    one. The core has no code for it: W105 is about keys, and no core lint
    reads an annotation's value.
  - **Why seconds, and no fraction.** Sub-second staleness is
    `timing.v1`'s (`lifespan_ms`, `deadline_ms`), judged on a receiver's
    clock. A GET reader ages a value across two clocks that agree to the
    delta, half a second by default (§2.6): a finer horizon could not be
    told apart from their disagreement.
- **Where it is written.** On a resource, or in `[defaults]` or
  `[defaults.<kind>]` (core §9.3). The horizon is the resolved resource's
  annotation. `[defaults.state]` gives every state resource of a contract
  one horizon, which tcgui's contracts repeat resource by resource
  (`examples/zk2/tcgui/README.md`, gap 2).
- **On a requirement** (`[requires.<role>]`), it has no meaning at this
  version, and a reader ignores it.

### 2.2 Which kinds

| Kind | What freshness means | Judged by |
|---|---|---|
| `state`, `@state` | A member is fresh while its value was confirmed within ttl. Its owner re-puts it (§2.4). | a subscriber (§2.5) and a GET reader (§2.6) |
| `stream`, `@stream` | A member is fresh while a sample arrived within ttl. Its owner publishes often enough (§2.4). | a subscriber only: a stream has no queryable (core §2.1), and its samples carry no owner's stamp (core §4.1) |
| `event` | None. | nobody |
| `operation` | None. | nobody |

- **Events.** An occurrence is never confirmed again: its age is its
  ULID's, and how long it is kept is `retention`'s (core §2.6). The time
  between occurrences is `rate`'s, an upper bound, and silence is an
  event's normal state (`rare`).
- **Operations** hold no value to age.
- **A horizon on an event or an operation is ignored.** `[defaults]`
  spreads annotations to every kind, so a contract may resolve one there
  without meaning it. A reader MUST NOT judge an event's or an operation's
  freshness, whatever its `freshness.ttl_s`. `[F: horizons.json, judgements.json]`
- **Streams.** A stream carries freshness so that a reader across a
  constrained face can judge liveness from what crosses, where no state
  does (§2.9): zenoh-modem's RF face carries four streams and no state
  (`examples/zk2/zenoh-modem/README.md`, gap 14).

### 2.3 No horizon, and never stale

- **No `freshness.ttl_s`:** the contract declares no horizon, so
  freshness is not asked of that resource. A reader MUST NOT report its
  values fresh or stale. `[F: judgements.json]` D16 leaves this case open,
  and this text closes it: no horizon is neither "never stale" nor a
  default horizon.
- **`0`: never stale** (D16): operator intent, desired state, an
  assertion that holds until it is changed or deleted. A reader MUST
  report every value it read of such a resource fresh, whatever its age,
  and needs no clock to do so. `[F: judgements.json]` `[Sc: §3]` Its owner
  does not re-put it (§2.4).
- **No value, no verdict.** A reader that read no value of a member, a
  GET that drew no reply, has nothing to call fresh, whatever the horizon:
  unobservable (§2.6).

### 2.4 The owner's obligation

- **State.** An owner of a state resource whose ttl is above 0 MUST put
  each member it holds at intervals of at most ttl/2. `[Sc: §1]`
  - Every change is a put, and starts the interval again.
  - When the interval ends with no change, the owner **re-puts** the
    member's current value, unchanged: the same payload, `Encoding` and
    attachment.
  - **A re-put is a mutation** (core S1, 0.21). It MUST carry a fresh
    timestamp the owner set, minted as core §4.3 says, so above every
    stamp the owner issued before. The owner's GET answers carry that
    stamp from then on (core S2). A GET reader therefore ages a member
    from its last confirmation, never from its last change. `[Sc: §1]`
  - **What a consumer sees** is a put of the same value under a later
    stamp of the same id. Core §4.3's consumer rule applies it, and it
    changes nothing but the value's age. A consumer that acts on changes
    compares values.
  - **A deleted member is not re-put.** The owner answers the delete within
    the tombstone window (core S3), and nothing after. `[Sc: §1]`
  - **The re-puts stop when the owner stops:** when its process ends,
    when its service closes, or when its writer for that member closes.
    Nothing else re-puts for it: a storage on an owner's state keys is
    refused (core S4), and an archive never puts on them (core §4.4,
    §2.8). `[Sc: §1]`
  - **Only a value it holds current.** A re-put says the value is still
    current. An owner that can no longer vouch for a value, because the
    source it reports went away, stops re-putting it and lets it go stale,
    or deletes it. Re-putting it would turn a stale value fresh.
  - **The bound is not a rate.** An owner MAY put more often. A member
    that changes at least every ttl/2 needs no re-put.
  - **Why half:** one lost put, on a `best_effort` state (core §2.4), still
    leaves a confirmation within ttl. It is v1's refresh rule (Appendix A).
  - **The cost** is at most one put per member every ttl/2: a collection of
    cardinality n with ttl 60 re-puts n values every 30 s. A deployment
    counts it on a constrained face (§2.9).
- **Stream.** An owner of a stream resource whose ttl is above 0 MUST
  publish a sample on each member it publishes at intervals of at most
  ttl/2, while the resource is exposed. `[Sc: §5]`
  - Each is a new sample: a stream's samples each stand alone (core
    §2.1), so repeating one would report one measurement twice. A runtime
    MUST NOT re-publish a stream sample on the owner's behalf.
    `[Sc: §5]` The cadence is the application's.
  - A stream with nothing to say between its samples declares no horizon.
- **ttl 0, or no horizon:** no obligation.

### 2.5 A subscriber's age

A subscriber ages a member on its own **monotonic** clock: the time since
it last received a confirmation of that member, a state put (re-puts
included) or a stream sample. It MUST NOT age a delivery against the
sender's stamp: a delivery's arrival is its evidence, and two clocks'
disagreement plays no part. `[F: judgements.json]` `[Sc: §2, §4]`

It judges a member with ttl above 0 by the first of these that holds:
`[F: judgements.json]`
1. The last delivery is a delete: **not this profile's**, the member has
   no value (§5).
2. The last delivery is at most ttl old: **fresh**.
3. It knows it lost deliveries (it fell behind and dropped some, for
   instance): **unobservable**, since a lost delivery may have been a
   confirmation.
4. The last delivery is older than ttl: **stale**.
5. There was no delivery, and it has listened for longer than ttl:
   **stale**.
6. There was no delivery, and it has listened for ttl or less:
   **unobservable**: it has not listened long enough to tell.

At ttl 0, a member is fresh unless its last delivery is a delete (§2.3).

- **It listens from its declaration.** "Listened" is the time since the
  subscription was declared, on the same clock.
- **A member it knows.** A subscriber judges a member it knows exists:
  one it received, or one a GET answered (§2.7). A subscription alone
  cannot tell a member that was never put from one gone silent.
- **A lost delivery** never makes a member stale. It makes the verdict
  unobservable, unless a later delivery within ttl shows the member fresh.

### 2.6 A GET reader's age

A GET reader ages a state member from its reply's stamp: the age is its
own clock (UTC) at the instant it judges, minus the stamp's time.
`[F: judgements.json]`

- **Only with a trusted clock.** A GET reader MUST NOT age a reply unless
  it trusts that its clock and the clock that stamped the reply (the
  stamp's id, core §4.1) agree within the delta. It trusts them on one of
  two grounds: `[F: judgements.json]` `[Sc: §4]`
  1. **The deployment's word:** it keeps its clocks within the delta (by
     NTP or PTP, for instance). A tool takes this word as an option its
     operator sets, as it takes the admin space's (core §4.2). A word the
     clocks do not keep makes the tool's verdicts wrong, and the error is
     the operator's. `[Sc: §4]`
  2. **A measurement:** it received, live, a put stamped by that clock
     whose stamp was within the delta of its own clock at receipt. Transit
     counts against the bound. Across a slow face a measurement can
     therefore claim more agreement than the clocks have, and a reader
     there relies on the deployment's word.

  Otherwise the age is **unobservable**: never fresh, and never stale.
- **The band.** A trusted clock knows the age to the delta δ, so the
  first of these rows that holds decides: `[F: judgements.json]`

  | Age | Verdict |
  |---|---|
  | below −δ (the stamp is ahead of the reader's clock by more than δ) | unobservable: the clocks disagree beyond what was trusted |
  | at most ttl − δ | fresh |
  | above ttl + δ | stale |
  | otherwise | unobservable: the clocks' disagreement could put it on either side of the horizon |

  At a horizon of δ or less, only a reply stamped ahead of the reader's
  clock can be judged fresh: a subscriber is the reader for such a
  horizon.
- **A reply without a stamp** breaks core S2, and its age is
  unobservable. `[F: judgements.json]`
- **A silent GET**, no reply for the member within the timeout, holds no
  value: unobservable (core O5, S6). So is a member a read did not reach
  before it ended. `[F: judgements.json]`
- **A `reply_del`** says the member is deleted: no value, not this
  profile's (§5). `[F: judgements.json]`
- **The order.** A GET reader judges a member with ttl above 0 by the
  first of these that holds: no reply (unobservable); a `reply_del` (not
  this profile's); no stamp (unobservable); a clock not trusted
  (unobservable); then the band. At ttl 0, any reply that carries a value
  is fresh, with or without a stamp or a trusted clock (§2.3).
  `[F: judgements.json]`
- **Never a router's stamp.** Routers stamp puts, never replies (core
  §4.1), and the owner stamps its replies (core S2): a GET reader ages the
  owner's confirmation. A reply stamped by another clock than the owner's
  breaks core S1, which a tool checks apart.

### 2.7 Several observations of one member

A reader that observed one member several ways, by a subscription and a
GET, judges each, then takes the most recent confirmation any of them
shows. `[F: judgements.json]`
- **Fresh** when any observation is judged fresh.
- Else **stale** when any is judged stale.
- Else **unobservable** when any is.
- Else **not asked**, or not this profile's, as the observations are.

A member one observation saw deleted, and another saw with a value, is
judged on the value: it was confirmed then, and was deleted after, or put
again after.

**The order of the checks.** For each observation, a reader asks in this
order, and stops at the first that answers: `[F: judgements.json]`
1. the resource declares no horizon: not asked (§2.3);
2. it is an event or an operation: not this profile's (§2.2);
3. the value is an archive's: not asked (§2.8);
4. its `freshness.ttl_s` is not a horizon: unobservable (§2.1);
5. the observation itself: §2.5 for a subscription, §2.6 for a GET.

Then it combines the observations as above.

### 2.8 Archives and last-known

- **Never current.** An archive's answer is last-known (core S6). Its
  freshness is **not asked**: a reader MUST NOT report an archived value
  fresh or stale, by its stamp or otherwise. `[F: judgements.json]`
- An archive never puts on its keys (core §4.4), so nothing confirms an
  archived value. Its `confirmed` member says whether alignment confirmed
  the key, which is not freshness.
- A consumer that turned to an archive because the owner was silent (S6)
  presents last-known, its stamp for display, and never "fresh".

### 2.9 Constrained faces

- **Liveness from freshness** (core R7, 0.21). Across a constrained face
  (core §8.5), presence does not cross, and a consumer judges a
  provider's liveness from the freshness of what crosses: the provider is
  live while a member of a resource it exposes across the face is fresh,
  by a subscriber's receive clock (§2.5). `[Sc: core constrained.md §3]`
- **Where nothing with a horizon crosses** (a resource with no
  `freshness.ttl_s`, or a face that `link.v1` closes to it), liveness is
  unobservable, as core R7 says. `[Sc: core constrained.md §3]`
- **Stale is never "down".** A member gone stale across a face says
  that the value is stale: the link may be out, and the provider up
  (§2.11).
- **A GET across the face** needs a trusted clock, as §2.6 says. Presence
  does not cross, so a measurement needs a delivery that does; a reader
  there usually relies on the deployment's word.
- **The re-puts cross** where `link.v1` lets the resource cross: a face's
  budget counts one put per member every ttl/2 (§2.4).

### 2.10 Clock ahead

An owner whose clock guard finds it ahead of its router beyond the delta
stops writing state (core §4.3): it MUST NOT re-put either, while the
guard holds its writes. `[Sc: §2]`
- Its members then go stale, by design: an owner that cannot stamp
  within its router's delta confirms nothing, and stale is what is true.
- When the guard releases its writes, the owner re-puts every member whose
  interval has run out. The reference retries a held re-put every second,
  or every ttl/2 when that is shorter.

### 2.11 Stale is a finding, never "down"

- **About a value.** Stale says that the owner has not confirmed a value
  within its horizon, as this reader observed. A reader MUST NOT report a
  stale value as a failed, absent or down provider: an absent provider is
  presence's word (core §8.1, R7). `[Sc: §2]`
- **Presence says nothing about freshness.** A provider holding its
  tokens can serve stale values (§2.4, a writer closed; §2.10), and a
  provider whose tokens are gone leaves values whose age a subscriber
  still measures. A tool reports both, and folds neither into the other.
  `[Sc: §2]`
- **Unobservable is never fresh** (core O5). `[F: judgements.json]`

## 3. Contract

None. `freshness.v1` defines no interface, resource, operation or key, and
puts nothing of its own on the bus: a re-put is the owner's own put of its
own member.

## 5. What a tool may conclude

"Not asked" (nobody put the question) and "unobservable" (it was put, and
the observation could not be had) are different facts, and neither is a
"no" (core O5). "Not this profile's" marks a question whose premise does
not hold. The fixture [`judgements.json`](conformance/judgements.json)
states the verdict tokens: `fresh`, `stale`, `unobservable`, `not_asked`,
with a reason class. A premise that does not hold is reported `not_asked`,
with the reason `kind` or `deleted`.

| Question | Yes | No | Unestablished | Finding on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is this member's value fresh? | A delivery at most ttl old (§2.5); a reply aged at most ttl − δ against a trusted clock (§2.6); any value read, at ttl 0 (§2.3). Several observations: §2.7. | A delivery older than ttl, or none while listening longer than ttl to a member known to exist (§2.5); a reply aged above ttl + δ against a trusted clock (§2.6). | **Not asked:** the resource declares no horizon (§2.3); the value is an archive's (§2.8). **Unobservable:** the horizon is not one (§2.1); the GET drew no reply for the member; the reply has no stamp; the clock is not trusted to the delta; the age is within δ of the horizon; the stamp is ahead beyond δ; the subscriber listened ttl or less and heard nothing; it lost deliveries (§2.5, §2.6). **Not this profile's:** an event or an operation (§2.2); a deleted member (§2.5, §2.6). | No: stale is the finding. |
| Is this resource's freshness kept? (one verdict per resource, as `check conform` reports it) | Every member read with a value is fresh, and there is at least one. A deleted member is set aside. | At least one member is stale: the finding, naming the stale members. | **Not asked:** no horizon. **Not this profile's:** an event or an operation. **Unobservable:** the horizon is not one; no member was read, or every member read is deleted; no member is stale and at least one is unobservable. | No. |
| Is this provider live across a constrained face? (core R7) | A member of a resource it exposes across the face, with a horizon, is fresh by a subscriber (§2.9). | Never from freshness: an absent provider is presence's word, and presence does not cross. | **Unobservable:** nothing with a horizon crossed fresh: stale, or nothing crossed (core R7). | Neither: informative. |

**Which cause** a stale finding has (the owner stopped, its writer
closed, its clock ahead, the link down) is unobservable from the value
alone. Presence tells an absent owner apart, where it crosses (§2.11).

## Appendix A. Against v1

v1 stated freshness in RFC 04 §1.2, "TTL & tombstones — the staleness
contract", with the registry's `ttl_s` (RFC 08 §2), its `detect_s` (RFC 04
§3.1) and the `refreshed` QoS profile (RFC 04 §3), on the `v1` branch:

| Rule | Binds | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| refresh | publisher | re-emit live state at ≤ `ttl_s`/2, the self-heal |
| aging | consumer | state older than `ttl_s` is stale; the producer's `alive` token absent ⇒ suspect at once, stale at TTL |
| retirement | publisher | retire a key with a Zenoh delete |
| tombstone visibility | deployment | a delete stays observable ≥ `ttl_s`; storage `gc.lifespan` ≥ the longest `ttl_s` |

**What changed.**

| v1 | `freshness.v1` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `ttl_s` required on every state subject (RFC 08 §2, v1.25) | Optional; absent is not asked (§2.3) | A horizon is declared where staleness means something. ZenSight's 190 subjects mixed a refresh cadence (900) with real expiry (86,400, 31,536,000) and "never" (G18). |
| `ttl_s = 0` written for "never" by convention (the config read-back, ZenSight's acks and silences), with no rule | `0` is never stale, stated (§2.3) | D16, G18 |
| State only | State and streams (§2.2) | R7's liveness across a face reads what crosses, which can be streams alone (zenoh-modem's gap 14). |
| Refresh by the publisher, "enforced by registry review" | A re-put is a mutation with a fresh owner's stamp (core S1, S2), required of the owner (§2.4), and made by the reference runtime's state writers | A GET reader ages from the stamp, so a re-put that kept the old stamp would read stale. |
| Aging: "older than `ttl_s`", on no stated clock | A subscriber's monotonic receive clock; a GET reader's stamp only against a clock trusted to the delta, with the band (§2.5, §2.6) | Architecture r4, `timing.v1` (spike S11): a check against the sender's stamp at ±250 ms of skew rejected every sample of a healthy commander. |
| The `alive` token absent ⇒ suspect, then stale at TTL | Presence and freshness are separate questions, each answered apart (§2.11) | Core R7 and O5: an absent provider is presence's word, and "suspect" was a fourth state no tool could act on. |
| Tombstone visibility ≥ `ttl_s`; storage `gc.lifespan` ≥ the longest `ttl_s` | The core's tombstone window (S3): 60 s unless configured, independent of the horizon | The owner answers its own deletes (S2, S3), and a storage on state keys is refused (S4). |

**What deliberately did not change.**
- **Half the horizon.** An owner confirms at least every ttl/2.
- **The unchanged value.** A re-put carries the value as it is.
- **Seconds** as the unit, and the name `ttl_s`.
- **`0`** as "never", as v1's data already used it.

**What is not ported.**
- **`detect_s`** (RFC 04 §3.1), the latency to detect a missed transition.
  Miss detection is core §2.5's advanced publication (`history`).
- **The `refreshed` QoS profile.** A contract states its QoS (core §2.4),
  and a `best_effort` state relies on its re-puts.
- **"Suspect".** A reader states fresh, stale, unobservable or not asked.
- **Aging out a population** (RFC 04 §1.2, cardinality budget: "entries
  past TTL are tombstoned by their publisher"). An owner deletes when it
  decides (core S3). A population budget is a profile's (#613).
- **Seeding by re-publishing.** v1 forbade it ("seeds come from a seed
  source, never from fake samples"), and zk2 has no seed: a late joiner
  GETs the owner (core S4). A re-put is a confirmation, not a seed.

**The record.** D16 (`docs/zk2/architecture.md`, its line 164) and the
profiles table of §3.12 (around its line 900) hold as written: `ttl_s` is
the staleness horizon, and `0` means never stale. This text adds what D16
left open, chiefly the absent horizon (§2.3), the clocks (§2.5, §2.6) and
the kinds (§2.2).
