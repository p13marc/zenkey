# `timing.v1`: a stream's cadence, its deadline and its lifespan

| | |
|---|---|
| **Text version** | 0.1 ([`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md)) |
| **Status** | draft |
| **Written against** | core 0.53 ([`../../core.md`](../../core.md)) |
| **Uses** | no other profile |
| **Evidence** | [`conformance/`](conformance/), [`scenarios.md`](scenarios.md) |

`timing.v1` says how often an owner publishes a stream, how long a reader
waits for its next sample before it reports the member **missed**, and how
old a sample may arrive before the reader discards it as **expired**. It
is what a control loop reads: a commander publishes at its period, an
actuator reports a deadline missed when the commander falls silent, and
discards a command that arrives too old, where the clocks allow it to tell.

```toml
# The walkthrough's commander (examples/zk2/walkthrough/twist_cmd.v1.toml)
[interface]
name = "twist_cmd"
major = 1
uses  = ["timing.v1"]

[resources.cmd]
kind        = "stream"
type        = "twist_cmd.v1.Twist"
annotations = { "timing.period_ms" = 20, "timing.deadline_ms" = 100, "timing.lifespan_ms" = 100 }
# published every 20 ms; missed after 100 ms of silence, seen within 102 ms;
# expired when it arrives more than 100 ms old, only on a stated clock bound
```

It defines three annotations, `timing.period_ms`, `timing.deadline_ms`
and `timing.lifespan_ms`, and no contract, no schema and no kind. It puts
nothing of its own on the bus. An instance whose contract uses it lists it
in its descriptor's `profiles`, as core §3.3 lists every profile a
contract uses.

---

## 0. Conventions

The keywords, the roles and the evidence tags are core §0's. Here,
`[F: <file>]` names a fixture in [`conformance/`](conformance/),
`[Sc: §n]` a section of [`scenarios.md`](scenarios.md), and `core` marks
the core's own evidence. References to `docs/zk2/architecture.md` (its
decision E14 and §3.12's row), to the spike report (S11) and to v1's RFCs
are informative: every rule is stated here.

Terms:
- **Timed resource:** a `stream` resource, `@stream` included, whose
  resolved annotations (core §9.3) hold a key of this profile's
  vocabulary, each value valid (§2.2).
- **Member:** one concrete key of a resource (core §2.2). Its key names
  its provider's address, `<system>/<service>` (core §1.1).
- **Period**, **deadline** and **lifespan:** the values of
  `timing.period_ms`, `timing.deadline_ms` and `timing.lifespan_ms`, in
  milliseconds (§2.2).
- **Reader:** a consumer or a tool that judges a deadline or a lifespan
  from the samples its subscription receives.
- **Monotonic clock:** a clock of the reader's host that never steps
  backwards and that no correction of the wall clock moves (POSIX
  `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, for instance). **Wall clock:** the host's UTC clock.
- **Arrival:** the instant, on the reader's monotonic clock, at which its
  session hands it a sample of a member that counts as one (§2.5).
- **Stamp:** the Zenoh timestamp a sample carries: a time, an NTP64 value,
  and the id of the clock that issued it, which is a session's zid (core
  §4.1, Appendix B).
- **Its owner's token:** the interface's token held by an instance of
  the member's provider address (core §8.1), at any revision, or, for an
  interface its descriptor marks `"token": false`, that instance's
  instance token, its descriptor listing the interface (core §3.3, §8.1).
  As the reader observes it, its owner is:
  - **present** while the reader holds that token as seen: shown by a
    liveliness read or heard declared, and no withdrawal of it heard
    since;
  - **absent** once a complete read shows no such token (core §8.1, 0.37);
  - **unconfirmed** between: a withdrawal heard, and no read since. A
    withdrawal heard live is never absence by itself (core §8.1).
- **Known member:** a member whose key the reader can write without
  hearing it: its resource's template has no parameter, or the reader's
  binding binds every parameter of it (core R2). Any other member the
  reader learns of only by hearing it.
- **ε (epsilon):** the deployment's stated bound, in milliseconds, on how
  far an owner's stamps and a reader's wall clock disagree (§2.6).

## 2. Rules

### 2.1 Who implements it

- **An owner** of a timed resource publishes each member at its period
  (§2.4), and stamps a resource that declares a lifespan with its own
  clock (§2.6).
- **A runtime** never publishes a stream sample on its owner's behalf
  (§2.4).
- **A consumer** of a timed resource judges its deadline and its lifespan
  as §2.5 and §2.6 say: a runtime that offers its application deadline
  events, or discards samples by their lifespan, does so by these rules.
- **A tool** answers §5's questions over a window (§2.7).
- **A deployment** states ε, where it wants a lifespan enforced (§2.6).

### 2.2 The values

A reader MUST read the three keys exactly as the fixture does.
`[F: declarations.json]`
- **Each is a whole number of milliseconds, from 1 to 2^53−1.** An
  integral float is that integer: `100.0` is `100`. The canonical form
  writes both as the integer (core §9.5, JCS), so a reader of a bundle
  cannot tell them apart, and neither may a reader of the source.
- **Not a value:** `0`, `-0.0`, a negative number, a fraction (`20.5`), a
  number above 2^53−1, a string (`"20"`), a boolean, an array or a table.
  There is no "never" to write as 0: a resource with no deadline omits
  `timing.deadline_ms`.
- **A declaration is invalid** when a key's value is not one. Each key is
  checked alone, in this order, and each that fails is a reason:
  `period_value`, `deadline_value`, `lifespan_value`.
  `[F: declarations.json]`
  - A reader MUST NOT judge a period, a deadline or a lifespan of a
    resource whose declaration is invalid, and reports each question of
    §5 unobservable, naming the reasons: one wrong value says the
    contract was not written as this profile reads it.
    `[F: declarations.json, conclusions.json]`
  - A contract linter SHOULD warn of one. The core has no code for it:
    W105 is about keys, and no core lint reads an annotation's value.
- **Another key** `timing.<key>` is W105's (§4), and makes nothing
  invalid. `[F: declarations.json]`

### 2.3 Where they mean something

- **On a `stream` resource,** plain or explicit, written on the resource,
  in `[defaults]` or in `[defaults.stream]` (core §9.3): the resource's
  merged annotations, the resource's own key over `[defaults.stream]`'s
  over `[defaults]`'s. `[F: declarations.json]`
- **On `state`, `event` and `operation` resources, nothing.** A reader
  ignores the keys there, whatever their values, and asks none of §5's
  questions of the resource. A contract linter SHOULD warn of one,
  `[defaults]` included, since `[defaults.stream]` says the same of
  streams alone. `[F: declarations.json]`
  - A state is its latest value: how long one stays current is
    `freshness.v1`'s horizon, which a state's owner confirms by putting
    it again, never this profile's period.
  - An event's silence is its normal state, and how often one may occur
    is core `rate` (core §2.6).
  - An operation has no sample.
- **On a requirement** (`[requires.<role>]`), nothing at this version: a
  reader ignores the keys there, and a contract linter SHOULD warn of
  one, which tightens nothing. `[F: declarations.json]`
  - A requirement's entry in the descriptor carries no annotations (core
    §3.3), so a tool reading a running consumer could never see a
    deadline written there (core R3).
  - A deadline a consumer tightens belongs to its binding, the record's
    choice (spike S11, rule 2), and a binding has no field for it at this
    version (§2.9).

### 2.4 The period

`period_ms` is the owner's nominal interval between two samples of one
member.
- **The owner SHOULD publish each member it publishes at least once every
  period** while the resource is exposed. It MAY publish more often: the
  period is not a rate limit. A member it has not started, or whose
  entity is gone, it does not publish (core §8.1's member, which exists
  from the owner's first declaration of it).
- **A runtime MUST NOT publish a stream sample on its owner's behalf,** a
  repeat of the last one included: a stream's samples each stand alone
  (core §2.1), so a repeat would report one measurement twice, and hide
  the silence a deadline is there to see. `[Sc: §1]` The cadence is the
  application's, as `freshness.v1` §2.4 says of a stream's.
- **A deadline below the period** is no deadline (§2.5).
- **Whether an owner kept its period** is a tool's question, over a
  window (§2.7), answered on the reader's monotonic clock from what
  arrived.

### 2.5 The deadline

`deadline_ms` is the longest silence a reader accepts between two
arrivals of one member. A reader that judges it MUST judge it exactly as
the fixture does. `[F: deadlines.json]` `[Sc: §1–§3]`

- **Per member of one provider.** A member's key names its provider's
  address, so a deadline is judged per provider address and key. A reader
  bound to several providers (core R1) judges each provider's members
  apart.
- **An arrival** is a put sample of the member that the reader's
  subscription receives, its key concrete, and not expired (§2.6). A
  sample on a wildcard key is discarded (core R6), and a delete sample is
  none: a stream's samples are values (core §2.1). A sample replayed from
  an advanced publisher's history at the subscription's start (core §2.5)
  is an arrival like any other.
- **On the reader's monotonic clock, from arrival to arrival.** A reader
  MUST NOT judge a deadline against a stamp, its wall clock, or a time a
  payload carries: a deadline judges what arrived when, and two clocks'
  disagreement plays no part. `[Sc: §4]` Spike S11 measured it: with a
  commander's clock 250 ms off either way, the receive-clock deadline
  followed it correctly.
- **Missed.** A member is **missed** at an instant when more than the
  deadline has passed since its last arrival. The miss is **due** at the
  last arrival plus the deadline, and holds at every instant after it: an
  arrival exactly the deadline after the one before is in time.
  `[F: deadlines.json]`
- **Edge-triggered.** A reader reports **`missed`** once, when a member
  becomes missed, and **`met`** once, at its next arrival. In between it
  reports nothing more of the member, however long the silence; `met`
  follows only a `missed`, and a member's first arrival reports nothing.
  `[F: deadlines.json]` `[Sc: §1]`
- **The detection bound.** A reader MUST report a miss at most
  max(deadline/50, 1 ms) after it is due: within deadline +
  max(deadline/50, 1 ms) of the last arrival. `[F: deadlines.json]`
  `[Sc: §1, §3]`
  - It judges every member it holds at each arrival, and at instants it
    sets itself. A one-shot timer set at the earliest instant a miss is
    due among them meets the bound, and so does a periodic tick at most
    the bound apart.
  - An arrival after a silence longer than the deadline that no
    evaluation saw reports the miss, then `met`, both at that arrival.
    `[F: deadlines.json]`
  - **E14, relaxed below 50 ms.** The record's rule, "a timer of at most
    deadline/50", is this bound for a deadline of 50 ms or more. Below
    50 ms, deadline/50 is under a millisecond, which a host's timer does
    not keep, so the bound is 1 ms there. `twist_cmd.v1`'s 100 ms deadline
    is seen within 102 ms; spike S11 saw 100–102 ms.
- **A member never heard** is judged only when the reader knows it
  another way: a known member (§0) whose owner is present. It is missed
  when more than the deadline has passed since the later of the
  subscription's start and the instant the reader first saw its owner
  present, or present again after it was absent. `[F: deadlines.json]`
  `[Sc: §3]`
  - The subscription's start is the instant the reader declared it, on
    its monotonic clock. "Never heard" is: no arrival of it since then.
  - If its owner is absent before the miss is due, the reader waits for
    nothing until the owner is present again, and counts from then. While
    its owner is unconfirmed, the wait goes on as it was. A miss already
    reported stands until an arrival.
  - Any other member is judged from its first arrival: before it, a
    reader reports nothing of it. A subscription alone cannot tell a
    member that was never put from one gone silent, as `freshness.v1`
    §2.3 says.
  - A consumer that must act on a member before its first arrival, a
    control loop's safe output at its start, does so on its own; a
    future `arbitration.v1` names that output (§2.9).
- **What a reader holds, and the bound.** Per provider and resource, a
  reader holds every known member, and every member it heard that has not
  ended (below). It holds at most the resource's bound, core §2.7's: the
  instance's stated `cardinality`, else the contract's, which is never
  lower; one for a template without parameters; no bound for 4294967295.
  `[F: deadlines.json]`
  - A member first heard while the bound's members are held is not held,
    and not judged: it is past the bound, which core §2.7 says an owner
    MUST NOT exceed. A reader MAY report it so.
  - Known members are held whatever the bound, and count toward it.
- **Ended.** A reader stops judging a member, and reports **`ended`** if
  it had heard it or reported it missed, when: `[F: deadlines.json]`
  - **its member tokens are gone:** for a template that declares `epoch`
    (core §2.3, §8.1), a complete read shows no member token of it (core
    §8.1, 0.37). A withdrawal heard live is a reason to read again, never
    the end by itself: the reader's own router restarting withdraws every
    token at once. `[Sc: §2]`
  - **it is forgotten:** it is not known, it is missed, and its last
    arrival was an hour or more before. Core §2.7 makes a stream member
    live for an hour after its owner last published on it, so a reader
    that forgets it then holds at most the bound. A known member is never
    forgotten.

  A member heard after it ended is judged from that arrival, as a member
  first heard.
- **A deadline below the period is no deadline.** When both are declared
  and the deadline is below the period, an owner keeping its period would
  be missed between every two samples. A reader judges no deadline for
  the resource, and a tool reports the deadline's question unobservable
  (§5). A contract linter SHOULD warn of it. The owner is not refused,
  since its period is a SHOULD. A deadline equal to the period is one.
  `[F: declarations.json, conclusions.json]`
- **A miss is never "down".** A missed member says that nothing arrived
  within the deadline, as this reader observed: the owner may be paused,
  the link out, or the samples lost. A reader MUST NOT report a missed
  member as a failed, absent or down provider: an absent provider is
  presence's word (core §8.1, R7), as `freshness.v1` §2.11 says of a
  stale value. A tool reports both, and folds neither into the other.
  `[Sc: §1]`
- **`arbitration.v1`,** not written yet, will take an arbitrated role's
  deadline from this section: a provider is fresh while its member is not
  missed.

### 2.6 The lifespan

`lifespan_ms` is how old a sample may be when it arrives. Its **age** is
the reader's wall clock at the arrival minus the stamp's time, negative
when the stamp is ahead of the reader's clock. A stamp's time counts from
the Unix epoch, as an HLC's clock reads its host's wall clock (core
Appendix B, 0.53).

- **The owner stamps.** An owner of a resource that declares a lifespan
  MUST stamp each of its samples from its own session's clock, and MUST
  state that session's zid as `meta.zid` in its descriptor, which core
  §3.3 makes a SHOULD. `[Sc: §4, §5]`
  - A session whose HLC is enabled stamps every put it is not given a
    stamp for (core §4.1), so enabling it meets the first half. Core §4.3
    asks the HLC only of a session that serves state: a stream-only
    owner's session may have none, and its samples then carry a router's
    stamp or none at all (core §4.1).
- **ε is the deployment's word.** A reader takes ε from its
  configuration, a tool from an option its operator sets, as
  `freshness.v1` §2.6 takes the deployment's word. A word the clocks do
  not keep makes the reader's verdicts wrong, and the error is the
  deployment's. `[Sc: §4]`
  - **Never the HLC's delta.** A reader MUST NOT take the HLC's maximum
    delta (500 ms unless configured, core §4.1), or any number of its own,
    for ε when the deployment states none. `[F: lifespans.json]`
    `[Sc: §4]` A router re-stamps or drops a put stamped more than its
    delta ahead of its own clock, and lets every stamp behind it pass
    (core §4.1): the delta bounds no clock that is behind. Spike S11's
    reader 250 ms ahead of its commander rejected every sample of it
    against a 100 ms lifespan, and 500 ms is five times that lifespan.
  - **What ε bounds** (core §4.1, 0.53). An owner stamps from its HLC,
    and a session moves its HLC forward to a later stamp it receives,
    within its delta. ε bounds the owner's stamps against the reader's
    wall clock, so a deployment states it of every clock whose stamps can
    reach its owners, not of their hosts' clocks alone.
  - **The reader's clock is its wall clock,** never its session's HLC,
    which the same rule moves toward the stamps it judges.
- **The judgement.** A reader judges each sample at its arrival, by the
  first of these that holds: `[F: lifespans.json]`
  1. the resource is not a stream: not this profile's (§2.3);
  2. its declaration is invalid: unobservable (§2.2);
  3. it declares no lifespan: not asked;
  4. the deployment states no ε: unobservable (`no_bound`);
  5. the sample carries no stamp: unobservable (`unstamped`);
  6. the reader holds no `meta.zid` of an instance of the provider's
     address: unobservable (`unattributed`), since a stamp is then
     unattributable (core §3.3);
  7. the stamp's id is not the `meta.zid` of an instance of the provider's
     address, compared by value (core §3.3): unobservable
     (`foreign_stamp`): a router's stamp, or another session's, which
     dates the sample's passage, not its owner's clock;
  8. the stamp's clock disagrees: this stamp, or an earlier one of the
     same id in this reading that reached this step, was ahead of the
     reader's wall clock by more than ε (its age below −ε): unobservable
     (`clock_disagrees`);
  9. its age less ε is above the lifespan: **expired**;
  10. otherwise: **live**.
- **A stamp ahead by more than ε falsifies the word for its clock.**
  Transit only adds to an age, so no delay puts a stamp ahead of the
  reader's clock: only the clocks can. For the rest of the reading,
  every sample that clock stamps is unobservable, since the reader no
  longer knows how far behind it may be either (`freshness.v1` §2.6
  reads a measured clock so). A reading is the subscription's lifetime,
  or a tool's window. Each clock is judged apart: a new instance's
  session is a new zid, and a new clock. `[F: lifespans.json]`
  `[Sc: §4]`
- **The bounds are inclusive.** An age of exactly the lifespan plus ε is
  live, and so is an age of exactly −ε.
- **An expired sample is discarded.** A consumer MUST discard an expired
  sample: it does not hand it to its application as a value, and it is not
  an arrival for the deadline (§2.5). It MUST NOT discard a sample whose
  lifespan is live, unobservable or not asked. `[F: deadlines.json]`
  `[Sc: §4]` A tool reports each judgement.
- **With no bound stated, nothing is discarded,** and every sample's
  lifespan is unobservable: a receive clock cannot see that a sample was
  already old when it arrived (E14), and a control loop relies on its
  deadline. `[F: lifespans.json]` `[Sc: §4]`
- **At its arrival only.** A sample held after its arrival is not judged
  again: how long a value stays usable without a new one is the
  deadline's (§2.5).
- **Never from a payload field.** A reader MUST NOT age a sample by a time
  its payload or its attachment carries, such as `twist_cmd.v1`'s
  `issued_at_ns`: nothing in the core attributes a payload's time to a
  clock, and a payload is whatever its writer sent. `[Sc: §5]`

### 2.7 A tool's window

A tool answers §5's questions of each member it holds over a **window**:
an interval of its monotonic clock over which it subscribes to the
resource and follows the provider's presence (core §8.1). The window's
start is the subscription's start. `[F: periods.json, conclusions.json]`

- **The owner present throughout:** present (§0) at every instant from
  the window's start to its end: unconfirmed or absent at none.
- **Lost:** the tool knows it lost a delivery of the member in the window
  (its own queue dropped one, or miss detection reported one, core §2.5),
  or its own receive path stalled within it (core §2.7, 0.27). Such a
  window can still show a member in time, since what arrived arrived, but
  never a miss or a period not kept as the owner's: the delivery it lost
  may have filled the gap.
- **Across a constrained face:** the tool reads through a face (core
  §8.5, §2.8).
- **The deadline over the window** is §2.5's judgement from the window's
  start, evaluated at its end. For each member it holds, the tool has:
  - how long it held the member in the window: from the later of the
    window's start and the instant it first held it (its first arrival,
    or, for a known member, the instant it began to wait for it) to the
    window's end, or to the member's end;
  - each miss it reported, and whether the owner was present (§0) when it
    reported it: unconfirmed is not present, since the withdrawal it
    heard may be the owner's, or its own router's restart, and neither
    makes the miss the owner's;
  - whether the member ended in the window.
- **The period over the window,** on the tool's monotonic clock:
  `[F: periods.json]`
  - **the span** runs from the window's start for a known member, else
    from the member's first arrival in the window, to the window's end, or
    to the member's end when it ended in the window; **its arrivals** are
    those within the span, both ends included;
  - a member not known with no arrival in the window is **not heard**:
    there is no member to ask about;
  - a span shorter than three periods is **too short** to tell;
  - otherwise the period is **kept** when (arrivals + 2) × period is at
    least the span, and **not kept** otherwise.
  - **Why two.** An owner at exactly its period puts the span's length
    divided by the period into the span, give or take one for where its
    phase falls, and transit's jitter can move a sample across each end:
    two samples of slack allow for both. The slack stays two however long
    the window, so a longer window tells a slower owner apart more
    finely: over 10 s at `twist_cmd.v1`'s 20 ms, an owner 0.2 % slow
    passes and one 1 % slow does not.
  - The period is a rate, and the deadline a gap: a burst after a pause
    can keep the period and miss the deadline.
- **The lifespan over the window** is each sample's §2.6 judgement.

### 2.8 Constrained faces

Across a constrained face (core §8.5), presence does not cross, and a
stream may cross downsampled (`link.v1` §2.8).
- **No member is waited for.** No owner is seen present across the face,
  so a known member is judged from its first arrival, as any other
  (§2.5), and no member token is seen gone, so a member ends only by being
  forgotten.
- **The face sets the rate.** A downsampled stream crosses at most once
  per the face's interval, so the far side's arrivals are the face's, not
  the owner's period, and a deadline below the interval is missed between
  every two samples, as a deadline below the period would be. A reader
  across a face judges its deadline as anywhere: what it reports is what
  crossed.
- **A tool across a face** answers the deadline's and the period's
  questions unobservable (§5): it cannot see its owner present, and the
  face's rate is not the owner's.
- **The lifespan** is judged as anywhere: a sample crosses with its
  owner's stamp, and the face's delay adds to its age.

### 2.9 Not at 1.0

- **A tightened deadline.** A consumer's own deadline, shorter than the
  contract's, belongs to its binding (spike S11, rule 2), which has no
  field for it; a requirement's annotations carry none either (§2.3).
- **A sequence check,** the record's remedy "where staleness in transit
  matters" (architecture §3.12): `twist_cmd.v1`'s `Twist` carries no
  sequence number, and no rule of the core numbers a stream's samples
  but advanced publication's miss detection (core §2.5).
- **Simulated time** (`clock.v1`): every clock here is a host's.
- **The dead-man output and choosing between providers,** which are
  `arbitration.v1`'s (core §3.2).
- **An owner's report of its own cadence,** and an owner refused for a
  period it cannot keep: the period is a SHOULD.

## 3. Contract

None. `timing.v1` defines no interface, resource, operation, schema or
key, and puts nothing of its own on the bus: a deadline and a lifespan
are judged from samples the owner publishes, and a deadline event is the
reader's own, handed to its application.

## 5. What a tool may conclude

"Not asked" (nobody put the question), "unobservable" (it was put, and
the window could not answer it) and "not this profile's" (the resource is
not a stream) are different facts, and none is a "no" (core O5). The
fixture [`conclusions.json`](conformance/conclusions.json) states the
tokens: `yes`, `no`, `unobservable`, `not_asked`, `not_this_profiles`,
each with a reason, and the order in which each question's premises are
read: each question reads the kind first, then the declaration, then
its key, then the window. Each is asked of one member over one window
(§2.7).

| Question | Yes | No | Unestablished | Finding on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Did this member meet its deadline over the window? | No miss reported in the window, and the member held for longer than the deadline in it (`met`). | A miss reported while its owner was present, and nothing lost (`missed`). | **Not this profile's:** not a stream (`kind`). **Not asked:** no `deadline_ms` (`no_deadline`); the member ended in the window without a miss (`ended`); never held in the window (`not_heard`). **Unobservable:** the declaration is invalid (`invalid`); the deadline is below the period (`below_period`); across a face (`across_face`); a miss while its owner was present, and a delivery lost (`lost`); a miss only while its owner was not present (`owner_not_present`); held for the deadline or less (`window_too_short`). | No. |
| Did the owner keep this member's period over the window? | Kept (`kept`, §2.7). | Not kept, the owner present throughout and nothing lost (`not_kept`). | **Not this profile's:** not a stream (`kind`). **Not asked:** no `period_ms` (`no_period`); the member not heard (`not_heard`). **Unobservable:** the declaration is invalid (`invalid`); across a face (`across_face`); a span too short (`window_too_short`); not kept, and a delivery lost (`lost`); not kept, and the owner not present throughout (`owner_not_present`). | No. |
| Did a sample of this member arrive expired? | At least one sample expired (`expired`). | Every sample judged live, at least one (`live`). | **Not this profile's:** not a stream (`kind`). **Not asked:** no `lifespan_ms` (`no_lifespan`). **Unobservable:** the declaration is invalid (`invalid`); a sample unobservable and none expired, the first such sample's reason (`no_bound`, `unstamped`, `unattributed`, `foreign_stamp`, `clock_disagrees`); no sample (`no_sample`). | Yes. |

- **Over a resource,** or a run: no when any member's answer is no; yes
  when every member's is yes, and there is at least one; unobservable
  otherwise. A member not asked is set aside.
- **Which cause** a miss has (the owner paused or gone, the link out, the
  samples lost on a `best_effort` path), and which an expired sample has
  (a slow path, or clocks beyond the word), is unobservable from the
  arrivals alone. Presence tells an absent owner apart, where it crosses
  (§2.5).

## Appendix A. Against v1

v1 had no timing profile. Its RFCs defined no period, deadline or
lifespan of a sample: RFC 04 §1.1 published telemetry "on a cadence" and
named none, RFC 04 §3.1's `detect_s` bounded how soon a consumer could
detect a missed state transition, and RFC 07 §1.3 (v1.26) named a media
frame's age, its arrival less the publisher's HLC stamp, an observation
"never a verdict", where a frame-age deadline was one of the consumer
behaviours "this plane is growing". RFC 05's deadline is a configuration
change's confirm window, which zk2 keeps in `config.v1`, and the
`lifespan` of RFC 06 and RFC 09 is a storage's garbage collection.

**What is new.**

| v1 | `timing.v1` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Telemetry "on a cadence" (RFC 04 §1.1), the cadence undeclared | `period_ms`, the owner's SHOULD per member, which no runtime fills by repeating a sample (§2.4) | A reader and a tool need the cadence the contract promises, and a repeat would hide the silence a deadline sees. |
| No deadline | `deadline_ms`, per member on the reader's monotonic clock, edge-triggered, seen within max(deadline/50, 1 ms) (§2.5) | Architecture E14 and spike S11: silence caught at 100–102 ms by a receive-clock deadline. |
| Frame age, an observation on the publisher's HLC stamp (RFC 07 §1.3) | `lifespan_ms`, enforced only on the deployment's stated ε, never on the HLC's delta, and only on the owner's own stamp (§2.6) | S11: a sender-stamp check rejected every sample of a healthy commander at ±250 ms of skew. |
| `detect_s` on state (RFC 04 §3.1) | Not ported here. A state's age is `freshness.v1`'s, and miss detection is core §2.5's advanced publication. | The deadline judges a stream's silence, not a state's transition. |

**What deliberately did not change.**
- **An age is an observation of two clocks.** RFC 07 §1.3 reported frame
  age as skewed latency, never a verdict; this text decides on it only
  where the deployment states how far the clocks may disagree, and reads
  an age it cannot trust as unobservable, never as expired or live.
- **Absence is not zero.** A miss is never "down" (§2.5), as v1 kept
  presence and staleness apart.

**The record.** E14 (`docs/zk2/architecture.md`, its line 239) and §3.12's
`timing.v1` row (around its line 887) hold as written, with two
precisions: the timer "of at most deadline/50" is a detection bound with a
1 ms floor (§2.5), and the spike's "either dropped or bounded by the HLC's
maximum delta" is bounded by the deployment's ε, never the delta (§2.6),
as E14 itself says ("uhlc's default 500 ms delta is not" such a bound).
Spike S11's `timing.v1` rules, "`period` is the expected rate",
"`deadline` runs on the receiver's clock" and "`lifespan` … is either
dropped or bounded", are §2.4 to §2.6. The two contracts that write the
keys, `twist_cmd.v1` and `nav.v2`, are published in
`examples/zk2/.history`, so the keys stay as written, `lifespan_ms`
included.
