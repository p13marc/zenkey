# `link.v2`: a constrained face, and the transport it runs

| | |
|---|---|
| **Text version** | 0.7 ([`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md)) |
| **Status** | draft |
| **Written against** | core 0.96 ([`../../core.md`](../../core.md)) |
| **Uses** | no other profile. It restates [`link.v1`](v1.md), text 0.16, by reference (§0). Its plan reads horizons and heartbeats as link.v1's does, [`timing.v1`](../timing/v1.md)'s deadline (§2.8), and [`telemetry.v2`](../telemetry/v2.md)'s run as a counter's epoch (§2.13). |
| **Evidence** | [`conformance/v2/`](conformance/v2/), [`scenarios-v2.md`](scenarios-v2.md) |

`link.v1` says what may cross a constrained face, and leaves the
transport the face runs to the deployment. At zenoh's defaults that
transport is wrong for a radio. A 10 s lease with 4 keep-alives sends
69,120 keep-alives a day over an idle link. At LoRa's SF12, a keep-alive
every 30 s takes more air than the duty budget allows.

`link.v2` puts the transport in the face policy. A link.v2 policy states:
- **the router's transport**, `[transport]`: its lease, keep-alives,
  `wait_before_close`, sequence width, batch and timeouts (§2.16);
- **the zenoh the router runs**, `binding`, which names its link
  protocols (§2.4);
- **the catalog** the far side decodes with, by its id (§2.1);
- **per face**, the medium's loss, whether it repairs a lost frame, and
  how long a caller across it waits for a reply (§2.1);
- **per face**, the frames that cross it, each at its cadence, and the
  shortest horizon it affords (§2.7, §2.12).

A contract that lists `link.v2` types what it exposes as packed (§2.17).
The plan denies a timed stream rather than thin it (§2.8). Given the
catalog, it refuses what the far side could not decode (§2.18).

A generator writes the transport into the router's configuration, and
for the far end on request (§2.18). A tool checks a router's
configuration against it, and the two ends against each other (§2.19).
The far side's callers wait the face's reply timeout, and its readers
declare a silent subscription again where the medium repairs no lost
frame (§2.20, §2.21).

A face may attach **devices**: owners on a first hop, each a client of
the near router across the face (core §8.1). Its plan lets each one's
instance token and descriptor cross toward the near side, and keeps the
rest of `@zk` denied (§2.5, §2.6). A tool checks a router's block for
that carve (§2.15).

```toml
# The RF node's router (Appendix B)
profile  = "link.v2"
system   = "lab"
services = ["radio-a"]
binding  = "zenoh-1.10.1"           # the zenoh this router runs

[transport]                         # the router's, for every link it holds
lease_ms             = 240000
keep_alive           = 4
wait_before_close_ms = 240000       # at least the lease
sequence             = "16bit"
batch_bytes          = 222          # the radio's SDU and 2 B of framing

[face.rf]
far              = "ground"
attach           = "client"
select           = { link_protocols = ["unixsock-stream"] }
class            = "constrained"
expose           = "link"
interval_s       = 60
max_outage_s     = 3600
link_bps         = 2400
loss_ppm         = 50000            # 5 %
arq              = false            # the radio repairs no lost frame
reply_timeout_ms = 15000            # what a caller across the face waits
```

## 0. Conventions

The keywords, the roles and the evidence tags are core §0's, and the
terms are `link.v1` §0's. Here, `[F: <file>]` names a fixture in
[`conformance/v2/`](conformance/v2/), and `[Sc: §n]` a section of
[`scenarios-v2.md`](scenarios-v2.md). A `link.v1` fixture or scenario
says so, as in `[F: link.v1 plan.json]`.

**By reference.** Every rule of `link.v1` holds for a link.v2 policy,
with these readings:
- §2.1 is this text's: a link.v2 policy is read as §2.1 below says;
- a face's `batch_bytes` and `lease_ms` are `[transport]`'s (§2.16);
- §2.1's closed list of link protocols is the binding's (§2.4);
- §2.10 reads `[transport]` (§2.10 below);
- §2.3, §2.5, §2.6, §2.7, §2.8, §2.12, §2.13 and §2.15 hold with what
  this text adds under their numbers.

A rule this text states under a number of `link.v1`'s replaces that
rule. Every other number is `link.v1`'s, and holds as written. This
version adds §2.16 to §2.21.

Terms:
- **Binding**: the zenoh a near router runs, as the policy names it
  (§2.4).
- **The ends** of a face: the near router, and the **far end**, the far
  router, the gateway client's session or each device's session that
  attaches over the face.
- **A device face**: a face whose `attach` is `"device"`. Its
  **devices** are the owners its `devices` lists (§2.5).
- **The keep-alive interval**: `lease_ms` / `keep_alive`. Each end sends
  a keep-alive when it has sent nothing for one interval.
- **A full batch**: `batch_bytes` bytes. Its **air** is `batch_bytes` ×
  8 / `link_bps` seconds.
- **Packed**: a resource whose type is a packed type (core §7.6). A frame
  resource's type always is.
- **A listed frame**: a frame resource (core §7.7) that a face's `frames`
  names (§2.7). A crossing frame resource's **cadence** on a face is its
  entry's `every_s` when it is listed, and its interval otherwise (core
  §7.7).
- **A timed stream**: a stream, plain or explicit, whose resolved
  annotations hold `timing.deadline_ms` (§2.8).

## 2. Rules

### 2.1 The face policy

A **link.v2 policy** is a face policy, a TOML 1.0 file, whose `profile`
is `"link.v2"`. A generator and a linter MUST read it exactly as the
fixtures do: the same file refused with the same findings, or accepted
with the same warnings and the same plan. `[F: v2/lints.json,
v2/plan.json]`

**One reader for both.** A policy with no `profile` is a `link.v1`
policy. A reader of this text MUST read it as `link.v1` §2.1 says,
finding for finding. `[F: v2/lints.json]` A `profile` of any other
value is `bad_value`, and the rest is read as a link.v2 policy.

**The top level.**

| Key | Value | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `profile` | `"link.v2"` | yes | This text. |
| `system`, `services` | as `link.v1` §2.1's | yes | The near router's system and its listed services. |
| `binding` | `"zenoh-1.10.1"` or `"zm-1.10.2-r1"` | yes | The zenoh the near router runs (§2.4). |
| `catalog` | a string of 8 lowercase hex digits | no | The id of the deployment catalog the far side decodes with (§2.19). |
| `transport` | a table (below) | yes | The near router's transport (§2.16). |
| `face` | as `link.v1` §2.1's | yes | The router's constrained faces. |

**A face**, `[face.<name>]`, takes `link.v1` §2.1's keys `far`,
`attach`, `region`, `select`, `class`, `expose`, `interval_s`,
`max_outage_s` and `link_bps`, with their values and meanings. `attach`
also takes `"device"` (§2.5). A face takes these keys too:

| Key | Value | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `select.link_protocols` | a non-empty array of the binding's link protocols, none repeated | yes | As `link.v1`'s, over the binding's list (§2.4). |
| `loss_ppm` | an integer from 0 to 1,000,000 | yes | The share of frames the medium loses, in parts per million. |
| `arq` | a boolean | yes | Whether the medium repairs a lost frame below zenoh. |
| `reply_timeout_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | yes | The deployment's word to every caller across the face: how long a reply may take to come back. |
| `frames` | a table of entries, each keyed `<iface>/<template>` | no; only with `expose = "link"` | The frame resources that cross the face, each at its cadence (§2.7). |
| `horizon_s` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | no; only with `expose = "link"` | The shortest horizon the face affords a crossing heartbeat or frame resource (§2.12). |
| `devices` | a non-empty array of addresses `<system>/<service>`, each two plain chunks (core §1.2), none repeated | with `attach = "device"`, and only then | The owners on a first hop that attach over the face (§2.5). |

**An entry of `frames`** is keyed by an interface id, `/`, and a
template with no parameter. Its value is a table:

| Key | Value | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `every_s` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | yes | How often the frame's owner puts one on this face, in seconds. |
| `keyframe_s` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | no | The longest the face accepts between two full records, in seconds. |

A face's `lease_ms` and `batch_bytes` are `[transport]`'s: on a face,
each is `moved_field`, an error. zenoh 1.10.1 reads them once per
router, for every link (§2.16).

**`[transport]`**, each key with zenoh 1.10.1's configuration key:

| Key | Value | Required | Zenoh's key | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lease_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | yes | `transport/link/tx/lease` | The lease each end announces. |
| `keep_alive` | an integer from 1 to 255 | yes | `transport/link/tx/keep_alive` | Keep-alives per lease. |
| `wait_before_close_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | yes | `transport/link/tx/queue/congestion_control/block/wait_before_close`, in µs | How long a `block` push waits for room before the transport closes. |
| `sequence` | `"8bit"`, `"16bit"`, `"32bit"` or `"64bit"` | yes | `transport/link/tx/sequence_number_resolution` | The frame sequence number's resolution. |
| `batch_bytes` | an integer from 1 to 65,535 | no; 65,535 | `transport/link/tx/batch_size` | The largest batch the router sends. |
| `open_timeout_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | no; 10,000 | `transport/unicast/open_timeout` | How long an end waits for a transport it opens. |
| `accept_timeout_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | no; 10,000 | `transport/unicast/accept_timeout` | How long an end waits for a transport it accepts. |
| `interests_timeout_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | no; 10,000 | `routing/interests/timeout` | How long a session waits for the interests a declaration answers. |

A key absent where it is optional takes the default the table gives,
which is zenoh 1.10.1's. A deployment that has not decided its lease,
keep-alives, `wait_before_close` and sequence width cannot write a
link.v2 policy.

**What a linter reports.** Findings are `link.v1` §2.1's: a `code`, a
`severity`, the `face` or none, and `at`. `at` is a key within the face
for a face's key, or `transport.<key>` for a key of `[transport]`.
Every code of `link.v1` §2.1's table holds. Two read differently:
`unknown_protocol` reads the binding's list (§2.4), and
`batch_over_lease` reads `[transport]` (§2.10). These are added:
`[F: v2/lints.json]`

| Code | Severity | When |
|---|---|---|
| `transport_missing` | error | A link.v2 policy with no `transport`. `at` is `transport`. |
| `moved_field` | error | A face's `lease_ms` or `batch_bytes`. |
| `wait_under_lease` | error | `wait_before_close_ms` is below `lease_ms` (§2.16). No face; `at` is `transport.wait_before_close_ms`. |
| `keep_alive_flaps` | warning | The face's loss makes more than one false close a day likely (§2.16). `at` is `transport.keep_alive`. |
| `sequence_window` | warning | One lease can carry as many full batches as the sequence window (§2.16). `at` is `transport.sequence`. |
| `open_under_handshake` | warning | `open_timeout_ms` or `accept_timeout_ms` is below twice the handshake's air at `link_bps` (§2.16). `at` is the key. |
| `reply_timeout_short` | warning | `reply_timeout_ms` is below the air of 16 full batches. `at` is `reply_timeout_ms`. |
| `no_arq` | warning | `arq` is false. `at` is `arq`. |
| `device_listed` | error | A device the policy lists elsewhere: among `services`, as `<system>/<service>`, or in another face's `devices`. Each face listing it has the finding. `at` is `devices`. |

- **Each key is checked alone**, as `link.v1` §2.1 says. A face's check
  that reads `[transport]` is made when the keys it reads are valid,
  whatever else the policy holds. An optional key of `[transport]` that
  is absent is read at its default.
- **`frames` and `horizon_s`** on a face whose `expose` is `"none"` are
  `bad_value`, as its `interval_s` is: nothing crosses there.
- **`devices` and `region`.** `devices` with any `attach` but
  `"device"` is `bad_value`, and so is `region` with `"device"`, as with
  `"client"`. Each is checked against `attach` only when `attach` is
  `client`, `south-region` or `device`.
- **`devices` is one value.** An empty array, an entry that is not an
  address of two plain chunks, a wildcard among them, and an entry
  repeated are each `bad_value` at `devices`, reported once.
- **A link.v1 policy** knows no device face: `attach = "device"` is
  `link.v1` §2.1's `bad_value`, and `devices` its `unknown_field`.
- **Each entry of `frames`** is checked alone. A key of the wrong form,
  or a value that is not a table, is `bad_value` at `frames.<key>`. Its
  own keys are at `frames.<key>.every_s` and `frames.<key>.keyframe_s`,
  and any other is `unknown_field` there. An empty `frames` lists no
  frame.
- **`no_arq`.** Zenoh 1.10.1 never repairs a declaration a medium lost: a
  subscriber, a queryable or a key declaration lost on the way stays
  lost while the transport stays up. A face whose medium repairs no
  frame is warned of, so that its deployment knows.
- **`reply_timeout_short`.** A reply queued behind other traffic can wait
  for a dozen batches to cross. Below 16 full batches' air, which is
  11.8 s at 2,400 bit/s with 222 B batches, a caller that waits
  `reply_timeout_ms` gives up on replies that are on their way. In
  integers: `reply_timeout_ms` × `link_bps` < 128,000 × `batch_bytes`.

### 2.3 The plan

`link.v1` §2.3 holds. A link.v2 policy's plan adds what follows.
`[F: v2/plan.json]`
- **A face's `frames`**: each listed frame that crosses it, as
  `{resource, every_s, keyframe_s}`, sorted by name. It is absent for a
  face with no `frames`, and empty for an empty `frames` (§2.7).
- **A device face's plan** reads its devices as §2.5 says. Its `attach`
  is `{shape: "device", devices}`, the devices sorted by their bytes, and
  its first three denies are §2.6's.
- **The deployment** names each device's interfaces, as it names a
  listed service's. A device it names none for is `service_unknown`, at
  the device's address.
- **A link.v1 policy's plan** is `link.v1`'s, finding for finding. It
  takes none of this section.

**Revisions read together** (`link.v1` §2.3). What this text weighs of
a resource is judged revision by revision: a warning that one revision
would draw, the plan draws.
- **Packed** only where every revision that declares it types it
  packed. So one revision's raw type draws `not_packed` (§2.17).
- **Timed** where any revision declares `timing.deadline_ms` (§2.8).
- **Its longest put** is weighed in each revision that declares it
  `block`. One whose longest put is above `batch_bytes` draws
  `block_over_batch` (§2.16).
- **Why.** A service running the older minor puts its raw bytes, or its
  timed samples, across the face, whatever a later minor declares. §2.18
  looks up each revision in the catalog alone, for the same reason.

**More refusals.** Each is an error, and no plan is derived. A refusal
lists the deployment's errors alone, as `link.v1` §2.3 says: no warning
of the policy's or the plan's comes with it.

| Code | Face | `at` | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| `packed_required` | none | the resource's name | A revision given lists `link.v2` and exposes a resource whose type is not packed (§2.17). |
| `frame_unknown` | the face | the name the entry names | An entry of `frames` names no frame resource of a listed interface (§2.7). |
| `keyframe_unreachable` | the face | the frame's name | A listed frame that crosses, whose `keyframe_s` its contract cannot reach (§2.7). |
| `catalog_missing` | none | `<iface>@<fingerprint>` | Given the catalog, a revision of an interface with something crossing is not an entry (§2.18). |
| `presence_required` | the device's face | the interface id | A device implements `archive.v1`, or an interface a revision given of which uses `redundancy.v1` (§2.5). |

**More warnings.** Each is about a face, `at` the resource's name unless
the row says otherwise.

| Code | When |
|---|---|
| `not_packed` | A crossing resource whose type is not packed (§2.17). |
| `timed_downsampled` | A timed stream, denied where `link.v1` downsamples (§2.8). |
| `frame_unlisted` | On a face with `frames`, an exposed frame resource it leaves out, which no face of the policy lets cross (§2.7). |
| `horizon_under_face` | A crossing frame resource or heartbeat whose horizon is below `horizon_s` (§2.12). A heartbeat's `at` is `freshness.v2/stream/heartbeat/<iface>`. |
| `block_over_batch` | A crossing `block` resource whose longest put is above `batch_bytes` (§2.16). |
| `keep_alive_not_suppressed` | The face's shortest crossing cadence is above the keep-alive interval (§2.16). `at` is `transport.keep_alive`. |
| `epoch_on_device` | On a device face, a template of a device's interface that declares `epoch` (§2.5). |

### 2.4 Link protocols per binding

`binding` names the zenoh the near router runs. With it, it names the
closed list of link protocols that `select.link_protocols` takes, and
that `link.v1` §2.4's narrowing reads, in this order:
`[F: v2/lints.json, v2/plan.json]`
- **`zenoh-1.10.1`**: `link.v1` §2.1's nine, `tcp`, `udp`, `tls`,
  `quic`, `serial`, `unixpipe`, `unixsock-stream`, `vsock`, `ws`;
- **`zm-1.10.2-r1`**, the zenoh fork zenoh-modem builds on: the same,
  with `unixsock-dgram` after `unixsock-stream`.

- **Stock zenoh refuses the fork's protocol.** Zenoh 1.10.1 refuses
  `unixsock-dgram` when it loads its configuration. A policy naming it
  under `zenoh-1.10.1` is `unknown_protocol`, and §2.4 never narrows a
  subject to it there.
- **The narrowing.** A subject bound to a principal P is selected by the
  binding's list, in its order, less the link protocols of every face
  whose far principal is not P.
- **A link.v1 policy** reads as `zenoh-1.10.1`.
- **A claim no check observes.** The zenoh a router runs is not on the
  bus (core §11.3). A policy naming the fork for a stock router writes a
  block that the router refuses to load, which at least fails loudly.

### 2.5 Attachment: a device face

`link.v1` §2.5 holds, and a face may attach devices, a third shape.
`[F: v2/plan.json]` `[Sc: core constrained.md §10]`
- **`device`**: the far side is the sessions of the owners `devices`
  lists, each a client of the near router across the face. Each is an
  owner on a first hop (core §8.1).
  - A device's session receives only the declarations its interests ask
    for, as a `client`'s does. The router needs no `gateway` block.
  - The face's `far` is the principal its devices' sessions present. A
    face has one, so its devices share it. Devices of distinct
    principals attach over distinct faces, each with link protocols of
    its own (`link.v1` §2.14).
  - The far principal runs each device (core §11.1). Its Own grant is
    what the carve lets cross (§2.6).
- **The devices are listed.** Every rule of `link.v1` that reads the
  listed services reads each device beside them, by its address. That is
  the exposure denies (§2.7), heartbeats (§2.9), tombstone windows
  (§2.11) and liveness evidence (§2.12), on every face of the policy.
  The one exception is downsampling, on the device's own face (§2.8).
- **Listed once.** A device is listed on one face, and never among
  `services` (`device_listed`, §2.1).
- **What a device declares** is its instance token and no interface
  token, and it holds no bundle (core §8.1, §8.4). The face spares it
  nothing it declares beyond that: its session's declarations cross the
  face before the router's access control reads them (core Appendix B).
  - A generator MUST refuse a device that implements `archive.v1`, or an
    interface a revision given of which uses `redundancy.v1`
    (`presence_required`, §2.3). Core §8.1 forbids an owner on a first
    hop both. `[F: v2/plan.json]`
  - The plan warns of each template of a device's interface that
    declares `epoch` (`epoch_on_device`). Its owner's member tokens cross
    the face at each transport, and the face denies them (§2.6).
    `[F: v2/plan.json]`
- **Backhaul keeps the whole deny.** A `client` face, a gateway's among
  them, and a `south-region` face keep `link.v1` §2.6's `zk`.

### 2.6 The `@zk` deny on a device face

`link.v1` §2.6 holds on every other face. On a device face, `zk` lets an
instance key cross where a device's own grant needs it, and keeps the
rest of `@zk` denied.
- **The carve.** On a device face, a generator MUST derive three denies
  in place of `link.v1`'s `zk`, in this order, before `adv`:
  `[F: v2/plan.json]` `[Sc: core constrained.md §10]`

  | `id` | `flows` | `messages` | `key_exprs` |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `zk` | both | the nine | `zk2/*/*/@zk/alive/**`, `zk2/*/*/@zk/member/**`, `zk2/@zk/**` |
  | `zk:instance:egress` | `egress` | `put`, `delete`, `declare_queryable`, `reply`, `liveliness_token`, `declare_liveliness_subscriber`, `liveliness_query` | `zk2/*/*/@zk/instance/**` |
  | `zk:instance:ingress` | `ingress` | `declare_subscriber`, `query`, `declare_liveliness_subscriber`, `liveliness_query` | `zk2/*/*/@zk/instance/**` |

- **What crosses** is an instance key, on the seven pairs of core
  §11.2's Own row. Those are ingress `put`, `delete`,
  `declare_queryable`, `reply` and `liveliness_token`, and egress `query`
  and `declare_subscriber`.
  - That is a device's instance token, its descriptor's queryable, puts
    and replies, and a near reader's GET of it and subscription to it.
- **Whose instance keys** is the far principal's grants' to decide. Its
  Own grant holds its devices' (core §11.1). No grant writes another
  service's presence: `deny` grants none, and under `allow` a
  principal's complement denies it (core §11.2). So the carve names no
  address.
- **The rest of `@zk` stays denied.** Every interface token, member token
  and contract key is denied on every pair, and every instance key on the
  eleven other pairs. So the near side's tokens never reach a device.
- **Why by pair.** Deny works by inclusion, and a key expression has no
  negation (core §11.2, §11.3). No set of denies holds every instance key
  but a device's.
- **The key forms.** The four key expressions cover each `@zk` form of
  core §1.1. `zk2/**/@zk/**` covers more: keys of no form, as
  `zk2/a/@zk/x`, which no participant writes.
- **Never omitted, carved only here.** `link.v1` §2.6's MUST holds: a
  generator puts `zk` in every face's plan. A generator MUST NOT carve it
  on any other face. A deployment MUST NOT carve it in a router's block
  but as a device face's plan does. `[F: v2/plan.json, v2/lints.json]`
- **The control.** A block that keeps `link.v1`'s whole `zk` on a device
  face is no fault (§2.15). The near side then finds no device, and the
  device's presence is unobservable there
  (`[Sc: core constrained.md §10]`, step 4).

### 2.7 What crosses: frames per face

`link.v1` §2.7 holds, and a face may list its frames.
`[F: v2/plan.json, v2/exposure.json]` `[Sc: §2]`
- **A face's `frames`** lists the frame resources that cross it. On a
  face with `frames`, a generator MUST let a frame resource of a listed
  interface cross only when the face lists it and its contract exposes
  it `link`. `[F: v2/plan.json]` `[Sc: §2]`
- **The rest are denied by name.** Every other frame resource of a
  listed interface is a template that does not cross, which the
  carve-out denies (`link.v1` §2.7).
- **A face with no `frames`** keeps `link.v1`'s rule: every frame
  resource exposed `link` crosses.
- **An empty `frames`**, `frames = {}`, is a face with `frames` that
  lists none: no frame resource crosses it. Each exposed one is denied by
  name, and draws `frame_unlisted` where no face of the policy lets it
  cross. The plan's `frames` is then empty. `[F: v2/plan.json]`
- **Why per face.** One contract serves several media. An RF face lets a
  link frame across every minute, and a satellite face a message frame
  alone. Each face lists what it can carry.
- **An entry** `<iface>/<template>` names the frame resource
  `<iface>/stream/<template>`. A generator refuses an entry that names no
  frame resource of a listed interface (`frame_unknown`).
- **Its cadence** is `every_s`: how often its owner puts a frame on this
  face. That is the owner's configuration, which no router can check.
  The plan states it as the deployment's word to the far side.

**Keyframes.** `keyframe_s` is the longest the face accepts between two
full records. A full record heals every field a lost record left
`unknown` (core §7.7). A generator refuses a listed frame that crosses
when `keyframe_s` cannot be met (`keyframe_unreachable`).
`[F: v2/plan.json]`
- An owner writes a full record at least once every `keyframe_every`
  records (core §7.7), and every frame holds a record. So at one frame
  every `every_s`, its full records come at most `keyframe_every` ×
  `every_s` apart.
- It may write them more often than its contract says, never less. So
  `keyframe_s` > `keyframe_every` × `every_s`, in integers, is
  unreachable.
- Across revisions, the plan reads the least `keyframe_every`.

**An exposed frame no face lists.** On a face with `frames`, the plan
warns of each exposed frame resource of a listed interface it leaves
out, when no face of the policy lets it cross (`frame_unlisted`). Its
owner then writes it for no far side. `[F: v2/plan.json]`

### 2.8 Downsampling

`link.v1` §2.8 holds, with a timed stream denied, and a listed frame at
its cadence.

**A timed stream is never downsampled.** A generator MUST NOT emit a
downsampling rule for a timed stream. It denies the stream instead, and
the plan warns (`timed_downsampled`). `[F: v2/plan.json, v2/exposure.json]`
`[Sc: §2]`
- A rule passes one sample per `interval_s`. A deadline below that is
  missed between every two samples (`timing.v1` §2.8). One above it
  judges the face's rate, not its owner's.
- Every arbitrated role's stream is timed: `arbitration.v1` §2.2
  requires its deadline. A thinned one leaves its provider ineligible
  between crossings (`arbitration.v1` §2.12).
- The key is read, not its value. An invalid value still says the
  contract meant a deadline, and the plan fails closed.
- Such a stream crosses as a frame resource, which is never downsampled
  and whose deadline core E050 admits. Otherwise it does not cross.

**A device's stream on its own face.** A generator MUST NOT emit a
downsampling rule for a device's stream on the face that lists the
device. `[F: v2/plan.json]`
- Its puts cross that face toward the near router, so a rule there,
  which is egress, would spare the face nothing.
- Its owner puts at the rate the face affords. That is its
  configuration, which no router can check.
- On another face of the policy, a device's crossing stream is
  downsampled as a listed service's is (§2.5).
- Every other rule here holds of a device's interfaces, since the plan's
  denies and warnings are per interface (`link.v1` §2.3). A device's
  timed stream is still denied (`timed_downsampled`), and so is its
  templated one (`templated_exposure`).

**A listed frame's cadence** is its entry's `every_s`.
`frame_over_interval` reads it in place of the frame's interval (core
§7.7), and so does `interval_over_horizon` (§2.12). `[F: v2/plan.json]`

### 2.10 The batch against the lease

`link.v1` §2.10 holds, over `[transport]`'s `batch_bytes` and `lease_ms`
and each face's `link_bps`. A linter warns of each face whose batch
takes more than a third of the lease to cross: `batch_bytes` × 24,000 >
`lease_ms` × `link_bps`, in integers (`batch_over_lease`, `at`
`transport.batch_bytes`). `[F: v2/lints.json]`

### 2.12 Liveness across the face

`link.v1` §2.12 holds, a listed frame's interval read as its cadence
(§2.8). A face also states the horizons it affords.
- **`horizon_s` is a budget floor.** A heartbeat and a frame resource
  each cross at their owner's cadence, never thinned (`link.v1` §2.8).
  Their owner puts one at least every half horizon (`freshness.v2` §2.4,
  core W108). A horizon below the face's `horizon_s` costs the face more
  than it affords.
- **The warning.** The plan warns of a crossing frame resource, or a
  crossing heartbeat, whose horizon is below `horizon_s`
  (`horizon_under_face`). A heartbeat's horizon is the shortest of the
  collections it covers. `[F: v2/plan.json]`
- **Never an override.** Horizons stay the contract's
  (`freshness.ttl_s`), and `horizon_s` changes no reader's judgement. A
  contract meant for a slow medium declares horizons that medium can
  carry.
- **A device's instance token is no evidence.** It crosses a device face
  toward the near side (§2.6), and shows the device's link, not its
  liveness (observer §3.11). The plan lists a device's evidence as a
  listed service's.

### 2.13 Counters

`link.v1` §2.13 holds, with `telemetry.v2`'s reading of a counter series.
- **The epoch is the run.** A counter field of a frame resource that
  `telemetry.series` declares is a series of `telemetry.v2`. Its epoch is
  the frame's run, in band (`telemetry.v2` §2.19). It crosses with its
  frame, so `link.v1` §2.13's in-band epoch costs no byte.
- **A decrease within a run is a finding.** Across the face, a reader
  reads such a series' pairs as `telemetry.v2` §2.19 and §2.20 say. A pair
  across two runs is a reset. A 64-bit counter's decrease within one run
  is the finding. `[F: telemetry.v2 v2/continuity.json]`
  `[Sc: telemetry.v2 §5]`
- **Where no epoch shows,** `link.v1` §2.13's reading holds: a decrease
  is a reset.

### 2.15 Checking a router's block

`link.v1` §2.15 holds, and reads `zk_not_denied` on a device face as
follows. `[F: v2/lints.json]`
- **On a device face** the check reads four key expressions in place of
  `zk2/**/@zk/**`, each with the namespace.
  - On each of the 18 pairs it reads `zk2/@zk/**`,
    `zk2/*/*/@zk/alive/**` and `zk2/*/*/@zk/member/**`.
  - On each of the eleven pairs outside core §11.2's Own row, it reads
    `zk2/*/*/@zk/instance/**` too.
- **The finding** is `zk_not_denied`, at `@zk`. It is reported when, on
  some pair, a key expression read for it is included by no deny of a
  subject that can match the far principal's session.
- **A whole `zk` passes.** `zk2/**/@zk/**` includes each of the four.
- **On any other face** the check is `link.v1`'s, so a device face's
  carve there is `zk_not_denied`.

### 2.16 The transport

- **The router's, for every link.** Zenoh 1.10.1 reads the lease, the
  keep-alives, the batch, `wait_before_close` and the timeouts once per
  router. They apply to every link the router holds, its near side's
  among them.
  - So `[transport]` is the router's, and no face's.
  - A router that faces two media needs both media's settings, and can
    run one. A deployment runs a router per medium.
- **The router runs it.** A deployment MUST run the near router with the
  policy's `[transport]`, as the generator writes it (§2.18).
  `[Sc: §1]`
- **`wait_before_close` at least the lease.** A deployment MUST NOT run
  a router whose `wait_before_close` is below its lease.
  `[F: v2/lints.json]` `[Sc: §1]`
  - A `block` push that cannot be queued within `wait_before_close`
    closes the transport. On a link that is slower than its traffic, or
    out for less than a lease, that close comes before the lease would
    have ended it.
  - Below the lease, a transport can close on one end while the other
    keeps it for up to a lease. zenoh-modem measured an outage band in
    which the link then never came back (Appendix B).
  - A linter reports `wait_under_lease` when `wait_before_close_ms` <
    `lease_ms`, and refuses the policy.
- **Both ends run one lease.** The lease is not negotiated. Each end
  sends keep-alives at its own interval, and expires its receive side
  after the other end's announced lease. Both ends of a face SHOULD run
  the policy's `lease_ms` and `keep_alive`. `[F: v2/transport.json]`
  `[Sc: §1]`
  - So the uplink's keep-alives are the near router's to bill, and the
    downlink's the far end's.
  - A far end left at zenoh's defaults sends 34,560 keep-alives a day
    down a link whose near end was tuned, and nothing on the near end
    shows it. A generator writes the far end's block (§2.18), and a tool
    compares the two ends (§2.19).
- **The keep-alives a day.** An idle link carries 2 × 86,400 ×
  `keep_alive` / (`lease_ms` / 1,000) keep-alives a day, both
  directions together. Data refreshes the lease too, and a batch sent
  resets its end's interval.
- **A keep-alive that flaps.** An idle transport closes when a lease
  passes with nothing received. A lease can hold as few as `keep_alive`
  − 1 keep-alives, so a false close takes that many losses in a row.
  - A deployment SHOULD NOT run a `keep_alive` whose expected false
    closes are more than one a day. `[F: v2/lints.json]`
  - That is when 2 × 86,400 × 1,000 × `keep_alive` ×
    `loss_ppm`^(`keep_alive` − 1) > `lease_ms` ×
    10^(6 × (`keep_alive` − 1)), in integers. A linter warns of it
    (`keep_alive_flaps`).
  - At a `keep_alive` of 2 and 5 % loss, every lost keep-alive closed the
    link in the baseline's LoRa runs. At 1, a keep-alive sits on the
    lease's boundary, and the lint warns whatever the loss.
- **The sequence window.** The resolution is the least of both ends',
  so one end's `16bit` suffices.
  - A frame whose sequence number is half the resolution's mask or more
    past the last one received is dropped as old. That **window** is 64
    frames at `8bit`, 8,192 at `16bit`, 2^27 at `32bit` and 2^31 at
    `64bit`, as zenoh 1.10.1 masks them.
  - A linter warns when one lease can carry the window in full batches:
    window × 8 × `batch_bytes` × 1,000 ≤ `lease_ms` × `link_bps`, in
    integers (`sequence_window`). `[F: v2/lints.json]`
  - `16bit` writes a sequence number in at most 2 bytes, where `32bit`
    takes 4.
- **The timeouts against the handshake.** Opening a transport exchanges
  about 384 bytes. A linter warns of an `open_timeout_ms` or
  `accept_timeout_ms` below twice that exchange's air:
  timeout × `link_bps` < 6,144,000, in integers
  (`open_under_handshake`). `[F: v2/lints.json]`
- **A `block` put larger than a batch.** Zenoh fragments a message
  larger than its batch, and each fragment queues behind what came
  before. A `block` push that cannot be queued within
  `wait_before_close` closes the transport.
  - The plan warns of a crossing `block` resource whose longest put is
    above `batch_bytes` (`block_over_batch`). `[F: v2/plan.json]`
  - A packed record's longest put is its longest record, every optional
    field present, after an 8-byte envelope. A frame resource's is its
    largest frame (`link.v1` §2.8). Another type's length is unbounded,
    and draws none.
  - A state or an event is `block` unless its contract says otherwise
    (core §2.4).
- **Data spares the keep-alives.** A batch sent resets its end's
  keep-alive interval. So an uplink that sends data at least once an
  interval sends no keep-alive.
  - The plan warns of a face whose shortest crossing cadence is above
    the keep-alive interval: cadence × `keep_alive` > `lease_ms`, in
    milliseconds (`keep_alive_not_suppressed`). `[F: v2/plan.json]`
  - The cadences are each crossing frame resource's, and each crossing
    heartbeat's period, half its horizon (`freshness.v2` §2.4). A frame
    resource with no interval stated has none.
  - A face with no cadence draws none. A downsampled stream's cadence is
    its owner's, which the plan does not know.

### 2.17 The contract side: packed

**A contract that lists `link.v2`** MUST type every resource it exposes
`link` as packed (core §7.6). `[F: v2/exposure.json]`
- **What it binds.** Streams, states and events. An operation crosses
  by grant, and is never exposed (`link.v1` §2.9).
- **No `@stream`.** An explicit stream's samples carry no envelope, and
  a packed type needs one (core E044). So such a contract exposes none.
- **Why.** A packed record costs little more than its information (core
  §7.6), and the far side decodes it through the catalog (§2.18).
- **The finding.** A linter reports `packed_required`, an error, at each
  such resource's name, with no face. `[F: v2/exposure.json]` The core
  has no code for it, since no core lint reads an annotation's value
  (`link.v1` §2.2).
- **A generator** refuses a deployment that gives such a revision
  (§2.3). `[F: v2/plan.json]`

**A contract that does not list `link.v2`** is not bound. Its exposed
resources cross a link.v2 face as `link.v1` says. The plan warns of each
crossing one whose type is not packed (`not_packed`). So a face can move
to link.v2 first, and its contracts when they are packed.
`[F: v2/plan.json]`

### 2.18 What a generator emits

- **The transport block.** A generator MUST write the policy's
  `[transport]` into the near router's configuration, beside the
  `access_control`, `downsampling` and `gateway` blocks of `link.v1`. It
  writes each key at zenoh's configuration key (§2.1's table), the
  values as the policy states them, and `wait_before_close` in µs: the
  policy's milliseconds × 1,000. `[F: v2/transport.json]` `[Sc: §1]`
  - All eight keys are written, the defaults of absent ones included, so
    the block states every setting it rests on.
  - The block is the router's top-level `transport` and `routing`
    objects. A router configuration that already holds a `transport`
    object (its authentication, its TLS) merges the two: a JSON5 object
    holds one `transport` key.
- **The far end's block.** On request, a generator writes the same two
  objects for the far end: the far router's configuration, the gateway
  client's session configuration, or each device's. (The reference:
  `zenctl acl gen --far-config`.)
- **The plan** carries the transport, the binding and the catalog id,
  and each face's `reply_timeout_ms` and `frames`. `[F: v2/plan.json]`
- **Given the catalog.** A generator given the catalog the far side
  holds (core §8.6) MUST refuse a deployment in which a revision of an
  interface with something crossing is not an entry. The far side could
  not decode what that revision's owners put (`catalog_missing`).
  `[F: v2/plan.json]`
  - Something crossing a face is a crossing resource of the interface,
    or the heartbeat covering it.
  - A revision is looked up by its interface id and fingerprint. One
    given as its resources alone, with no contract, has no fingerprint,
    and is not looked up.
  - (The reference: `zenctl acl gen --catalog`.)

### 2.19 Checking a router's transport

A tool checks a router's configuration against the policy, and the
far end's against the near router's. It MUST report what the fixture
reports. `[F: v2/transport.json]` (The reference: `zenctl check face`,
beside `link.v1` §2.15's checks.)

**What a configuration omits.** A tool reads each of the eight keys the
configuration states, and a key it omits at the binding's default:

| Key | `zenoh-1.10.1` | `zm-1.10.2-r1` |
|---|---|---|
| `transport/link/tx/lease` | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| `transport/link/tx/keep_alive` | 4 | 4 |
| `transport/link/tx/queue/congestion_control/block/wait_before_close` | 5,000,000 µs | 10,000,000 µs |
| `transport/link/tx/sequence_number_resolution` | `"32bit"` | `"32bit"` |
| `transport/link/tx/batch_size` | 65,535 | 65,535 |
| `transport/unicast/open_timeout` | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| `transport/unicast/accept_timeout` | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| `routing/interests/timeout` | 10,000 | 10,000 |

**The codes**, each with no face:
- **`transport_differs`** (error, `at` the policy's key, as
  `transport.lease_ms`): the router's value of the key is not the
  policy's.
- **`wait_under_lease`** (error, `at` `transport.wait_before_close_ms`):
  the router's `wait_before_close` is below its lease, whatever the
  policy says. It reads both as integers, a negative one too. A string
  or a fraction is no value zenoh runs. It is `transport_differs`, and
  this check skips it.
- **`peer_differs`** (warning, `at` `transport.lease_ms` or
  `transport.keep_alive`): given the far end's configuration, its lease
  or its `keep_alive` is not the router's. A mismatch closes the link on
  one end sooner than the policy says, or spends more keep-alives on the
  other, silently (§2.16).
- **`catalog_mismatch`** (error, `at` `catalog`): given the deployment
  catalog the far side holds, its id is not the policy's `catalog`, or
  the policy names none. `[F: v2/transport.json]`
  - **The catalog** is read as core §8.6 reads one, and its id is
    §8.6's. A successor's id is its own, so a far side holding the
    policy's catalog's successor is a mismatch too.
  - **A file §8.6 refuses** holds no id to compare. A tool names the
    refusal, and does not ask the question (§5).

**What it cannot see.** A tool checks a file someone says a router runs
(core §11.3). That the router runs it, and which zenoh runs it, are
not on the bus.

### 2.20 A caller across the face

A face's `reply_timeout_ms` is core §8.1's reply timeout. A caller, a
consumer or a tool across the face waits at least that long for each
reply it asks for, as core §8.1 says. `[Sc: core constrained.md §8]`
- **Why it is the policy's.** A reply queued behind a radio's traffic
  came back after every default timeout, and the router drops a reply
  later than its query's timeout (core Appendix B). §2.1's
  `reply_timeout_short` warns of a timeout below 16 full batches' air.
- **Its session** runs the face's `[transport]`, as the far end's block
  writes it (§2.18), so that both ends run one lease (§2.16).
- **On a device face** the near side's readers are across the face from
  each device. A device's replies, its descriptor's among them, take as
  long to come back (core §8.1).
- (The reference: `zenkey_model::link::FacePolicy::far_side`, read by
  the runtime's `Client::across`, `Consumer::across` and
  `TargetStartup::across`, and by `zenctl`'s raw observers given
  `--face-policy` and `--face`.)

### 2.21 A reader across the face

A face whose `arq` is false repairs no frame its medium lost, and
zenoh 1.10.1 repairs no declaration such a frame carried (core
Appendix B). A consumer or a tool across it declares a subscription
again after two of its horizons of silence, its transport up, as core
§8.5 and observer §7.5 say. `[Sc: core constrained.md §9]`
- **With `arq` true** nothing is declared again: a lost frame is
  repaired below zenoh, or the transport closes, and a transport that
  opens again declares everything anew.
- **It repairs a subscriber's declaration**, never a lost
  `DeclareKeyExpr`, which zenoh 1.10.1 sends once a transport (core
  §8.5). Link ARQ is what keeps that one.
- §2.1's `no_arq` warns of a face whose medium repairs nothing, so that
  its deployment knows its readers will.
- (The reference: `zenkey::redeclare`, through `Consumer::across` and the
  fleet's `Monitor::watch_across`.)

## Appendix A. Against link.v1

| `link.v1` | `link.v2` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No `profile` | `profile = "link.v2"`, required | An older linter refuses a link.v2 policy as `unknown_field`. An older generator must not write a block that silently drops its transport. |
| A face's `lease_ms`, optional, and `batch_bytes` | `[transport]`, the router's, with `wait_before_close`, the sequence width and the timeouts; four keys required | Zenoh 1.10.1 reads them once per router. A deployment that omits them runs zenoh's 10 s lease by omission. |
| §2.1's nine link protocols | The binding's list (§2.4) | The fork zenoh-modem builds on adds `unixsock-dgram`, which stock zenoh refuses. |
| Nothing on the medium | `loss_ppm`, `arq`, `reply_timeout_ms` per face | The keep-alive lint reads the loss. A lost declaration is never repaired, and a reply over RF took longer than every default timeout. |
| No catalog | `catalog`, its id | The far side decodes with the catalog, and a tool checks it holds the policy's. |
| The generator writes `access_control`, `downsampling`, `gateway` | And `transport` and `routing` (§2.18) | The settings are worth nothing in a policy that no router runs. |
| Every frame resource exposed `link` crosses | A face's `frames` lists those that cross it, each at its cadence (§2.7) | One contract serves several media. |
| A timed stream is downsampled | It is denied (§2.8) | A thinned stream misses its deadline between two samples. |
| A contract's types are its own | A contract that lists `link.v2` exposes only packed resources (§2.17) | A packed record costs little more than its information. |
| No horizon on the face | `horizon_s`, a budget floor, never an override (§2.12) | A heartbeat or a frame is never thinned, so its horizon is its cost. |
| A far side's callers wait their own timeouts | At least the face's `reply_timeout_ms` (§2.20, core §8.1) | A reply over RF came back after every default timeout, and the router drops a late one. |
| `client` and `south-region` | And `device`: owners on a first hop, their instance subtree carved from `zk` on the Own grant's pairs (§2.5, §2.6) | A first-hop device's instance token lets the near side find it. The rest of its presence costs air a deny cannot spare. |
| A lost declaration stays lost | A reader declares a silent subscription again where `arq` is false (§2.21, core §8.5) | Zenoh never repairs a declaration its medium lost while the transport stays up. |

## Appendix B. The media

These are the settings the reference's examples run, one router per
medium (`examples/zk2/link/media/`), and zenoh-modem's routers too
(below). SF9's row is zenoh-modem's alone. Each row's expected false
closes are the keep-alives a day × loss^(`keep_alive` − 1), at 5 % loss
on an idle link.

| Medium (`link_bps`) | Lease / `keep_alive` | Keep-alives a day | False closes a day | `wait_before_close` | Timeouts | Batch |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|
| RF (2,400) | 240 s / 4 | 2,880 | 0.36 | 240 s | 10 s | 222 |
| LoRa SF7 (5,469) | 120 s / 4 | 5,760 | 0.72 | 120 s | 10 s | 224 |
| LoRa SF9 (1,758) | 240 s / 4 | 2,880 | 0.36 | 240 s | 10 s | 117 |
| LoRa SF12 (293) | 1,080 s / 4 | 640 | 0.08 | 1,080 s | 40 s, interests 40 s | 224 |
| SBD (2,400, store and forward) | 3,600 s / 2 | 96 | none: delayed, not lost | 3,600 s | 1,000 s | 271 |

- **RF.** zenoh-modem's RF router shipped a 10 s lease with 4
  keep-alives and a 10 s `wait_before_close`. 240 s with 4 keep-alives
  needs three losses in a row to close.
- **LoRa.** At SF7, 60 s with 2 keep-alives closed the link at every lost
  keep-alive (the baseline's LoRa runs, 9 of 9). 120 s with 4 sends as
  many keep-alives and needs three losses in a row. At SF12, 540 s with
  2 cost more air in reopens than the duty allows. The handshake's 384
  bytes take 10.5 s at 293 bit/s, so the timeouts are 40 s. SF10 to
  SF12 carry 51 bytes, below zenoh's floor: a fragment layer below zenoh
  carries the 224 B batch in tiles.
- **LoRa at SF9.** At 60 s with 2, a keep-alive went every 30 s, so a
  frame a minute still left one between two frames. 240 s with 4 puts
  the interval at the frames' minute. Its 115 B frame makes the batch
  117.
- **SBD.** zenoh-modem's tuned values, unchanged: 96 keep-alives a day.
  The service retries, so a keep-alive is late rather than lost.
- **`wait_before_close` below the lease.** zenoh-modem measured that a
  transport whose `wait_before_close` is below its lease strands the
  link in an outage band: one end closes on a full queue while the
  other keeps its lease. Its RF router runs 10 s for that reason.

**zenoh-modem's faces** (`examples/zk2/zenoh-modem/faces/`). Each
policy names the binding `zm-1.10.2-r1`, the catalog the ground holds,
and the frames of `modem.v3` its face carries. Their plans are
`plan.json`'s last five cases.
- **RF**: `frames/link` and the seven group frames every 60 s, which is
  the keep-alive interval. `horizon_s` is 120.
- **LoRa SF7**: the same eight at 60 s, above the 30 s keep-alive
  interval (`keep_alive_not_suppressed`).
- **LoRa SF9**: `frames/link` at 60 s, the group frames at 120 s.
- **LoRa SF12**: every frame at 900 s, and a group frame's full record
  at least hourly (`keyframe_s`). Its owner writes one every fourth
  frame, which the contract's 15 records allow (§2.7). `frames/link`'s
  150 s horizon draws `interval_over_horizon` and `horizon_under_face`.
  The 270 s interval draws `keep_alive_not_suppressed`.
- **SBD**: `frames/message` alone, every 1,800 s, the keep-alive
  interval: a paid message replaces a keep-alive.
- **Every face** warns `frame_unlisted` of the frames its policy leaves
  out, which its device does not publish.
- **`modem.v3` lists `link.v1`.** It crosses these faces as §2.17's last
  paragraph says. Every resource it exposes is packed, so no plan warns
  `not_packed`.
