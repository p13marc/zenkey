# `link.v2`: a constrained face, and the transport it runs

| | |
|---|---|
| **Text version** | 0.1 ([`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md)) |
| **Status** | experimental |
| **Written against** | core 0.77 ([`../../core.md`](../../core.md)) |
| **Uses** | no other profile. It restates [`link.v1`](v1.md), text 0.10, by reference (§0), and its plan reads [`freshness.v1`](../freshness/v1.md)'s horizon as link.v1's does. |
| **Evidence** | [`conformance/v2/`](conformance/v2/), [`scenarios-v2.md`](scenarios-v2.md) |

`link.v1` says what may cross a constrained face, and leaves the
transport the face runs to the deployment. At zenoh's defaults that
transport is wrong for a radio. A 10 s lease with 4 keep-alives sends
69,120 keep-alives a day over an idle link. At LoRa's SF12, a keep-alive
every 30 s takes more air than the duty budget allows.

`link.v2` puts the transport in the face policy. A link.v2 policy states:
- **the router's transport**, `[transport]`: its lease, keep-alives,
  `wait_before_close`, sequence width, batch and timeouts (§2.16);
- **the zenoh the router runs**, `binding`, which names its link
  protocols (§2.4);
- **the catalog** the far side decodes with, by its id (§2.1);
- **per face**, the medium's loss, whether it repairs a lost frame, and
  how long a caller across it waits for a reply (§2.1).

A generator writes the transport into the router's configuration, and
for the far end on request (§2.18). A tool checks a router's
configuration against it, and the two ends against each other (§2.19).

```toml
# The RF node's router (Appendix B)
profile  = "link.v2"
system   = "lab"
services = ["radio-a"]
binding  = "zenoh-1.10.1"           # the zenoh this router runs

[transport]                         # the router's, for every link it holds
lease_ms             = 240000
keep_alive           = 4
wait_before_close_ms = 240000       # at least the lease
sequence             = "16bit"
batch_bytes          = 222          # the radio's SDU and 2 B of framing

[face.rf]
far              = "ground"
attach           = "client"
select           = { link_protocols = ["unixsock-stream"] }
class            = "constrained"
expose           = "link"
interval_s       = 60
max_outage_s     = 3600
link_bps         = 2400
loss_ppm         = 50000            # 5 %
arq              = false            # the radio repairs no lost frame
reply_timeout_ms = 15000            # what a caller across the face waits
```

## 0. Conventions

The keywords, the roles and the evidence tags are core §0's, and the
terms are `link.v1` §0's. Here, `[F: <file>]` names a fixture in
[`conformance/v2/`](conformance/v2/), and `[Sc: §n]` a section of
[`scenarios-v2.md`](scenarios-v2.md). A `link.v1` fixture or scenario
says so, as in `[F: link.v1 plan.json]`.

**By reference.** Every rule of `link.v1` holds for a link.v2 policy,
with four readings:
- §2.1 is this text's: a link.v2 policy is read as §2.1 below says;
- a face's `batch_bytes` and `lease_ms` are `[transport]`'s (§2.16);
- §2.1's closed list of link protocols is the binding's (§2.4);
- §2.10 reads `[transport]` (§2.10 below).

A rule this text states under a number of `link.v1`'s replaces that
rule. Every other number is `link.v1`'s, and holds as written. This
version adds §2.16, §2.18 and §2.19.

Terms:
- **Binding**: the zenoh a near router runs, as the policy names it
  (§2.4).
- **The ends** of a face: the near router, and the **far end**, the far
  router or the gateway client's session that attaches over the face.
- **The keep-alive interval**: `lease_ms` / `keep_alive`. Each end sends
  a keep-alive when it has sent nothing for one interval.
- **A full batch**: `batch_bytes` bytes. Its **air** is `batch_bytes` ×
  8 / `link_bps` seconds.

## 2. Rules

### 2.1 The face policy

A **link.v2 policy** is a face policy, a TOML 1.0 file, whose `profile`
is `"link.v2"`. A generator and a linter MUST read it exactly as the
fixtures do: the same file refused with the same findings, or accepted
with the same warnings and the same plan. `[F: v2/lints.json,
v2/plan.json]`

**One reader for both.** A policy with no `profile` is a `link.v1`
policy. A reader of this text MUST read it as `link.v1` §2.1 says,
finding for finding. `[F: v2/lints.json]` A `profile` of any other
value is `bad_value`, and the rest is read as a link.v2 policy.

**The top level.**

| Key | Value | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `profile` | `"link.v2"` | yes | This text. |
| `system`, `services` | as `link.v1` §2.1's | yes | The near router's system and its listed services. |
| `binding` | `"zenoh-1.10.1"` or `"zm-1.10.2-r1"` | yes | The zenoh the near router runs (§2.4). |
| `catalog` | a string of 8 lowercase hex digits | no | The id of the deployment catalog the far side decodes with (§2.19). |
| `transport` | a table (below) | yes | The near router's transport (§2.16). |
| `face` | as `link.v1` §2.1's | yes | The router's constrained faces. |

**A face**, `[face.<name>]`, takes `link.v1` §2.1's keys `far`,
`attach`, `region`, `select`, `class`, `expose`, `interval_s`,
`max_outage_s` and `link_bps`, with their values and meanings, and these:

| Key | Value | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `select.link_protocols` | a non-empty array of the binding's link protocols, none repeated | yes | As `link.v1`'s, over the binding's list (§2.4). |
| `loss_ppm` | an integer from 0 to 1,000,000 | yes | The share of frames the medium loses, in parts per million. |
| `arq` | a boolean | yes | Whether the medium repairs a lost frame below zenoh. |
| `reply_timeout_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | yes | The deployment's word to every caller across the face: how long a reply may take to come back. |

A face's `lease_ms` and `batch_bytes` are `[transport]`'s: on a face,
each is `moved_field`, an error. zenoh 1.10.1 reads them once per
router, for every link (§2.16).

**`[transport]`**, each key with zenoh 1.10.1's configuration key:

| Key | Value | Required | Zenoh's key | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lease_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | yes | `transport/link/tx/lease` | The lease each end announces. |
| `keep_alive` | an integer from 1 to 255 | yes | `transport/link/tx/keep_alive` | Keep-alives per lease. |
| `wait_before_close_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | yes | `transport/link/tx/queue/congestion_control/block/wait_before_close`, in µs | How long a `block` push waits for room before the transport closes. |
| `sequence` | `"8bit"`, `"16bit"`, `"32bit"` or `"64bit"` | yes | `transport/link/tx/sequence_number_resolution` | The frame sequence number's resolution. |
| `batch_bytes` | an integer from 1 to 65,535 | no; 65,535 | `transport/link/tx/batch_size` | The largest batch the router sends. |
| `open_timeout_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | no; 10,000 | `transport/unicast/open_timeout` | How long an end waits for a transport it opens. |
| `accept_timeout_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | no; 10,000 | `transport/unicast/accept_timeout` | How long an end waits for a transport it accepts. |
| `interests_timeout_ms` | an integer from 1 to 2^32−1 | no; 10,000 | `routing/interests/timeout` | How long a session waits for the interests a declaration answers. |

A key absent where it is optional takes the default the table gives,
which is zenoh 1.10.1's. A deployment that has not decided its lease,
keep-alives, `wait_before_close` and sequence width cannot write a
link.v2 policy.

**What a linter reports.** Findings are `link.v1` §2.1's: a `code`, a
`severity`, the `face` or none, and `at`. `at` is a key within the face
for a face's key, or `transport.<key>` for a key of `[transport]`.
Every code of `link.v1` §2.1's table holds. Two read differently:
`unknown_protocol` reads the binding's list (§2.4), and
`batch_over_lease` reads `[transport]` (§2.10). These are added:
`[F: v2/lints.json]`

| Code | Severity | When |
|---|---|---|
| `transport_missing` | error | A link.v2 policy with no `transport`. `at` is `transport`. |
| `moved_field` | error | A face's `lease_ms` or `batch_bytes`. |
| `wait_under_lease` | error | `wait_before_close_ms` is below `lease_ms` (§2.16). No face; `at` is `transport.wait_before_close_ms`. |
| `keep_alive_flaps` | warning | The face's loss makes more than one false close a day likely (§2.16). `at` is `transport.keep_alive`. |
| `sequence_window` | warning | One lease can carry as many full batches as the sequence window (§2.16). `at` is `transport.sequence`. |
| `open_under_handshake` | warning | `open_timeout_ms` or `accept_timeout_ms` is below twice the handshake's air at `link_bps` (§2.16). `at` is the key. |
| `reply_timeout_short` | warning | `reply_timeout_ms` is below the air of 16 full batches. `at` is `reply_timeout_ms`. |
| `no_arq` | warning | `arq` is false. `at` is `arq`. |

- **Each key is checked alone**, as `link.v1` §2.1 says. A face's check
  that reads `[transport]` is made when the keys it reads are valid,
  whatever else the policy holds. An optional key of `[transport]` that
  is absent is read at its default.
- **`no_arq`.** Zenoh 1.10.1 never repairs a declaration a medium lost: a
  subscriber, a queryable or a key declaration lost on the way stays
  lost while the transport stays up. A face whose medium repairs no
  frame is warned of, so that its deployment knows.
- **`reply_timeout_short`.** A reply queued behind other traffic can wait
  for a dozen batches to cross. Below 16 full batches' air, which is
  11.8 s at 2,400 bit/s with 222 B batches, a caller that waits
  `reply_timeout_ms` gives up on replies that are on their way. In
  integers: `reply_timeout_ms` × `link_bps` < 128,000 × `batch_bytes`.

### 2.4 Link protocols per binding

`binding` names the zenoh the near router runs. With it, it names the
closed list of link protocols that `select.link_protocols` takes, and
that `link.v1` §2.4's narrowing reads, in this order:
`[F: v2/lints.json, v2/plan.json]`
- **`zenoh-1.10.1`**: `link.v1` §2.1's nine, `tcp`, `udp`, `tls`,
  `quic`, `serial`, `unixpipe`, `unixsock-stream`, `vsock`, `ws`;
- **`zm-1.10.2-r1`**, the zenoh fork zenoh-modem builds on: the same,
  with `unixsock-dgram` after `unixsock-stream`.

- **Stock zenoh refuses the fork's protocol.** Zenoh 1.10.1 refuses
  `unixsock-dgram` when it loads its configuration. A policy naming it
  under `zenoh-1.10.1` is `unknown_protocol`, and §2.4 never narrows a
  subject to it there.
- **The narrowing.** A subject bound to a principal P is selected by the
  binding's list, in its order, less the link protocols of every face
  whose far principal is not P.
- **A link.v1 policy** reads as `zenoh-1.10.1`.
- **A claim no check observes.** The zenoh a router runs is not on the
  bus (core §11.3). A policy naming the fork for a stock router writes a
  block that the router refuses to load, which at least fails loudly.

### 2.10 The batch against the lease

`link.v1` §2.10 holds, over `[transport]`'s `batch_bytes` and `lease_ms`
and each face's `link_bps`. A linter warns of each face whose batch
takes more than a third of the lease to cross: `batch_bytes` × 24,000 >
`lease_ms` × `link_bps`, in integers (`batch_over_lease`, `at`
`transport.batch_bytes`). `[F: v2/lints.json]`

### 2.16 The transport

- **The router's, for every link.** Zenoh 1.10.1 reads the lease, the
  keep-alives, the batch, `wait_before_close` and the timeouts once per
  router. They apply to every link the router holds, its near side's
  among them.
  - So `[transport]` is the router's, and no face's.
  - A router that faces two media needs both media's settings, and can
    run one. A deployment runs a router per medium.
- **The router runs it.** A deployment MUST run the near router with the
  policy's `[transport]`, as the generator writes it (§2.18).
  `[Sc: §1]`
- **`wait_before_close` at least the lease.** A deployment MUST NOT run
  a router whose `wait_before_close` is below its lease.
  `[F: v2/lints.json]` `[Sc: §1]`
  - A `block` push that cannot be queued within `wait_before_close`
    closes the transport. On a link that is slower than its traffic, or
    out for less than a lease, that close comes before the lease would
    have ended it.
  - Below the lease, a transport can close on one end while the other
    keeps it for up to a lease. zenoh-modem measured an outage band in
    which the link then never came back (Appendix B).
  - A linter reports `wait_under_lease` when `wait_before_close_ms` <
    `lease_ms`, and refuses the policy.
- **Both ends run one lease.** The lease is not negotiated. Each end
  sends keep-alives at its own interval, and expires its receive side
  after the other end's announced lease. Both ends of a face SHOULD run
  the policy's `lease_ms` and `keep_alive`. `[F: v2/transport.json]`
  `[Sc: §1]`
  - So the uplink's keep-alives are the near router's to bill, and the
    downlink's the far end's.
  - A far end left at zenoh's defaults sends 34,560 keep-alives a day
    down a link whose near end was tuned, and nothing on the near end
    shows it. A generator writes the far end's block (§2.18), and a tool
    compares the two ends (§2.19).
- **The keep-alives a day.** An idle link carries 2 × 86,400 ×
  `keep_alive` / (`lease_ms` / 1,000) keep-alives a day, both
  directions together. Data refreshes the lease too, and a batch sent
  resets its end's interval.
- **A keep-alive that flaps.** An idle transport closes when a lease
  passes with nothing received. A lease can hold as few as `keep_alive`
  − 1 keep-alives, so a false close takes that many losses in a row.
  - A deployment SHOULD NOT run a `keep_alive` whose expected false
    closes are more than one a day. `[F: v2/lints.json]`
  - That is when 2 × 86,400 × 1,000 × `keep_alive` ×
    `loss_ppm`^(`keep_alive` − 1) > `lease_ms` ×
    10^(6 × (`keep_alive` − 1)), in integers. A linter warns of it
    (`keep_alive_flaps`).
  - At a `keep_alive` of 2 and 5 % loss, every lost keep-alive closed the
    link in the baseline's LoRa runs. At 1, a keep-alive sits on the
    lease's boundary, and the lint warns whatever the loss.
- **The sequence window.** The resolution is the least of both ends',
  so one end's `16bit` suffices.
  - A frame whose sequence number is half the resolution's mask or more
    past the last one received is dropped as old. That **window** is 64
    frames at `8bit`, 8,192 at `16bit`, 2^27 at `32bit` and 2^31 at
    `64bit`, as zenoh 1.10.1 masks them.
  - A linter warns when one lease can carry the window in full batches:
    window × 8 × `batch_bytes` × 1,000 ≤ `lease_ms` × `link_bps`, in
    integers (`sequence_window`). `[F: v2/lints.json]`
  - `16bit` writes a sequence number in at most 2 bytes, where `32bit`
    takes 4.
- **The timeouts against the handshake.** Opening a transport exchanges
  about 384 bytes. A linter warns of an `open_timeout_ms` or
  `accept_timeout_ms` below twice that exchange's air:
  timeout × `link_bps` < 6,144,000, in integers
  (`open_under_handshake`). `[F: v2/lints.json]`

### 2.18 What a generator emits

- **The transport block.** A generator MUST write the policy's
  `[transport]` into the near router's configuration, beside the
  `access_control`, `downsampling` and `gateway` blocks of `link.v1`. It
  writes each key at zenoh's configuration key (§2.1's table), the
  values as the policy states them, and `wait_before_close` in µs: the
  policy's milliseconds × 1,000. `[F: v2/transport.json]` `[Sc: §1]`
  - All eight keys are written, the defaults of absent ones included, so
    the block states every setting it rests on.
  - The block is the router's top-level `transport` and `routing`
    objects. A router configuration that already holds a `transport`
    object (its authentication, its TLS) merges the two: a JSON5 object
    holds one `transport` key.
- **The far end's block.** On request, a generator writes the same two
  objects for the far end: the far router's configuration, or the
  gateway client's session configuration. (The reference:
  `zenctl acl gen --far-config`.)
- **The plan** carries the transport, the binding and the catalog id,
  and each face's `reply_timeout_ms`. `[F: v2/plan.json]`

### 2.19 Checking a router's transport

A tool checks a router's configuration against the policy, and the
far end's against the near router's. It MUST report what the fixture
reports. `[F: v2/transport.json]` (The reference: `zenctl check face`,
beside `link.v1` §2.15's checks.)

**What a configuration omits.** A tool reads each of the eight keys the
configuration states, and a key it omits at the binding's default:

| Key | `zenoh-1.10.1` | `zm-1.10.2-r1` |
|---|---|---|
| `transport/link/tx/lease` | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| `transport/link/tx/keep_alive` | 4 | 4 |
| `transport/link/tx/queue/congestion_control/block/wait_before_close` | 5,000,000 µs | 10,000,000 µs |
| `transport/link/tx/sequence_number_resolution` | `"32bit"` | `"32bit"` |
| `transport/link/tx/batch_size` | 65,535 | 65,535 |
| `transport/unicast/open_timeout` | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| `transport/unicast/accept_timeout` | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| `routing/interests/timeout` | 10,000 | 10,000 |

**The codes**, each with no face:
- **`transport_differs`** (error, `at` the policy's key, as
  `transport.lease_ms`): the router's value of the key is not the
  policy's.
- **`wait_under_lease`** (error, `at` `transport.wait_before_close_ms`):
  the router's `wait_before_close` is below its lease, whatever the
  policy says.
- **`peer_differs`** (warning, `at` `transport.lease_ms` or
  `transport.keep_alive`): given the far end's configuration, its lease
  or its `keep_alive` is not the router's. A mismatch closes the link on
  one end sooner than the policy says, or spends more keep-alives on the
  other, silently (§2.16).
- **`catalog_mismatch`** (error, `at` `catalog`): given the deployment
  catalog the far side holds, its id is not the policy's `catalog`, or
  the policy names none. The id is the first 4 bytes of the sha256 of
  the catalog document's JCS bytes (RFC 8785), as 8 lowercase hex
  digits.

**What it cannot see.** A tool checks a file someone says a router runs
(core §11.3). That the router runs it, and which zenoh runs it, are
not on the bus.

## Appendix A. Against link.v1

| `link.v1` | `link.v2` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No `profile` | `profile = "link.v2"`, required | An older linter refuses a link.v2 policy as `unknown_field`. An older generator must not write a block that silently drops its transport. |
| A face's `lease_ms`, optional, and `batch_bytes` | `[transport]`, the router's, with `wait_before_close`, the sequence width and the timeouts; four keys required | Zenoh 1.10.1 reads them once per router. A deployment that omits them runs zenoh's 10 s lease by omission. |
| §2.1's nine link protocols | The binding's list (§2.4) | The fork zenoh-modem builds on adds `unixsock-dgram`, which stock zenoh refuses. |
| Nothing on the medium | `loss_ppm`, `arq`, `reply_timeout_ms` per face | The keep-alive lint reads the loss. A lost declaration is never repaired, and a reply over RF took longer than every default timeout. |
| No catalog | `catalog`, its id | The far side decodes with the catalog, and a tool checks it holds the policy's. |
| The generator writes `access_control`, `downsampling`, `gateway` | And `transport` and `routing` (§2.18) | The settings are worth nothing in a policy that no router runs. |

**Not in this version.** What a contract that lists `link.v2` must type,
frames per face, and how a far side's readers wait and re-declare are
not in this text yet.

## Appendix B. The media

These are the settings the reference's examples run, one router per
medium (`examples/zk2/link/media/`). Each row's expected false closes
are the keep-alives a day × loss^(`keep_alive` − 1), at 5 % loss on an
idle link.

| Medium (`link_bps`) | Lease / `keep_alive` | Keep-alives a day | False closes a day | `wait_before_close` | Timeouts | Batch |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|
| RF (2,400) | 240 s / 4 | 2,880 | 0.36 | 240 s | 10 s | 222 |
| LoRa SF7 (5,469) | 120 s / 4 | 5,760 | 0.72 | 120 s | 10 s | 224 |
| LoRa SF12 (293) | 1,080 s / 4 | 640 | 0.08 | 1,080 s | 40 s, interests 40 s | 224 |
| SBD (2,400, store and forward) | 3,600 s / 2 | 96 | none: delayed, not lost | 3,600 s | 1,000 s | 271 |

- **RF.** zenoh-modem's RF router shipped a 10 s lease with 4
  keep-alives and a 10 s `wait_before_close`. 240 s with 4 keep-alives
  needs three losses in a row to close.
- **LoRa.** At SF7, 60 s with 2 keep-alives closed the link at every lost
  keep-alive (the baseline's LoRa runs, 9 of 9). 120 s with 4 sends as
  many keep-alives and needs three losses in a row. At SF12, 540 s with
  2 cost more air in reopens than the duty allows. The handshake's 384
  bytes take 10.5 s at 293 bit/s, so the timeouts are 40 s. SF10 to
  SF12 carry 51 bytes, below zenoh's floor: a fragment layer below zenoh
  carries the 224 B batch in tiles.
- **SBD.** zenoh-modem's tuned values, unchanged: 96 keep-alives a day.
  The service retries, so a keep-alive is late rather than lost.
- **`wait_before_close` below the lease.** zenoh-modem measured that a
  transport whose `wait_before_close` is below its lease strands the
  link in an outage band: one end closes on a full queue while the
  other keeps its lease. Its RF router runs 10 s for that reason.
