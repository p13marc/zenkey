# `alarms.v2`: the active alarms of one service, in slots

| | |
|---|---|
| **Text version** | 0.2 ([`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md)) |
| **Status** | experimental |
| **Written against** | core 0.115 ([`../../core.md`](../../core.md)) |
| **Uses** | [`freshness.v2`](../freshness/v2.md), text 0.12: the alarms' horizon and their heartbeat (§2.7). It restates [`alarms.v1`](v1.md)'s rules by reference, text 0.14, and replaces its key, its document, its resolve and what establishes an owner's alarms. |
| **Evidence** | [`conformance/v2/`](conformance/v2/), [`scenarios-v2.md`](scenarios-v2.md), and `alarms.v1`'s for the rules it keeps |

`alarms.v1` lets a service say which of its alarms fire, and lets a
reader tell a firing alarm from a resolved one, from one its owner no
longer confirms, and from one whose owner is gone. Each transition is a
JSON document on a key of 16 hexadecimal digits: 113 to 161 bytes of
payload as measured, and a delete after each resolve.

`alarms.v2` keeps what `alarms.v1` lets a reader conclude, on a wire a
constrained link can afford:
- **An alarm lives in a slot.** A slot is a number from 0 to 255, and
  the last chunk of the alarm's key (§2.2).
- **A transition is one packed record,** 9 bytes for the usual one. It
  carries its rule and its labels as numbers, so no record depends on
  one heard before it (§2.4, §3).
- **The words are a table's.** A rule's name, its labels' values, its
  summary and its category are in a table that one of the service's own
  contracts holds (§4). An alarm no table names has its words on a key
  beside its record (§2.4).
- **A resolve is one put,** and no delete follows it (§2.5).
- **"Nothing fires" is read from one snapshot** (§2.15).

Its standard contract, [`alarms.v2.toml`](alarms.v2.toml), gives a
service two resources:

```text
zk2/<system>/<service>/alarms.v2/state/alarms/<slot>   one alarm's record, vouched for at least every 450 s while firing
zk2/<system>/<service>/alarms.v2/state/words/<slot>    its words, only where no table names them
```

A firing alarm is never put again unchanged, as since `alarms.v1`'s
revision 1.1: its owner's heartbeat confirms it. So this major saves
nothing on a quiet day. What it saves is each transition: one SDU of 45
bytes on zenoh-modem's RF face, where `alarms.v1`'s raise is 158 and its
resolve 259 in two (Appendix B).

---

## 0. Conventions

The keywords, the roles and the evidence tags are core §0's. Here,
`[F: <file>]` names a fixture in [`conformance/`](conformance/), and
`[Sc: §n]` a section of [`scenarios-v2.md`](scenarios-v2.md). `v1 §n`
names a section of [`alarms.v1`](v1.md), and a fixture of v1's says so,
as in `[F: v1 keys.json]`.

**By reference.** Every rule of `alarms.v1` holds for an owner, a reader
and a tool of this major, as written, except where §2 replaces it.
- Where a rule of v1 names `alarms.v1`, the interface or its keys, it
  reads `alarms.v2` and its keys.
- Where it names an alarm's key, it reads the alarm's slot. Where it
  names the alert key as a chunk of that key, it reads the alert key
  (below), which no key of this major holds.
- Where it names the document, it reads the record and its words. Where
  it names `timestamp`, it reads `at` (§2.4).

Terms are v1 §0's, and these:
- **Slot:** a number from 0 to 255, the value of `{slot}` in both
  templates (§2.2).
- **Record:** an `Alarm` of the standard packed schema (§3), on
  `alarms/<slot>`.
- **Identity:** an alarm's rule and its discriminating labels, as v1 §0
  has them. Its **alert key** is v1 §2.2's and §2.3's recipe over that
  rule's name and those labels as text. It is 64 bits, written as 16
  hexadecimal digits.
- **Table:** the rules an instance declares in `alarms.rules` (§4). A
  **table's rule** is one of them, and its **index** its place in the
  list, from 0.
- **Words:** an alarm's rule name, its labels, its summary and its
  category, as text. A table's rule has its words in the table. An
  alarm **by words** has them in an `alarms.v2.Words` document on
  `words/<slot>` (§2.4).
- **Named:** an alarm whose alert key a reader has: derived through the
  owner's table, or read from the record of an alarm by words.
  **Unnamed** is any other (§2.4).
- **Reference:** an alarm's owner's system, its owner's service and its
  alert key (§2.12).
- **Holds:** an instance holds a record on a slot from its put of it,
  until it puts another identity's there (§2.2).

## 2. Rules

### 2.1 Who implements it

v1 §2.1 holds. These items are added.
- **One major per instance.** An instance has one set of alarms. An
  owner MUST NOT implement `alarms.v1` and `alarms.v2` in one instance.
  `[Sc: §1]` A reader would count its alarms twice. A service moves from
  one to the other with a new instance, which adopts its alarms onto
  slots (§2.9).
- **A reader reads either.** A reader MUST read a service's alarms by
  the major its descriptor lists, and by this text where it lists both.
  `[F: v2/conclusions.json]` `[Sc: §1, §11]`

### 2.2 Slots

This section replaces v1 §2.2's key. Its recipe stays: it derives the
alert key, which is the alarm's identity (§2.12).
- **The key.** An alarm's record is on
  `zk2/<system>/<service>/alarms.v2/state/alarms/<slot>`. The slot is
  written in decimal with no leading zero, as core §2.2 writes a `uint`.
  - A slot is below the instance's bound: 256, or the cardinality its
    descriptor states (§2.8).
  - A member whose last chunk is no such number is on no slot. A reader
    reads nothing from it, and a tool MAY report it.
- **Which slot.** An owner MUST put an alarm's record on the slot this
  rule gives its identity, as the fixture does. `[F: v2/slots.json]`
  `[Sc: §1, §4]` The identity takes:
  1. the slot on which its instance holds a record of that identity,
     firing or resolved;
  2. else the lowest slot on which its instance holds no firing record:
     one it holds nothing on, or one whose record is resolved.
- **One identity, one slot.** An owner MUST NOT hold one identity's
  records on two slots. `[F: v2/slots.json]` `[Sc: §4]`
- **Never a firing one.** An owner MUST NOT put an identity's record on
  a slot whose record fires for another identity. `[F: v2/slots.json]`
  `[Sc: §7]` Where every slot fires, the raise is refused (§2.8).
- **Freed by its resolve.** A slot whose record is resolved is free for
  another identity at once. Its record stays until one takes it (§2.5).
- **Why the lowest.** Slots stay one digit, and few resolved records
  are kept: a resolved record on a low slot is the first one retaken.
- **Across a restart** an adopted alarm keeps its slot (§2.9).
- **The slot is an alias.** It names an alarm only while that alarm's
  record is on it. Nothing outside the owner's keys names an alarm by
  its slot (§2.12).
  - An archive or a recording read by key shows one slot's alarms in
    turn. Each record names its own.

### 2.3 The framing, and the sanitizing step

v1 §2.3 holds, over an alarm's words: the rule's name and each
discriminating label's name and value.
- **A table's words are framed.** A table that reads holds no name that
  breaks the framing (§4), so the sanitizing step changes nothing in
  them.
- **Words on `words/<slot>`** are sanitized as v1's documents are, by
  their owner and by every reader (§2.4).

### 2.4 The record, and its words

This section replaces v1 §2.4. An alarm's record is an `Alarm` (§3):

| Field | Says |
|---|---|
| `state` | `firing` or `resolved` (§2.6) |
| `severity` | `info`, `warning` or `critical`, in that order, as v1's. The record's escape is a severity a later text names |
| `rule` | the index of the alarm's rule in the owner's table, or `not_available` for an alarm by words |
| `at` | when the alarm's latest transition happened (§2.5), on the owner's clock, in whole seconds from the deployment epoch (core §7.6). Optional |
| `labels` | the codes of the rule's labels, as one integer (§4). Present exactly where the rule declares a label |
| `argument` | the rule's own number, where the rule declares one (§4): the reading that changes while the condition holds. Else `not_available` |
| `key` | the alert key, as an integer of 64 bits. Present exactly for an alarm by words |

- **The slot is not in the record.** It is the key's last chunk (§2.2).
- **`at` is optional.** An owner whose clock reads no instant the epoch
  codes writes none (§2.5). A device with no real-time clock at boot
  reads 1970. Under a catalog's later epoch that instant has no code,
  and core §7.6 forbids a record written for it. So such a device still
  raises its alarms, in 5 bytes.
- **By the table.** An owner writes an alarm by its table where the
  table names it: the rule's name is a table's rule, the alarm's
  discriminating labels are exactly the labels that rule declares, and
  each one's text has a code (§4).
  - `rule` is the index, `labels` the codes, and `key` is absent.
  - Host-scoped labels are not written. The key's system names the
    host, and v1 never hashed them.
- **By words.** An owner writes any other alarm by words: a rule
  configured at run time, a label whose values no contract can list, or
  text outside printable ASCII (§4).
  - `rule` is `not_available`, `labels` is absent, `argument` is
    `not_available`, and `key` is the alert key.
  - Its words are an `alarms.v2.Words` document on `words/<slot>`, the
    slot its record is on:

    ```text
    alarms.v2.Words   { "rule": string, "labels"?: { name: string }, "summary": string, "category"?: string }
    ```

  - The document is v1's without `timestamp`, `observed_at_ms`,
    `severity` and `state`. `rule`, `summary`, `category` and `labels`
    mean what v1 §2.4 says, host-scoped labels included.
  - It is put as strict JSON, in the envelope a state put carries (core
    §7.5). It is JSON because core §7.6 has no text field.
- **The owner's record.** An owner MUST write each record as the fixture
  does. `[F: v2/records.json]` It MUST NOT write one that a reader of
  this text reads as undecodable (below), nor `labels` or an `argument`
  other than `not_available` under a `rule` of `not_available`.
  `[F: v2/records.json]`
- **Words before their record.** At a raise by words, an owner MUST put
  the alarm's words before its record. `[Sc: §9]` It puts them again
  when the summary, the category or a host-scoped label changes. That is
  v1's refresh for such an alarm, and it moves no record (§2.5).
  - The words of a slot stay until other words are put there. An owner
    need not delete them.
  - The two puts ride two priorities, so a reader can receive the
    record first (core Appendix B). It then shows the alarm by its alert
    key until its words arrive.
- **A reader takes words by the key.** A reader MUST take a slot's
  words as the words of the slot's alarm only as the fixture does.
  `[F: v2/words.json]` `[Sc: §9]` It takes them while all of these hold:
  - the slot's record reads, and its `rule` is `not_available`;
  - the words read: strict JSON as v1 §2.4 reads a payload, inside the
    `Words` schema;
  - v1's recipe over the words, through the sanitizing step, gives the
    record's `key`.

  Otherwise it takes none, for the first of these: it holds no record
  on the slot (`no_record`); the record does not read (`undecodable`);
  the record names a table's rule (`table`); it read no words
  (`no_words`); the words do not read (`unreadable`); the recipe gives
  another key (`mismatch`). An alarm by words is then shown by its alert
  key, with no words.
- **Undecodable.** A reader MUST read a record as undecodable, as v1
  §2.4 reads a payload outside its schema, for the first of these:
  `[F: v2/records.json]`
  - core §7.6's reading refuses it: by its tag, its length or its pad
    (`tag`, `length`, `pad`);
  - `key` is present and `rule` is a table's (`key_with_rule`);
  - `key` is absent and `rule` is `not_available` (`key_missing`).

  It never reads such a record at a severity or in a state.
- **Unnamed is not undecodable.** A record that reads names a table's
  rule by numbers. A reader that cannot turn them into words reads the
  alarm as **unnamed**, for the first of these: `[F: v2/tables.json]`
  - it holds no table of the alarm's owner (`no_table`, §4);
  - the table holds no rule at the record's index (`rule`);
  - `labels` is present where the rule declares no label, or absent
    where it declares one, or holds a code a label does not have
    (`labels`).

  An unnamed alarm's state, severity and time are read. Its identity is
  not: the reader has no alert key for it (§2.6, §2.14).
- **Under `not_available`,** a reader ignores a `labels` field and an
  `argument` that an owner wrote against this text.
- **A severity it does not know.** A reader reads the escape as no
  severity known, which v1 §2.15's step 4 answers.
- **`at` is shown, and compared.** A tool shows it to the second, and an
  absent one as absent, never as the epoch. An acknowledgement compares
  it (§2.5).

### 2.5 The lifecycle

This section replaces v1 §2.5. An owner raises, escalates, refreshes and
resolves each alarm by putting its record on its slot. It confirms a
firing alarm by its heartbeat (§2.7).

| Act | When | Put | `state` | `at` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raise | the condition is found | the record, after its words if it is by words | `firing` | new |
| escalate | the severity changes, either way | the record | `firing` | new |
| refresh | the argument changes at one severity | the record | `firing` | kept |
| refresh, by words | the summary, the category or a host-scoped label changes | the words alone | as held | kept |
| confirm | neither, within 450 s | none: the heartbeat's bit (§2.7) | as held | kept |
| resolve | the condition no longer holds, or will be evaluated no more | the record, and no delete | `resolved` | new |

- **Its MUSTs.** An owner MUST put each act as the table says: a new
  `at` at each transition, and the one it holds at a refresh.
  `[F: v2/acks.json]` `[Sc: §1–§4]`
  - **New** is the owner's clock, in whole seconds. Where that is not
    later than the greatest `at` the owner knows it wrote for that
    identity, new is that `at` plus one second.
  - **No code, no `at`.** While its clock reads no instant the epoch
    codes, an owner writes no `at`. So does one whose new `at` would
    have no code.
  - **What stays.** A record's `rule`, `labels` and `key` are its
    identity's, and never change on its slot. A discriminating label
    that changes makes another alarm, as in v1.
  - **A resolved record** keeps the severity the alarm last fired at.
    Its `argument` is the last reading, or `not_available`.
- **The acknowledgement identity is `at`,** with the alarm's reference.
  It moves at a raise, an escalation and a resolve, and never at a
  refresh or a confirmation.
  - An acknowledgement applies while the alarm's `at` is at most the
    acknowledged one, as ZenSight's does with `timestamp` (v1 Appendix
    B). `[F: v2/acks.json]`
  - **Not the seq.** Every put carries a new seq, a refresh's too, and a
    late joiner sees only the latest. The seq orders a slot's samples
    within a run (core §4.3), and is no part of the identity.
  - **To the second.** A burst of n transitions of one alarm within one
    second moves `at` up to n − 1 seconds ahead of the clock. v1's step
    was a millisecond.
  - **With no `at`,** no acknowledgement applies. That is the safe side:
    the alarm is told again.
  - **Across a move from `alarms.v1`,** an adopted alarm's `at` is its
    `timestamp` in whole seconds (§2.9). So an acknowledgement made
    against v1 still applies. `[F: v2/acks.json]`
- **A resolve is one put.** After its resolved put the owner puts and
  deletes nothing: the record stays the slot's value. An owner MUST NOT
  delete a slot's key while its record fires. `[Sc: §3]` It need never
  delete one.
  - **Until its slot is retaken.** The resolved record stays until an
    identity takes the slot (§2.2), or its instance ends. A new instance
    puts no resolved record again (§2.9).
  - **What it gives.** A late joiner sees when each kept alarm
    resolved, where v1 showed nothing once the delete's window had
    passed. An archive keeps the slot's last record, resolved, with its
    time.
  - **What it costs.** Each kept record is one bit of the heartbeat and
    22 bytes of a snapshot (Appendix B).
- **A refresh is rare.** An owner SHOULD NOT refresh one alarm more than
  once in 450 s. `[Sc: §2]` Its put is express, as every put of the
  resource is (§3).
- **A rule that flaps** costs one express put at each transition, as in
  v1 at four times the bytes. Hysteresis is the owner's.
- **A re-raise** is a raise of an identity resolved before. It goes on
  the slot the instance still holds for it, else on the lowest free one
  (§2.2). `[Sc: §4]`
- **A duplicate observation** is no act, as in v1. `[Sc: §1]`
- **What a resolve says** is v1's: the condition cleared, or will be
  evaluated no more. A restart and a close are §2.9's.

### 2.6 Reading transitions

This section replaces v1 §2.6. A reader reads by identity.
- **The state is the record's.** A reader MUST read an alarm's state
  from its record's `state`, never from the sample's kind, from a
  heartbeat's bit or from a count. `[F: v2/transitions.json]`
- **One identity.** Two records of one slot have one identity when:
  - each has an alert key, and the two are equal. A record by words has
    its `key`, and a named table's rule the key the reader derives;
  - or either is unnamed, and their `rule` and their `labels` are
    equal, an absent `labels` equal only to an absent one.
- **In the owner's order.** A reader MUST read each put of a slot
  against the record it holds on that slot, as this table and the
  fixture do. `[F: v2/transitions.json]` It holds each record with the
  run of the put that carried it: its envelope's, or its snapshot
  entry's (core §7.5).

  | Held on the slot | The put | Reads |
  |---|---|---|
  | nothing | firing | **raised** |
  | nothing | resolved | **resolved** |
  | the same identity, firing | firing | **escalated** if the severity differs; else **raised** if `at` differs; else **confirmed** if the records are equal; else **refreshed** |
  | the same identity, firing | resolved | **resolved** |
  | the same identity, resolved | firing | **raised** |
  | the same identity, resolved | resolved | **resolved** if `at` differs; else **confirmed** if the records are equal; else **refreshed** |
  | another identity, firing, under the put's run | any | the held alarm **resolved**, by `rebound`. Then the put, as against nothing |
  | another identity, resolved, under the put's run | any | the held record is `replaced`, with nothing to read. Then the put, as against nothing |
  | another identity, under another run | any | the held record is `dropped`: an earlier run's last word. Then the put, as against nothing |

  - **Equal** is equal in every field §2.4 names. An absent `at` is
    equal to an absent one, and differs from any instant.
  - **Why `rebound` is a resolve.** An owner never takes a slot whose
    record fires (§2.2). So another identity on the slot, under the same
    run, says the held alarm resolved, and the reader lost that put.
  - **Under another run** nothing is known of the held alarm. A new
    instance that did not adopt it leaves it its previous instance's
    last word (§2.9, §2.14).
- **A delete,** or a tombstone, reads as v1's column: **resolved** over
  a firing record, **retired** over a resolved one or nothing. The
  reader then holds nothing on the slot. No rule here has an owner send
  one (§2.5).
- **A put that does not read** (§2.4) is **unreadable**, and the reader
  holds nothing on the slot after it.
- **A late reader** reads its first put of a slot as raised or resolved.
  The record's `at` says when that transition happened.
- **Words** a reader takes for a record it holds (§2.4) are a refresh
  where they differ from those it held. They move no state.
- **One identity on two slots.** Only two instances leave that: one
  that restarted with no record, on other slots. A reader takes the
  record under the run its owner's present descriptor states (core
  §3.3). `[F: v2/slots.json]`
  - Where no slot is under that run, it takes none: each is an earlier
    instance's last word.
  - Two under that one run break §2.2. The reader takes the later by
    seq, and a tool MAY report the break (§5).
  - **Taking chooses between an identity's slots.** A slot is taken
    only under the present run. Taking moves no slot's answer. A reader
    answers each record it holds by its slot (§2.14).
    `[F: v2/slots.json]`
  - **With no run stated,** as across a face, a reader takes no slot.
    It compares no identity across slots. It answers each entry by its
    slot, as §2.15's table does. So an established owner's firing entry
    is aged by its confirmation, never `not_held`.
    `[F: v2/conclusions.json]`

### 2.7 Confirmation: the heartbeat's bit

v1 §2.7 holds, with nothing new on the wire. `alarms/{slot}` is the
contract's one collection, of cardinality 256, at a 900 s horizon. Its
heartbeat carries a digest, a live count of 9 bits, and one bit or one
rank for each live member (core §7.5, `freshness.v2` §2.4).
- **The horizon is the revision's.** A reader ages an alarm at the
  `freshness.ttl_s` of the revision its owner's descriptor names (core
  §3.3). That is v1 §2.7's "contract's horizon". Across a face, the
  deployment's word names the revision (§2.11): the `alarms.v2`
  revision a reader is given, else the standard one, which every
  reader holds. A deployment that runs another revision gives it.
  `[Sc: §2, §6]`
  - The standard revision's horizon is 900 s. Every 900 s and 450 s in
    this text is that revision's.
  - A copy with another horizon is read at its own.
  - **A revision not in hand** gives no horizon. A reader that neither
    holds nor can retrieve the revision its owner names reads that
    owner's alarms' freshness as unobservable, never at 900 s: a
    shorter horizon read at the standard one would call a stale alarm
    fresh. Its reason is `freshness.v1`'s `not_a_horizon`, no horizon
    in hand (v1 §2.16's second list). `[F: v2/conclusions.json]`
  - **No descriptor read** names no revision. On the near side, an
    owner whose descriptor the reader could not read is read as a
    revision not in hand: unobservable, never 900 s. Only across a face
    does the deployment's word stand for it.
- **The bit** of a firing alarm is set only while its owner still
  evaluates its condition (v1 §2.7). Cleared, the alarm ages from its
  last confirmation and reads **stale** at 900 s: neither firing nor
  resolved. `[F: v2/confirmations.json]` `[Sc: §6]`
- **A resolved record has its bit clear,** from its resolved put on. It
  is a live member, since its key has a value (core §7.5). So it takes
  one bit of a bitmap, and nothing of a list.
- **Neither a count nor a bit is a state.** A reader MUST NOT read a
  section's live count as the number of firing alarms, nor a clear bit
  as resolved, nor a set bit as firing. `[F: v2/confirmations.json]`
  `[Sc: §6]` The record says the state (§2.6), and a resolved alarm
  reads resolved whatever its age (§2.14).
- **Ranks follow key order,** in bytes: slot 10 ranks before slot 2
  (core §7.5). A rank is a place among the live members, never a slot.
  `[F: v2/confirmations.json]`
- **Which heartbeat.** The alarms keep a heartbeat of their own, at
  most 450 s apart, or ride an owner heartbeat as one group of its
  `host` class (`freshness.v2` §2.4). A group rides at its key's period,
  so beside a 60 s horizon it is sent fifteen times as often (Appendix
  B). That is the owner's choice.
- **The cost** is one heartbeat at least every 450 s, whatever fires: a
  17-byte payload with three alarms firing, and 49 with 256.

### 2.8 The bound

This section replaces v1 §2.8.
- **256 firing, or the descriptor's.** An owner holds at most as many
  firing alarms as it has slots: 256, the contract's cardinality, which
  its descriptor MAY lower (core §3.3, §2.7).
- **A resolved record takes no room.** Its slot is free (§2.2). Under v1
  a resolved alarm counted until its delete.
- **At the bound,** every slot firing, the owner refuses the raise that
  would add an alarm, as core §2.7 refuses a write, and its writer is
  told. It MUST NOT resolve a firing alarm to make room, nor put over
  one: a resolve says the condition cleared (§2.5).
  `[F: v2/slots.json]` `[Sc: §7]`
- It reports the refusal where a reader looks, as v1 §2.8 asks: a
  health check at `degraded`, or a fault.
- **The live members** are the firing alarms and the resolved records
  kept. Both are on slots below the bound, so core §2.7's rule holds.

### 2.9 A restart, and a clean close

v1 §2.9 holds, on slots, with these steps in place of its four.
- **A new instance puts again what it keeps.** An owner whose instance
  changes MUST put each alarm it adopts again from its new instance, on
  its slot, before it answers a GET with it. It SHOULD do so before its
  new instance token (core §8.2). `[Sc: §5]`
- **A restart adopts.** Before its first put, an owner takes the alarms
  its previous instance held from its own persistent record, else from
  an explicit read of an archive on its own side, as v1 §2.9 says. For
  each slot it finds, in this order: `[F: v2/slots.json]` `[Sc: §5]`
  1. **resolved:** nothing is put. The slot is free;
  2. **it does not read, or its words do not derive its alert key:**
     nothing is put. The slot is free;
  3. **firing, under a rule the owner no longer evaluates:** the owner
     puts it resolved, on its slot (§2.5);
  4. **firing otherwise:** the owner adopts it. It puts it again on its
     slot, its `at` kept, under its new run, and resolves it at its
     first evaluation that finds the condition clear.
- **One identity firing on two slots** of the record breaks §2.2. The
  owner adopts it on the lowest of them, by the steps above, and puts
  nothing on the others: they are free. `[F: v2/slots.json]`
- **By name, never by index.** The record holds each alarm's words. The
  new instance writes each alarm it puts by its own table where that
  table names those words, else by words (§2.4). So a table that
  changed between two instances moves no alarm.
- **What the record holds.** An owner's record SHOULD hold, by slot,
  each record as put, its alarm's words and its alert key, a resolved
  one included. `[Sc: §5]`
  - For an alarm by its table, the words are the rule's name and each
    label's text.
- **A reader that held a slot the new instance leaves** reads it as the
  previous instance's last word (§2.14). The next complete snapshot
  leaves it out, and it then reads `not_held`.
- **Past a lowered bound,** a slot at or above the new bound is neither
  put nor resolved, as v1 leaves an alarm past its bound. A resolve
  would say its condition cleared.
- **From `alarms.v1`.** An owner that moves adopts each firing v1
  document onto a slot that §2.2 gives it. Its `at` is the document's
  `timestamp` in whole seconds (§2.5). `[Sc: §12]`
- **With no source,** the owner adopts nothing, as in v1.
- **A clean close leaves them,** as in v1: an owner SHOULD NOT resolve
  an alarm because it closes. `[Sc: §8]`

### 2.10 Device-as-service

v1 §2.10 holds, each device service implementing one major for itself.

### 2.11 Constrained faces

v1 §2.11 holds: the contract declares no `link.exposure`, so nothing of
it crosses a constrained face by push, and a far reader GETs the alarms
by grant. These items change.
- **One snapshot establishes them.** A far reader's collection read is
  answered by the alarms' snapshot, with an entry or with none (§2.15).
  Under v1 an empty GET across a face established nothing, for ever.
  `[F: v2/conclusions.json]`
- **A face closed to them** is v1's: a tool across it MUST answer
  whether an alarm fires, and whether anything does, unobservable, and
  MUST NOT report that nothing fires. `[F: v2/conclusions.json]`
- **The words stay home.** `words/{slot}` is never exposed. A far
  reader shows an alarm by words by its alert key, as v1's key showed
  it.
- **The table crosses by the catalog.** A far reader takes an owner's
  table from the revision its catalog names for the owner's contract
  (core §8.6, §4).
- **The cost** of a far reader's read is one reply: 163 bytes on RF for
  three firing alarms, with its final (Appendix B).

### 2.12 References from other interfaces

v1 §2.12 holds: three members, never one string, and the MUST NOT.
`[F: v1 keys.json]` Its second item is replaced.
- **A reference names no key.** The alert key is in no key of this
  major. A reader finds the alarm by a collection read of its owner's
  `zk2/<system>/<service>/alarms.v2/state/alarms/**`, among the records
  it reads there. `[F: v2/keys.json]`
- **Never the slot.** A slot is no part of a reference: it names
  another alarm once its record is retaken (§2.2).
- **The same reference as v1's.** An identity's alert key is v1's for
  the same words. A service that moves keeps its references, and the
  acknowledgements keyed by them.

### 2.13 The tokenless set

v1 §2.13 holds. A reader MUST find `alarms.v2`'s owners through
instance tokens and descriptors, and MUST NOT conclude that a service
does not implement it from a missing `alive/alarms.v2/…` token.
`[F: v2/conclusions.json]`

### 2.14 What a reader concludes of one alarm

v1 §2.14's six steps hold, read over this major's records. A reader MUST
answer "is this alarm firing?" as the fixture does.
`[F: v2/conclusions.json]`
- **Which alarm.** The question is asked of an alarm the reader holds
  on a slot, or of a reference. An alarm asked by reference is the one
  whose record derives that alert key.
- **Step 2, the descriptor.** One whose `interfaces` holds `alarms.v2`
  is read by this text. One that holds `alarms.v1` and not `alarms.v2`
  is read by `alarms.v1`'s. One that holds neither: **not asked**,
  `not_listed`.
- **Steps 4 and 5, a GET's answer.** A GET answers a slot when a
  snapshot lists it, live or as a tombstone. It leaves the slot out
  when a complete snapshot of its owner's alarms lists neither (core
  §4.2).
- **Step 5, the latest sample,** has one answer more. The alarm's
  record was `rebound` (§2.6): **no**, `rebound`.
  - A record `replaced` or `dropped` leaves the reader holding nothing
    of its alarm. That is step 5's "none": `not_held` where the owner's
    alarms are established, else `not_read`.
  - **A GET's entry** is attributed by its snapshot's run, as in v1.
- **Step 6, its freshness,** is `freshness.v2`'s, at the horizon of
  the revision its owner names (§2.7). A resolved record is answered at
  step 5, whatever its age.
- **An unnamed alarm** is answered as any other, by its slot.
  `[F: v2/conclusions.json]` It counts in §2.15's "anything firing". It
  matches no reference, so no acknowledgement and no remembered notice.

"Is this firing alarm past its horizon, its owner present?" keeps v1's
three places, with `rebound` beside a resolved record: **not asked**,
`not_firing`. `[F: v2/conclusions.json]`

### 2.15 What a reader concludes of an owner's alarms

This section replaces v1 §2.15's "Established", its window and its
order. Its owners in scope and its "anything firing" hold.
- **Established.** An owner's alarms are known whole, **established**,
  when the reader's latest collection read of its
  `alarms.v2/state/alarms/**` meets all of these:
  `[F: v2/conclusions.json]` `[Sc: §10]`
  - it ran to its final reply with no error reply;
  - it drew a complete snapshot of the alarms (core §7.5), with any
    number of entries or none;
  - that snapshot's run is one a present descriptor of the owner
    states (core §3.3). Across a face the deployment's word stands for
    the descriptor, and no run is compared;
  - it was asked after the reader's last loss, and after the last gap
    in the owner's presence;
  - the reader's subscription, if it has one, lost nothing since.
- **Why no entry is enough.** A snapshot with no entry is the owner's
  word that the collection had no member (core §4.2). An empty reply set
  was silence (core O5), which is why v1 needed its window.
- **It does not lapse.** An owner's alarms stay established while those
  conditions hold, as v1's seed stands. An owner that hangs with nothing
  firing reads "nothing fires" until its health says otherwise.
- **The order.** A reader MUST judge an owner's alarms by the first of
  these that answers, as the fixture does. `[F: v2/conclusions.json]`
  1. **v1 §2.14's steps 1 and 2,** with their answers and reasons, the
     descriptor read as §2.14 reads it;
  2. **the read:** none made, `no_seed`; one that ended at its timeout
     or with an error reply, `seed_incomplete`: **unobservable**;
  3. **since the read,** when the reader subscribes: a delivery lost,
     `window_lost`; a gap in the owner's presence, `owner_gap`:
     **unobservable**. Across a face, presence does not cross, so no
     gap is asked there;
  4. **the snapshot:** no page of it, or a page missing,
     `snapshot_incomplete`; its run one that no present descriptor
     states, where one states a run, `snapshot_run`: **unobservable**;
  5. otherwise `snapshot`: **established**.
- **What went.** v1's `answered`, `window`, `window_short`,
  `window_unconfirmed`, `no_member` and, for this question,
  `across_face`.
- **A gap in the owner's presence** is v1's: its instance token
  withdrawn, or a new instance's appearing. The new instance answers
  the next read with what it holds (§2.9).
- **`snapshot_run`** is a snapshot an earlier instance built. The
  reader reads again.
- **Across a face:**

  | What crosses | The reader concludes |
  |---|---|
  | a GET by grant, answered by a complete snapshot | established. Each firing entry is answered by its slot (§2.6). It is aged by its confirmation (`freshness.v2` §2.6) |
  | a GET that ends at its timeout, or a page missing | unobservable: `seed_incomplete`, `snapshot_incomplete` |
  | no GET granted, as by default | unobservable, `face_closed`. Never "nothing fires" |
  | pushes and a heartbeat, were a later revision to expose the alarms, and no GET | nothing established: `no_seed` |

- **Anything firing at or above a severity** is v1's, over the owners
  so established. `[F: v2/conclusions.json]` An unnamed alarm counts. A
  firing alarm whose severity is the escape is v1's "yes without a
  severity".

### 2.16 Reporting changes

v1 §2.16 holds, with these items.
- **`rebound` is a resolve to report.** The held alarm's answer moves
  from yes to no (§2.14). `[F: v2/transitions.json]`
- **A record `replaced` or `dropped`** is no change of its alarm to
  report by itself. Its alarm's answer is §2.14's.
- **A notice's identity is the reference,** never the key and never the
  slot. A notifier that remembers what it told remembers it by
  reference. So it tells nothing twice when a service moves from
  `alarms.v1`, or when an alarm comes back on another slot.
- **An unnamed alarm** is reported by its owner, its slot and its `at`,
  and the tool says it is unnamed. A tool that restarts cannot match it
  to a notice it remembers, and MAY tell it again.

### 2.17 Beside `alarms.v1`

- **An owner implements one major** per instance (§2.1). The reference's
  default stays `alarms.v1` until its readers are released.
- **A scope names owners, not a major.** A tool asked of a selector of
  either major's alarms reads both majors of the owners it selects: one
  subscription and one read for each (core §1.7). So a rule written
  over `zk2/*/*/alarms.v1/state/alarms/*` goes on reporting an owner
  that moves. `[Sc: §11]`
  - Its answers, its reasons and its counts are one set for both.
- **Readers first, strictly.** A reader built before this major finds
  no `alarms.v1` in an `alarms.v2` owner's descriptor. It answers **not
  asked**, `not_listed`, and counts that owner among those with no
  alarms to ask. `[Sc: §11]`
  - So its "is anything firing?" can read no while that owner fires.
    That is worse than a stale answer: it is quiet.
  - A deployment moves every reader before its first owner. Nothing on
    the wire guards the order.

## 3. Contract

The standard contract is [`alarms.v2.toml`](alarms.v2.toml). Its packed
type is in [`alarms.v2.json`](alarms.v2.json), and its JSON type in
[`schemas/alarms.v2.json`](schemas/alarms.v2.json). Revision 2.0 is
`sha256:2292477cadf555dd09b07499ca048add4e48c63d60a209c13eaf543988c32cb7`,
published in `../.history/alarms.v2/` (core §9.7). Its heartbeat's tag
is 7 (core §7.5).

| Resource | Kind | Type | QoS | Annotations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `alarms/{slot}` | `state`, `slot` a `uint`, cardinality 256 | `packed:Alarm` | `reliable`, `block`, `interactive_high`, `express`: v1's | `freshness.ttl_s = 900` |
| `words/{slot}` | `state`, `slot` a `uint`, cardinality 256 | `json:alarms.v2.Words` | `reliable`, `block` (state's default) | none |

The interface `uses = ["freshness.v2"]`, for the horizon. It declares
no `link.exposure` (§2.11).

**The schemas.** The packed file's artifact id is
`sha256:fe59e748fc957322428ac633a0908d09aa8b6c66952f7a9cdd8608db715a2b2a`,
and the JSON Schema's
`sha256:a3515ad47772ac778ccfbad3850a086ac18c28a9975eb7c8288c3437db9b99de`
(core §9.4). `[F: v2/schema.json]`

```text
Alarm   state · severity · rule · at · labels · argument · key · "..."

state      enum firing, resolved; no escape                        1 bit
severity   enum info, warning, critical; an escape                 2
rule       int 0 to 254, reserved not_available                    8
at         time, no milliseconds, optional                         1 + 32
labels     int 0 to 16,777,215, optional                           1 + 24
argument   int 0 to 1,048,574, reserved not_available              20
key        counter of 64 bits, optional                            1 + 64
"..."      the extension marker                                    1
```

| A record | Bits | Bytes | Put's payload, 16-bit run |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| a table's rule, no label: the usual one | 67 | 9 | 15 |
| a table's rule, with labels | 91 | 12 | 18 |
| a table's rule, no `at` | 35 | 5 | 11 |
| by words, with `at` and `key` | 131 | 17 | 23 |

`[F: v2/records.json]`
- **The tag** is 11 (core §7.6). A record's put has CH `0x0b` under a
  16-bit run, and `0x1b` under a 32-bit run. `[F: v2/records.json]`
  - **It is not 0,** the tag a type of any other kind carries. So a
    reader refuses a JSON payload put on a slot's key by its tag, before
    it reads a bit, and a tool that holds no contract shows the record
    as packed and never sniffs it (observer §7.3).
    `[F: v2/records.json]`
  - **The length would not refuse one.** A marker read as 1 leaves the
    length only a lower bound (core §7.6), so bytes of another kind can
    read as a record under the right tag.
- **`rule` comes before `at`.** A record says what fires, then since
  when. Its state, its severity and its rule are its first 11 bits,
  whether or not it carries a time. The other order hashes to the tag
  0.
- **`key` is a `counter`,** which counts nothing here. It is core
  §7.6's one kind that holds 64 bits: an `int`'s bound of 2^64 − 1 has
  no portable form in a schema file (core E028). Its JSON reading is an
  integer, the alert key's 16 hexadecimal digits read as one number.
- **No unused code.** Every code of every field is a value, a declared
  reserved code or the escape. So core §7.6's reading refuses a record
  by its tag, its length or its pad, never by a code.
- **It grows behind its marker.** A later text appends optional fields,
  which a reader of this one ignores (core §7.6). A marker read as 1
  ends this text's reading: the record's state and severity stand.
- **In a snapshot** the alarms are collection 0 and the words
  collection 1: the contract's `state` resources in the canonical
  form's order (core §7.5). A member's id is its slot.
  `[F: v2/confirmations.json]`
- **`reliable` and `block`,** as v1's: no queue on the path drops a
  raise or a resolve.
- **Express.** The contract's QoS is the resource's (core §2.4), so
  every put on `alarms/{slot}` is express: transitions and refreshes.
  The heartbeat, the words and a snapshot's reply are not.
- **No application field,** as in v1.

## 4. Vocabulary

| Key | Type | On | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `alarms.rules` | a list of at most 255 rules (below) | any resource; written once, on `[defaults]` | The service's own table: the rule at index i is the one a record's `rule` i names (§2.4). |

W105 reads this table for a contract that lists `alarms.v2` (core §10).
`[F: core contracts/ok-alarms-v2-rules]` Any other key `alarms.<key>` is
outside it, and every key is outside `alarms.v1`'s.
`[F: core contracts/w105-alarms-v2-vocabulary]`
`[F: core contracts/w105-alarms-rules]` The standard contract writes
none. A service's table is in a contract of its own:

```toml
[interface]
name  = "seed"
major = 1
minor = 0
uses  = ["alarms.v2"]

[[defaults.annotations."alarms.rules"]]
name     = "hot"
category = "sensor_health"
summary  = "Temperature {argument} C"
argument = { minimum = "-40", resolution = "0.1" }

[[defaults.annotations."alarms.rules"]]
name    = "link_down"
summary = "Link to {peer} on {port} is down"
labels  = [
  { name = "peer", values = ["r1", "r2", "r3"] },
  { name = "port", values = ["eth0", "eth1"] },
]

[[defaults.annotations."alarms.rules"]]
name    = "sensor_lost"
summary = "Sensor {sensor} lost"
labels  = [{ name = "sensor", minimum = 0, maximum = 63 }]

[resources.a]
kind = "stream"
type = { raw = "text/plain" }
```

- **A list of TOML tables.** TOML 1.0 keeps an inline table on one line
  (core §9.1). So each rule is a table of the array. Its canonical form
  is the same list.

**A rule** is a table with these members, and no other:

| Member | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | a string, required | The rule's name: v1 §2.4's `rule`, and the recipe's first input. |
| `summary` | a string, required | A template: the alarm's one line for a person (below). |
| `category` | a string | v1 §2.4's `category`, the same for every alarm of the rule. |
| `argument` | `{ minimum, resolution }`, two decimal strings as core §7.6 writes them, the resolution above 0 | How the record's `argument` reads (below). |
| `labels` | a list of one or more labels | The rule's discriminating labels, in the order their codes are written. |

**A label** is a table holding `name`, a string, and one of:
- `values`: a list of one or more strings, the label's values;
- `minimum` and `maximum`: two integers, the first not above the second.
  The label's values are the integers from one to the other.

**Its strings are printable ASCII,** as every string of a contract's
canonical form is (core §9.5). An alarm whose words need more is by
words (§2.4).

- **A label's code** is its value's index in `values`, or its integer
  less `minimum`. A label of n values takes ⌈log₂ n⌉ bits, so a label
  of one value takes none.
- **`labels`** holds the codes of the rule's labels in list order, the
  first in the most significant bits, with no bit between them. It is
  one integer below 2^w, w being the widths' sum.
  - A rule with no label writes no `labels`.
  - `link_down` above has `peer` in 2 bits and `port` in 1. Its alarm
    with `peer` `r2` and `port` `eth0` writes `labels` 2.
- **A label's text** is its value, or its integer in decimal: `-` for
  a negative one, and no leading zero.
- **The alert key** of a table's alarm is v1's recipe over the rule's
  `name` and each label's name and text. `link_down` with `r2` and
  `eth0` derives v1's test vector, `a659f813308ad1da`.
  `[F: v2/keys.json]`
- **The argument** reads as `minimum` + code × `resolution`, in exact
  decimal arithmetic, in its shortest form (core §7.6).
  - An owner writes a reading as round((reading − `minimum`) /
    `resolution`), a tie to the even code, as core §7.6 writes a
    `fixed`.
  - A reading whose code is outside 0 to 1,048,574 is written
    `not_available`, and so is none. The alarm still fires.
  - A rule with no `argument` writes `not_available`. A reader ignores
    any other code under such a rule.
- **The summary** is the rule's template with each placeholder
  replaced.
  - A `{` opens a placeholder, which ends at the next `}`. The text
    between them is its name.
  - `{argument}` is replaced by the argument's reading, or by `?`
    where the record's is `not_available`.
  - `{<label>}` is replaced by that label's text.
  - A summary holds no other `{` or `}`.

**Reading and writing by a table.** An owner MUST write an alarm's
`rule`, `labels` and `argument` by its instance's table, and a reader
MUST read them, exactly as the fixture does. `[F: v2/tables.json]`
`[F: v2/keys.json]`

**A contract's table.** A reader MUST read a contract's table exactly
as the fixture does. `[F: v2/tables.json]` A contract holds the table
its resources carry, or none. Its declaration is invalid, and it holds
none, for the first of these:
- `not_used`: a resource carries the key, and the contract's `uses`
  does not list `alarms.v2`. A contract that lists no major of `alarms`
  does not load (core E020). One that lists `alarms.v1` loads, with
  W105, and holds none;
- `value`: a resource's value is not a list of at most 255 rules of the
  shape above;
- `rule_name`: a rule's name is empty, holds a newline, or is another
  rule's;
- `label`: a label's name is empty, is `argument`, holds a newline,
  `=`, `{` or `}`, or is another label's of its rule; or one of its
  values holds a newline, or comes twice;
- `labels_width`: a rule's labels take more than 24 bits;
- `host_label`: a label's name is host-scoped (v1 §2.2);
- `placeholder`: a summary holds a `{` with no `}` after it, a `}`
  outside a placeholder, or a placeholder whose name is neither a label
  of its rule nor `argument` under a rule that declares one;
- `disagrees`: two resources carry two different lists.

A resource that does not carry the key takes no part. In a contract
that lists `alarms.v2`, a linter reports each as an error, as `link.v2`'s
`packed_required` is. `not_used` is W105's.

**An instance's table** is the one table among the contracts it
implements: the revisions its descriptor names (core §3.3).
`[F: v2/tables.json]`
- Where none holds one, it has none. Its owner writes every alarm by
  words, and asks a newcomer for no table.
- Where two hold two different lists, it has none (`differ`), and its
  owner writes every alarm by words.
- A reader reads it from those of the named revisions it holds. A
  revision it does not hold adds no table, and neither does one it
  holds that the descriptor does not name.
- Across a constrained face no descriptor crosses. The deployment's
  word names the revisions, as it names the interface (§2.11, core
  §8.6).

**Never another revision's table.** A reader MUST NOT show a name read
from a revision its owner's descriptor does not name.
`[F: v2/tables.json]` `[Sc: §9]` A table read under the wrong revision
gives a wrong name over a right state and severity. A reader that holds
neither the descriptor nor the deployment's word shows numbers, as in
`rule 1, labels 2`.

**A table grows by appending.** A change to a table is an annotation's
change, a review (core §9.8). A later revision SHOULD append rules, and
SHOULD NOT remove or reorder one, nor change a rule's name or its
labels. `[F: v2/tables.json]` Then every index and every code keeps its
words across revisions. A rule's summary, category and argument MAY
change in place.

**What the tag does not check.** The split of `labels` is the table's,
not the record's type. So a wrong revision of the table reads wrong
names, where a type's tag would refuse a wrong layout. The descriptor's
fingerprint and the catalog pin the revision.

## 5. What a tool may conclude

The verdict tokens are v1 §5's, and
[`conclusions.json`](conformance/v2/conclusions.json) states them with
their reasons.

| Question | Yes | No | Unestablished | Finding on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is this alarm firing? (§2.14) | As v1 §5's first row, over `alarms.v2`: the latest a firing record of the present instance, fresh within 900 s. | The latest a resolved record, a delete or a tombstone; the alarm's record `rebound`; or the alarm not held by the owner's alarms, established (§2.15). | As v1's. An unnamed alarm is answered by its slot. | Yes. |
| Is anything firing at or above *S* under this selector? (§2.15) | As v1's, over owners of either major. | As v1's: every owner asked established, an `alarms.v2` owner by one complete snapshot. | As v1's, an `alarms.v2` owner with §2.15's reasons. | Yes. |
| Do this slot's words derive its record's key? (§2.4) | **`strict`:** v1's recipe over the words gives the record's `key`. **`sanitized`:** only through the sanitizing step, which a tool MAY report. | **`mismatch`:** the words are another alarm's, or none's. A finding about the owner only while it lasts: words are put before their record (§2.4). | **Not asked:** the record names a table's rule (`table`); no words were read (`no_words`); no record is held (`no_record`). **Unobservable:** the words or the record do not read (`unreadable`, `undecodable`). `[F: v2/words.json]` | No. |
| Does this owner hold each identity on one slot? (§2.2) | No two slots of its present run name one alert key. | Two do: a finding about the owner. `[F: v2/slots.json]` | **Unobservable:** its alarms are not established (§2.15). Unnamed alarms are not compared. | No. |
| Is this firing alarm past its horizon, its owner present? (§2.7, §2.14) | As v1's. | As v1's. | As v1's, `rebound` among the latest not firing. | Yes: about the owner. |

- **A slot is shown,** beside the alarm's words or its alert key. It is
  where the alarm is now, never its name.
- **A named alarm** is shown by its rule's name, its labels and its
  summary, where the reader has its words. An alarm by words whose
  words the reader did not take is shown by its alert key.
- **An unnamed alarm** is shown by its numbers, as in `rule 1, labels
  2`, and the tool says why (§2.4).
- **`at`** is shown to the second. An absent one is shown as absent,
  never as the epoch.
- **Which cause** a stale alarm has is unobservable from the alarm
  alone, as in v1.

| `alarms.v1` | `alarms.v2` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The key is the alert key, 16 hexadecimal digits | The key is a slot, 1 to 3 digits. The alert key is derived, or carried for an alarm by words (§2.2, §2.4) | A state member is a key, so the slot is carried once, as 2 bytes of the key |
| A JSON document of 107 to 155 bytes, as measured | A packed record of 9 bytes, 12 with labels (§3) | A transition is one SDU on every face |
| `timestamp`, milliseconds since the Unix epoch | `at`, whole seconds from the deployment epoch, optional (§2.4) | Readers show it, and an acknowledgement compares it |
| `observed_at_ms` on a refresh | Gone | No reader concludes from it. A refresh's own put says the owner re-observed |
| `rule`, `category`, `summary` and discriminating `labels` as text | An index into the service's table, label codes and an argument. Text only for an alarm by words (§2.4, §4) | A table's word costs bits |
| Host-scoped `labels` | Not written for a table's rule. Kept in an alarm's words (§2.4) | The key's system names the host, and v1 never hashed them |
| Members a later minor adds | Fields behind the extension marker (§3) | As core §7.6 grows a record |
| A resolve is a put, then a delete | A resolve is one put. The record stays until its slot is retaken (§2.5) | The delete was a second SDU |
| A resolved alarm counts in the bound until its delete | A resolved record takes no room (§2.8) | Its slot is free at once |
| Read by key | Read by identity: `rebound`, `replaced` and `dropped` (§2.6) | A slot holds one alarm after another |
| Established by a GET that answered a value, or by a lossless window longer than 900 s | Established by one complete snapshot, with an entry or with none (§2.15) | A snapshot with no entry is the owner's word, where an empty reply set was silence |
| An empty GET across a face establishes nothing | A complete snapshot across a face establishes (§2.11) | The same |
| A reference names one key to read | A reference names an alarm, found by a collection read (§2.12) | The alert key is in no key |
| One interface, `alarms.v1` | Two interfaces, an instance implementing one (§2.1, §2.17) | A record's kind cannot change within a major (core §9.8) |

**What deliberately did not change.**
- **The identity.** The alert key is v1's recipe, byte for byte, over
  the same words. v1's test vector holds.
- **What a reader may conclude:** firing, resolved, stale, last-known
  and unobservable, by v1 §2.14's steps and reasons.
- **The confirmation:** `freshness.v2`'s heartbeat at 900 s, with no
  re-put. `alarms.v1`'s revision 1.1 already had it.
- **The QoS,** the horizon, the bound of 256 and the exposure.
- **Never a resolve for room, nor at a close.**

**What is narrowed.**
- **No member read by reference.** A reference no longer names a key.
- **A transition's instant** is to the second, or absent, where v1's
  was to the millisecond. A burst dates its last transitions ahead of
  the clock.
- **When a refresh was observed** is gone.
- **Words across a face.** A table's words cross with the catalog.
  Words by `words/{slot}` stay on the host side, where v1's text
  crossed with its document by grant.
- **A table's words are printable ASCII,** and a rule's category is one
  for all its alarms.
- **A resolved record is a member** a subscriber holds to stay in step
  with the heartbeat, until its slot is retaken.

## Appendix B. The bytes, computed

Every figure is computed from §3's layouts through the wire baseline's
model (`docs/zk2/measurements/wire-baseline/`): RF, the lean stack, a
16-bit run. `alarms.v1`'s payloads are measured. No `alarms.v2` byte has
crossed a wire.
- **The frame.** An alarm's put is `interactive_high`, so its frame is
  5 bytes on the face: the face's 3 and the QoS extension. That frame
  and the 2-byte key suffix `/0` are inferred from `alarms.v1`'s
  measured totals, not decoded.

| | `alarms.v1` | `alarms.v2` | SDU on RF, v1 → v2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| a raise, a table's rule, no label | 113 B | 15 B, a 9-B record | 158 → 45 B |
| a resolve | 161 B, then a delete | 15 B | 259 B in 2 SDUs → 45 B |
| a raise with two labels | 159 B | 18 B, a 12-B record | 205 → 48 B |
| a raise with no `at` | | 11 B, a 5-B record | 41 B |
| a raise by words: the words, then the record | | 94 B and 23 B | 175 B in 2 SDUs |
| a late joiner's snapshot, 3 firing | 514 B | 64 B | 672 B in 4 SDUs → 163 B in 1 |
| the heartbeat, 3 firing | 17 B | 17 B | 40 B, 8.1 KB a day |

- **W2, a day.** Five alarms raised and five resolved cost 1.98 KB
  under `alarms.v1` and 0.45 KB here. On 32-bit runs it is 0.47 KB.
  - Where zenoh declares the scope at `…/alarms.v2/state`, the suffix
    is `/alarms/0`, an SDU is 52 bytes, and the day 0.52 KB.
- **A slot of two digits** adds 1 byte to a put, and one of three 2.
- **A kept resolved record** adds 22 bytes to a snapshot, and one bit
  to the heartbeat. A firing one adds 16.
- **The heartbeat** is 17 bytes with nothing live, 18 with three firing
  and five resolved records kept, and 49 with 256 firing: 8.1, 8.3 and
  14.6 KB a day at the reference's 427.5 s.
- **256 firing alarms** are 4.5 KB of snapshot: 5 pages of 1,024 B.
- **In an owner heartbeat** the alarms are one group of 68 bits with
  three firing. At a 28.5 s period that is 25.8 KB a day, where their
  own heartbeat costs 8.1 KB.
